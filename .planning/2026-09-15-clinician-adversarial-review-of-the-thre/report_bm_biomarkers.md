## Biomarkers: what the clinician sees today

The Biomarkers page (`Client/src/views/Reports/Biomarkers/index.js` and children) shows, for RCS08, a data-availability timeline, a binarization/matching control panel, and — as the page's headline, per decision 62/66 — two heat maps: one grid of correlation (r) and one of area-under-the-curve (AUC), 22 frequency-band rows... actually columns (8.5-29.5 Hz, 5 Hz wide, `analytics.py:5399`) crossed with 10 lengths of averaged signal (1 s to 5 min, `analytics.py:5390`), built for each of six sensing-contact pairs at once. Hovering a cell shows only a bare number; clicking pins a scatter plot with a re-computed, explicitly-labelled "plain, uncorrected" Pearson r/p. A circled cell means it clears a stricter correction across all 22 band centres. Below that sits the older, full-spectrum exploratory panel (continuous 1-100 Hz scan), which is a different, wider-range analysis with its own statistics, kept separate on purpose (decision 61).

What matters most for a clinician deciding whether to trust and act on this page: the corrected numbers that decide whether a cell is circled — the count of ratings behind it, the confidence interval, the selection-corrected p-value, the family-wise q-value — are computed and stored, but **none of them ever reach the screen**. I verified this live: for the strongest band on the left 1-3 contact (12.5 Hz, NRS, 5 minutes of signal), the real numbers are r = -0.53, 95% interval [-0.66, -0.40], n = 117 ratings, family-wise q = 0.0022. A clinician looking at that circled cell sees a colour and, if they click it, a different, re-derived r/p pair computed on the spot from raw points — not this one, and explicitly not meant to be this one.

## Findings

**CRITICAL — The number that decides whether a band is trustworthy is never shown.** `best_correlation_rows`/`best_auc_rows` in `BRAVO/modules/Biomarkers/routines/analytics.py` (`_best_rows_correlation`, `_apply_family_wise_correction`, ~line 6491-6611) carry the sample count, the bootstrap interval, and the corrected p- and q-values for the one winning cell in each band's column. The only component that prints these as numbers, `BandTimeSweepPanel.js`, is imported nowhere — `index.js:23` mentions it only in a comment ("the older, non-interactive... rendering"). The live grid (`BiomarkerHeatmapGrids.js`) shows a bare `r = %{z:.3f}` or `AUC = %{z:.3f}` on hover (line ~299), and a pinned cell shows a **different**, re-computed, uncorrected r/p from raw points (`ScatterStatsLine`, lines ~627-648) — a statistic the code's own comment says must never be conflated with the grid's corrected number. The only visible signal of statistical strength is a binary circle. A clinician cannot tell, from the page, whether a circled cell rests on 12 ratings or 400, or how wide its interval is. **Fix (frontend):** read the matching row of `best_correlation_rows`/`best_auc_rows` for the hovered/pinned cell and print n, the interval, and the q-value next to (not instead of) the existing plain statistic, clearly labelled as two different things, as the code comments already intend but the UI does not deliver.

**CRITICAL — No out-of-sample check and no reliable-change threshold on this page.** Forward-chaining (train on past weeks, test forward — decision 12) and the individual reliable-change floor (decision 111) both exist and both run, but only inside `deployment_summary()`/`useDeploymentSummary` on the **Closed-Loop Deployment** page (`Client/src/views/Reports/ClosedLoopSim/DeploySignoffCard.js`, `useDeploymentSummary.js`), reached only after a band has already been committed from this page. On the Biomarkers page itself, every number a clinician sees is in-sample. There is no caption saying so. A clinician who stops at this page — which is exactly what "exploration" invites — has no way to know that the correlation they're looking at has not yet been checked on data the model hasn't seen. **Fix (frontend/doc):** a one-line caption under the grids ("this is an in-sample result; whether it survives on new data is checked after you commit a band, on the Closed-Loop Deployment page") turns a silent gap into a stated limitation.

**CRITICAL — "Does this relationship survive a different stimulation setting?" is deliberately hidden from this page.** The cross-setting stability check exists, is computed in the background for this exact stored grid entry (decisions 96-98), and is read by the Closed-Loop Deployment page's `BandSweepGridPanel.js`. But `bravo_service.py:5552` states outright that `IncludeCrossSettingStability` is "deliberately absent" from the Biomarkers request, and `BiomarkerHeatmapGrids.js` never mentions stability anywhere. For a novel indication with no published biomarker to lean on, this is the single most important honesty check available — is a promising band-pain correlation a real neural signal, or an artefact of one programmed setting that happened to coincide with a good or bad stretch? It is computed for this page's own data and simply not shown here. **Fix (backend/frontend):** carry the already-stored `cross_setting_stability_raw` through the Biomarkers request and mark each grid cell the same three-way way (stable / setting-dependent / cannot tell) that the Closed-Loop page already does.

**HIGH — The strongest correlations require more averaging than the device can deliver.** Both live examples above (12.5 Hz r=-0.53, and 15.5 Hz r=-0.33 on the right-side contact) resolved at 300 seconds (5 minutes) of averaged signal — the longest length the grid tries. Per decision 149, the Percept RC's documented averaging range for closed-loop control is **0-30 seconds**. The page never states that a band whose best evidence needs 5 minutes of averaging cannot be programmed as the device's real-time averaging window; a clinician reading "established" on a 5-minute row could reasonably believe that band is ready to set, when the device physically cannot average that long. Shorter-length cells do sometimes clear significance (e.g. 13.5 Hz AUC at 15 s, q=0.029) but nothing on the page distinguishes "best evidence, wrong length for the device" from "best evidence, a length the device can use." **Fix (frontend):** flag, on the pinned-cell panel and in the grid legend, which lengths of signal are within the device's own 0-30 s averaging range, and prefer showing the best result *within* that range alongside the unconstrained best.

**HIGH — In-clinic, current-stepped sessions are invisible on this page.** The clinic-sheet stream (`StimOptimizer/clinic_pain.py`, decision 161: 816 steps, 472 with a usable pain score, ingested into a raw store kind `clinic_pain_steps`) is read by Stim Optimizer only. No file in `BRAVO/modules/Biomarkers/` or `Client/src/views/Reports/Biomarkers/` references clinic sheets, in-clinic sessions, or the clinic-pain stream at all (confirmed by grep). Every number on the Biomarkers page comes from at-home REDCap ratings matched to whatever the device happened to be doing at the time (default ±60 minutes, symmetric — `sweep_settings.py:48,85-92`). In-clinic sessions are the only place amplitude is deliberately stepped, which is exactly what would let a clinician distinguish "this band tracks pain" from "this band tracks stimulation, and pain happens to track stimulation too." That distinction is what the (hidden) stability check partially covers, but the Biomarkers page gives no hint that a second, structured data source even exists. **Fix (doc, then backend):** state on the page that it pools only at-home ratings; consider a filter or at least a count of how many matched ratings coincided with a clinic visit.

**MEDIUM — The confidence interval and the p-value for the same cell rest on different, inconsistent assumptions about independence.** `p_selection_aware` (the number that decides the circle) comes from a circular **block** permutation of the pain scores, sized by the measured autocorrelation of the ratings (`stats_utils.block_length_for`, called at `analytics.py:6247`) — correctly treating nearby-in-time ratings as non-independent. But `pearson_r_low`/`pearson_r_high`, the interval for the same cell, comes from a plain, unblocked bootstrap that resamples individual report rows as if each were independent (`analytics.py:6550-6572`, `rng.integers(0, idx.size, ...)`, no block structure at all). On a participant who rates roughly 1.9-2.4 times a day (599 of 764 reports over 315 days, decision 119), that interval is very likely narrower than it should be — the p-value is honest about day-to-day similarity, the interval is not. This does not change today's conclusion for the CRITICAL findings above (the interval isn't shown), but it will matter the moment it is. **Fix (backend):** rebuild the interval with the same block-resampling scheme used for the permutation null, and quantify the difference on live data before shipping either version as displayed.

**MEDIUM — Every one of the 220 raw grid cells shows a bare number on hover, with no n and no p.** Only the one winning cell per band column ever gets a corrected statistic computed at all (`best_correlation_rows` is one row per centre, not one per cell); the other 198 cells have no such statistic to show even if the frontend were fixed. This is a real limit of what is computed, not just what is displayed, and is fine as designed (computing 220 bootstrap intervals per contact would be expensive) — but it means "hover any cell for context" is not actually available anywhere in this grid, and the page does not say so.

## What is done well

The statistics here are genuinely careful in places most dashboards are not: a selection-aware null (decision, `_best_of_windows_null_correlation`) that corrects for having tried ten lengths of signal and picked the best, computed with a circular block permutation rather than a naive shuffle; a second, independent Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the grid's own 22 band centres, kept separate per contact and per grid (never pooled across channels — verified by test name); a historical, per-band, per-chunk 99.5th-percentile outlier ceiling applied to individual 3-second recording pieces *before* averaging, chosen specifically because applying the same ceiling to an already-averaged cell was measured to distort the exclusion rate 2-3x down the length-of-signal axis; an honest three-state answer (established / not resolved / not assessed) that never manufactures "no relationship" from an absent measurement; explicit, counted flagging of cells answered from the device's own 30-second FFT snapshots rather than the higher-resolution voltage trace, with the exact share printed in an orange caption; and a clearly labelled distinction, in the code and in the drawer text, between the grid's own corrected statistic and the plain one shown on click. The band centres and 5 Hz width match the device's real 8-30 Hz sensing range exactly (decision 32), so a band a clinician reads off this page is one the Percept RC can actually be set to sense.

## Three things a clinician could be misled by

1. **Reading a circled cell as "ready to program."** The drawer text itself says a circled cell is "a research finding, not a sign a setting is ready for the device" — but that sentence sits inside a closed-by-default drawer, not beside the circle.
2. **Reading n = 117 "pain reports" as 117 independent trials.** These are self-ratings from one person, filed roughly twice a day, with an interval computed as if they were unrelated draws (see the block-permutation-vs-plain-bootstrap finding above).
3. **Reading a 5-minute-long best cell as directly programmable.** The device cannot average for 5 minutes in closed-loop mode (0-30 s documented range); the page does not say when its own best answer exceeds what the device can deliver.
