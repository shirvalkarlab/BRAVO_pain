## Findings

**CRITICAL 1 — the same rating means something different, silently, in three different places, and nothing cross-checks them.**
`Biomarkers/routines/local_time.py:33` buckets a rating into a whole California day; `availability.py:1946` (`live_lsb_spectrum_match`) ties a rating to one specific 3-second recording inside a tolerance window (`MatchToleranceMin`, default up to 60 minutes, decision 120); `StimOptimizer/adapter.py:429` (`attach_pros`) ties a rating to whichever device-programmed block was running when she filed it — which decision 130 shows can run for days between exports. Decision 118 already measured, on this same 60-minute window, that a single report can be claimed by up to 24 recording sessions at once; nothing analogous was measured for how many DIFFERENT device-setting blocks share one rating in the Stim Optimizer path, but by construction it is unbounded — a rating filed on day 10 of an unchanged-setting stretch that has run since day 1 counts as evidence for the whole stretch, not for "10 days after the change." **Clinical consequence:** the Biomarkers grid could show band power tracking pain tightly at one contact while the Stim Optimizer picture, built from the same ratings assigned to long device-setting blocks, shows no resolvable current effect — and a clinician has no way to know the disagreement is a matching artifact rather than a real biological difference. **Fix (doc, then backend):** add one paragraph to `ARCHITECTURE_modules_and_store.md` naming the three rules explicitly and why each exists; then, as a backend follow-up, report on each page how many OTHER analyses' epochs/windows the same rating set was also assigned to, so a reader can see when two pages are drawing on non-overlapping slices of the same 4-5 ratings a day.

**HIGH 2 — the calibrated grid's confidence interval is not block-bootstrapped, even though its p-value is.**
`analytics.py:6546-6572`: the interval on the reported correlation for the winning cell resamples pain-report ROWS with ordinary replacement, no block structure, while the p-value two functions away (`_best_of_windows_null_correlation`) explicitly uses a circular block/rotation permutation because "pain scores on nearby days resemble each other." Decision 111 measured that two ratings an hour apart differ by only 0.365 NRS points — they carry almost no independent information relative to a plain i.i.d. resample, which assumes each row IS independent. **Clinical consequence:** the printed interval on the best band's correlation is narrower than the data justify, in the same direction that made decision 19 add a block bootstrap to the older routine in the first place. **Fix (backend):** reuse the already-computed `block_length_for`/`circular_block_perm_matrix` machinery to resample whole blocks of ratings for the interval too, in `_best_rows_correlation` (`analytics.py:6544` onward); pin with a test that the interval widens on a hand-built series with strong lag-1 autocorrelation.

**HIGH 3 — the honest-current "coverage" check counts ratings, not independent occasions.**
`StimOptimizer/stage1_openloop.py:486` (`current_coverage`) requires 5 REPORTS per (left mA, right mA) pair before that pair counts toward resolving a recommendation (`CURRENT_COVERAGE_MIN_REPORTS_PER_PAIR = 5`). It groups by exact current pair and sums report counts with no check on how far apart in time those 5 reports were filed. For the in-clinic titration ladder (2 minutes per step, decision 160), 5 REDCap ratings could never be filed inside one step; the function is really being fed by the separately-ingested clinic-sheet stream for that case (decision 161), which is correct — but the SAME function, with the SAME floor, is also applied to the REDCap-epoch stream, where 5 ratings filed within one afternoon at an unchanged home setting would satisfy the floor exactly as well as 5 ratings spread over 5 days, given decision 111's finding that same-day ratings are close to redundant. **Clinical consequence:** the "coverage" check can be satisfied by 5 near-duplicate numbers, giving false confidence that a current pair has been genuinely explored. **Fix (backend):** add a minimum time-span requirement per pair (e.g., ratings must span at least N hours, or come from at least 2 distinct calendar days) alongside the existing count, mirroring the span-on-each-axis check already present for the design as a whole.

**HIGH 4 — the visit-to-visit drift the record already contains is never surfaced next to a pooled number.**
Decision 16 found a declining transient after a configuration change that only stabilized after 2026-02-15; decision 99 found the SAME point moving from p = 0.29 to p = 0.03 across a handful of days purely because the record grew. Every pooled statistic in `ClosedLoopDeployment` (E1, the pooled shape, the robustness interval) carries `n_clusters`/run count, satisfying House Rule 2's letter, but none report a per-visit spread or a monotone-drift check. **Clinical consequence:** a pooled slope built from 3-4 visits could be dominated by one recent visit's shift, and a clinician reading "13 points, 4 runs" cannot tell that from "13 points, 4 runs, stable." **Fix (backend):** in `amplitude_effect.pooled_table_from_build` / `within_visit.py`, report the per-visit point estimate alongside the pooled one and a simple monotonicity flag; surface it as one line next to E1 on the evidence-triangle panel, Closed-Loop Deployment page.

**MEDIUM 5 — the occupancy check's known averaging-duration sensitivity (decision 153) is not shown next to the number it affects.**
`ClosedLoopDeployment/occupancy.py:98` computes the between-thresholds fraction at whatever averaging duration the timing card recommends (3 s today), and states that duration in its sentence — good. But decision 153 itself measured that the SAME committed band read 2.0% between at 3 s and roughly 15-21% at 30 s, with the off-centre warning flipping on and off between the two. None of that sensitivity reaches the card. **Clinical consequence:** a clinician who later reprograms the device to run its native 30 s averaging (which is still what many Percept clinics actually use) would see a materially different occupancy picture than the one on the card today, with no warning that this could happen. **Fix (frontend + backend):** compute occupancy at both 3 s and the device's native 30 s and show both numbers, or at minimum a one-line caveat: "this fraction is sensitive to averaging duration; at 30 s averaging it reads differently (see decision 153)."

**MEDIUM 6 — the Stim Optimizer's "no current can be recommended" sentence does not name which of the three checks failed or by how much.**
Decision 158 built three explicit checks (not-flat, beats-today, coverage) with named thresholds, but the frontend surface described in decision 159/400708d2 wraps this into a single plain sentence. The underlying `sentence` field built in `_rate_stratum_resolution` (`stage1_openloop.py:523`) does carry the individual reasons, but whether that full text (rather than a summary) reaches the page was not directly confirmed here. **Clinical consequence:** a clinician cannot tell whether "no current" means "we haven't tried enough combinations yet" (an invitation to titrate) versus "the effect is too small to matter" (a different clinical conclusion) without opening the JSON. **Fix (frontend):** render all three named checks with their pass/fail state next to the current-map picture, not only the final verdict sentence.

**LOW 7 — clinic-sheet ingestion has no scheduled job in this repository.**
`manage.py ingest_clinic_sheets` (decision 161) exists as a management command; the daily ingest script named in decision 130 (`~/dev/bravo_daily_ingest.sh`) runs `ingest_percept_folder` only. No grep of this repository or its scripts turns up a scheduled call to `ingest_clinic_sheets`. **Fix (protocol/doc):** either add it to the daily launchd job or write a one-line runbook step ("after every clinic visit, run X") into `OPERATIONS_runbook.md`.

## What Wednesday's session must produce, and whether the platform can use it

For the full chain (recordings → tiles → three-source comparison → pooled slope → E1 → verdict, AND clinic scores → `clinic_pain_steps` → `rate_strata_clinic` → the honest-current checks) to actually resolve on this record, Wednesday's session needs:

1. **Continuous streaming throughout the ladder**, at ONE fixed rate, so the resulting recordings tile cleanly and are not split across rate changes — the device side is automatic once recordings are exported and the daily ingest runs; nothing further is needed there.
2. **At least 8 settled current steps per side** (the curvature floor, decisions 55/56) — decision 160's ladder already delivers 10 distinct currents up plus a 1.0 mA down-leg, which clears this.
3. **A pain score recorded at (or immediately after) each step**, on the clinic sheet, not only in REDCap — this is what feeds `current_coverage`'s 5-reports-per-pair, 6-pairs, 1 mA-span floor, and it will NOT be filled by REDCap alone given the 2-minute step duration (Finding 3).
4. **A person running `manage.py ingest_clinic_sheets`** after the session, since it is not automatic (Finding 7) — without this step, the recording side of the chain updates but the clinic-pain side does not, and the platform will silently keep serving the pre-session "no current can be recommended" answer.

If all four happen, the platform can compute the full chain end to end without further code changes; the gaps above (Findings 1-6) affect how MUCH to trust the numbers it produces, not whether it can produce them.

## Three things a clinician could be misled by

1. **Seeing the Biomarkers grid and the Stim Optimizer picture disagree and reading that as a real physiological contradiction**, when it may simply be that the two are drawing on different slices of the same handful of daily ratings (Finding 1) — one tied to a specific 3-second recording, the other to a multi-day device-setting block.
2. **Trusting the tightness of a printed confidence interval on the biomarker grid's best band**, when the interval itself was built as if every pain rating were independent, while the p-value next to it was built with the opposite, more honest assumption (Finding 2).
3. **Seeing "no current can be recommended" on the Stim Optimizer page and reading it as "the current has no effect"**, when it may only mean the coverage check has not yet been satisfied — which, given the 2-minute clinic step duration, can only be satisfied by the separately-ingested clinic sheet, and only after someone runs the ingest command by hand (Findings 3, 6, 7).
