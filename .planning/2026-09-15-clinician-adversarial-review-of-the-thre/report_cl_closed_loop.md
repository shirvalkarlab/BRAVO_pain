## Findings

**CRITICAL — The evidence triangle's amplitude→power edge (E1) silently falls back to a "screening statistic only" that the module's own documentation says must not be read as real, with no visual difference from a properly-measured edge.**
Verified live: for the committed band (L 0-2+, 24.5 Hz) `implied_control_direction` reports `"within_visit_direction": "not assessed"` because this contact has only 5 settled current points across 1 visit — below the curvature test's 8-point floor (decisions 55/56). Per decision 126, the clean pooled-titration-slope should replace the historical edge in this exact situation, but the swap only happens *when a pooled slope is actually stored*, and here it explicitly is not (`within_visit_pooled_shape.reason`: "the comparison was built from only the runs the page draws, and a pooled answer from a fraction of the visits must not be stored"). So `edges.E1` is the *old*, time-confounded, setting-epoch estimate (32,466 samples pooled across 59 historical settings over the full clinical record, not one clean titration ladder), and its own machine-written note says, verbatim: **"SCREENING STATISTIC ONLY... this cannot be read as the causal effect of amplitude on power... confounded by time, impedance drift, concurrent rate changes."** That caveat sits inside a folded "How this edge was estimated" accordion, closed by default. On the triangle graph itself (`EvidenceTrianglePanel.js`), E1 is drawn with a solid line and a full arrowhead — the exact same visual treatment as a properly resolved, unconfounded edge — because the panel's `resolved` flag only checks whether the point estimate is nonzero, not whether it is the confounded fallback. A clinician skimming the picture, not the fold, sees a confident arrow for the one edge whose own note disclaims causal meaning.
*Fix (frontend):* `EvidenceTrianglePanel.js`'s `edgeInk`/arrow logic should read a `screening_only` flag (backend needs to emit one on `edges.E1` whenever it is serving the historical/confounded estimate rather than the pooled titration slope) and draw that edge with a visibly different stroke (e.g., the same dashed treatment already used for "no point estimate"), not the solid accent line.
*Fix (backend):* `pipeline.run` / `edges.py` should set an explicit `edges.E1.source = "screening" | "pooled_titration"` field rather than leaving the caller to infer it from the prose in `note`.

**HIGH — Two of the four T3–T6 record-derived timing checks explicitly say the record cannot decide the value, and those two values (transitions and detection blanking) are then used, unflagged, inside the headline switch-rate summary the simulation card prints.**
Live: `RECORD_DERIVED_WHY["transition_up_ms"]` and `["detection_blanking_ms"]` are both graded "Low" confidence with the sentence "The record cannot decide this... every ramp on the device's grid is fast against it" / "at a filtering onset every blanking value from 3 to 60 s gives the identical switching rate." The confidence label *is* shown on the parameter table (small grey text under each row), but the `ClosedLoopSimulationPanel`'s headline sentence ("Closing the loop moves time at the upper amplitude limit from X% to Y%...") is generated from the same run that used these guessed values, with no confidence qualifier carried into the headline text itself.
*Fix (frontend):* When any input parameter behind a drawn simulation carries "Low" confidence, prepend a short qualifier to `headline()` in `ClosedLoopSimulationPanel.js` (e.g. "using two timing values the record cannot determine").

**HIGH — The two occupancy findings (2.0% of readings land between the thresholds; the pair's centre sits 12.4 units above this patient's own median reading, more than the pair's half-width) are exactly the "single threshold in disguise" and "miscentered pair" findings the six-method contest flagged as clinically important — and on the live page they appear only as one paragraph of prose folded under each of the two threshold rows, with no distinct color or icon.**
Live number, verified: `threshold_occupancy.frac_between = 0.0200`, `warning: true`, and the pair's centre is 12.4 device units above the median with a half-width of only 2.6 — i.e., **the pair is centred nearly 5 half-widths away from where this patient's signal actually sits.** This is arguably the single most actionable, most safety-relevant number on the whole page (a mis-centred pair means the device will sit pinned against one amplitude limit almost all the time — confirmed independently by the duty-cycle numbers: 22.2% of controller steps at the upper limit, 21.6% at the lower, longest continuous stretch at the upper limit 204 s and at the lower limit 1,701 s), yet it reaches the clinician as one sentence inside `design_rule_note`/`occupancy_note` text attached to the threshold field row, same font size and same grey ink as every other row's provenance text.
*Fix (frontend):* `PrescriptionPanel.js` should render `occupancy_note` in the warning color (`PAL.warnText`/`warnFill`) whenever the payload's own `warning: true` flag is set, matching the treatment already given to the "peak-side" and "onset inoperative" couplings elsewhere on the same page.

**HIGH — The pooled current→power edge (E1) that decision 147 relies on to call the evidence "supported (provisional)" has both of its two supporting intervals spanning zero, and the coherence sentence that gets printed to a clinician says "supported" before it says "provisional."**
Verified live: `coherence.note` reads, in full: *"...PROVISIONAL: 2 of 3 intervals span zero -- E1 (interval -18.6 to +9.73, p 0.54); E2 (interval -0.0235 to +0.195, p 0.12)..."* This is honestly reported and the code (`EvidenceTrianglePanel.js`) does draw hollow (not filled) markers on both unestablished edges — a real, careful piece of UI. But this session's committed band shows `verdict: "blocked"` for an unrelated reason (three unevaluable device rules), which happens to mask how the page would read if all 51 rules cleared: the top-level verdict badge would read "supported," a word a clinician skimming the header could reasonably act on, on the strength of two of three intervals crossing zero. This is not a bug — it is the PI's explicit rule (decision 147) — but it is exactly the kind of number this review was asked to interrogate adversarially: a sign-only verdict on n=59 confounded clusters (E1) and n=27 pain-report clusters (E2, itself below the module's own 40-cluster reliability floor, requiring a bootstrap) is thin evidence to word as "supported" rather than, say, "not contradicted."

**MEDIUM — The reliable-change (noise) floor rests on 12–15 rating pairs per pain score, and while the pair count is printed, the panel does not say how those pairs are distributed in time — a floor built from pairs clustered in one week is not the same guarantee as one built from pairs spread across the whole record.**
Live: `reliable_change.max_gap_hours = 1.0`, `population_bar = {points: 2.0, fraction: 0.3}` (Farrar et al.). The panel correctly states "gates nothing" and shows the pair/stretch count. It does not show the date range the pairs come from.
*Fix (backend + frontend, low cost):* add `earliest_pair_date`/`latest_pair_date` to each `reliable_change.items[k]` and print it beside "Built from N pairs across M stretches."

**MEDIUM — D01/D02 (off-label use, Parkinson's-only labelling) are, by explicit PI ruling, excluded from "What would change this" and live only inside the collapsed "Show all 51 rules" ledger fold, with no persistent off-label banner anywhere on the page or on the printable sign-off sheet.**
I did not find the string "off-label," "off label," or "not an approved indication" printed as a standing banner in `DeploymentDecisionHeader.js` or `DeploySignoffCard.js` — the two places most likely to be read at a glance or printed and filed. The information exists (D01/D02 rows, `kind: "advisory_failed"`), but a clinician who never expands the ledger's fold, or who prints the sign-off sheet to a chart, would not see the words "not an approved indication" anywhere on paper.
*Fix (frontend, low cost):* one persistent, always-visible line on `DeploySignoffCard.js` stating the device is being used off-label for chronic pain, sourced from `eligibility.deferred`/`advisories` where `rule_id in {"D01","D02"}`.

**LOW — No rule anywhere checks the paused amplitude (D34) against anything; the field is left blank for the clinician with no sanity range at all**, not even the general device envelope (0–25.5 mA) that every other amplitude field prints.
*Fix (backend):* extend `Field_("Paused amplitude", ...)` to carry `range_=GENERAL_ENVELOPE["amp_mA"]` at minimum, since even that coarse bound is better than none.

## What is done well

The design does most of what an adversarial reviewer would ask for by name, and does it honestly rather than cosmetically. The three-state discipline (a number, an interval, or "not assessable" — never a collapsed pass/fail) is applied consistently: `band_stability` reports "cannot tell" for 1 of 1 assessed point and explicitly states "it is neither a pass nor a failure, and it must not be shown as either." The device-rule ledger distinguishes eight different outcome kinds (violated / unevaluable / deferred / advisory-failed / pinned-recorded / etc.) with a distinct actor named on every row, rather than collapsing everything into pass/fail. The simulation card replays *two* timing regimes side by side (as programmed today vs. record-derived recommendation) and states plainly which one the drawn figures reflect, so a reader is never shown one number silently swapped for another. The duty-cycle computation explicitly refuses to call its own fractions "percent of the day" when coverage is low (0.36% here) and says so in the caveat text rather than printing a misleading percentage. The occupancy, design-rule, robustness, and startup-bias checks — four separate, real statistical answers to "is this pair of thresholds sane" — are all wired to the actual threshold rows on the actual page, not left as dead backend code (verified: all four notes are non-null and rendered in `PrescriptionPanel.js`).

## Three things a clinician could be misled by

1. **The evidence-triangle arrow for current→power looks the same whether it is a clean measurement or the confounded historical fallback** (Critical finding above) — the single most consequential visual gap found in this review.
2. **A 2.0%-of-readings-between-thresholds finding — meaning the "Dual Threshold" pair behaves as a single threshold — is buried in prose, not colored as a warning**, on the page's own threshold rows, even though the module computed and flagged it correctly on the backend.
3. **"Supported" is printed before "provisional" in the coherence sentence**, and the top-level verdict badge would read the same warm color whether both intervals of a two-edge pattern exclude zero or span it — a clinician trained to trust a green "supported" badge has to read a full sentence, not glance at a color, to learn that two of three intervals include zero.
