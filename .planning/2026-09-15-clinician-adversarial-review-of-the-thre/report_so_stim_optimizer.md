## Stim Optimizer: what the clinician sees today

The Stim Optimizer page (`Client/src/views/Reports/StimOptimizer/index.js`) opens with a Recompute control, then a card called "What the joint search prefers, per side" (`DecisionStrip.js`), then a table of which brain-signal bands respond to stimulation current (`SensingEvidenceTable.js`), a row of plain counts ("Evidence base"), a card telling the clinician exactly what to test at the next visit ("Titration session to run next," `TitrationSessionCard.js`), a card showing where the two stimulation currents have actually been tried ("Where the two currents have been tried, and what the record says," `CurrentMapCard.js`), a home-testing schedule card, and finally the full technical detail ("Two-stage plan," `TwoStagePlanCard.js`).

As of today (2026-09-15), calling the real backend for RCS08 shows: the module recommends **no specific milliamp current on either side**, on either data source. The open-loop search (module file `stage1_openloop.py`) that used to fit the left and right stimulators as two separate, independent statistical pictures now fits them together in one three-dimensional picture (rate, left current, right current), because the device changes both currents at once and the earlier per-side approach could credit a change in pain to the wrong side. That joint change (decision 157) is real and current.

But a newer safeguard (decision 158, "the honest current rule") now blocks a current recommendation unless three checks all pass: the picture actually varies meaningfully rather than being flat, the best option beats what is running today by more than the normal day-to-day scatter in the pain ratings, and the record has tried at least 6 different (left, right) current combinations with enough ratings and enough spread to actually tell the two currents apart. Verified live today: **0 of 4 fitted (pulse-width pair, rate) combinations from the patient's REDCap pain diary pass all three checks, and 0 of 19 from the clinic and home-testing sheets pass either.** The page says so plainly ("no current can be recommended from this record — see the current map," `DecisionStrip.js` line 69), which is honest and correct given the data.

## Does it collect the data that resolves the closed-loop questions? (question by question)

**Which rate and pulse width to freeze.** Traced through `stage1_openloop.run_stage1` and the gate condition `openloop_choice_resolved` (`routines/stage_gate.py`). Live today: both sides are frozen at 55 Hz, pulse width 60 microseconds on the left and 160 on the right (the settings already in force), but `rate_resolved` is **False** and `pulse_width_resolved` is **None** (not assessable) on both sides. The module is honest that this is inherited, not chosen — every existing (pulse-width-pair, rate) combination is too close to the setting already running, or has too little data, to call it better. This does collect the right kind of evidence (a statistical test against the setting in force, with an explicit "we can't tell" state), but it does not yet resolve the question.

**What current range is safe and useful.** The safety picture (module `safety_ceiling.py`) is seeded from a number the PI stated directly (4.5 mA each side, changed from 5.0 mA on 2026-09-14) plus every setting the device actually delivered for long enough without a reported problem. This IS collected and reported. But "useful" (which current relieves the most pain) is the honest-current rule above, and today it returns nothing for either side.

**Whether band power responds to current, and where the peak is.** This is answered, and answered well, by a different part of the module (`routines/within_visit.py`, `amplitude_response_shape_pooled`) and by the readiness screen shown on this page (`SensingEvidenceTable.js`, fed by `routines/lfp_evidence.py`). Live today: 12 of 18 tested brain-signal bands on the left contact respond to current, and the module correctly pools every visit's current-testing runs (not just one day) and tests for a rise-then-fall shape, not only a straight line. This is a genuine strength — see "what is done well" below.

**Whether the response holds across visits.** Directly addressed. The pooling described above is exactly across-visit, and it is the right design for a patient tested in short bursts on different days (decision 55, 56).

**Whether sensing and stimulation contact can be programmed together.** Partly addressed. The module knows the device rule that in the closed-loop mode a stimulator can only be driven by a sensing signal recorded on the SAME side of the brain (`routines/lfp_evidence.py` line ~1235, `routines/percept_adaptive.py` line ~703), and the titration-session card names which sensing contact to record from, reading it from the readiness screen's own per-side result (`bravo_service.titration_plan_block`, `titration_plan.py` `record_today_for_contact`). It correctly flags, in amber, when the best-passing contact for a side is actually on the OTHER hemisphere (meaning it cannot license closed-loop for that side at all). It does not check the finer device rule of whether the exact same electrode contact can be used for sensing and stimulating simultaneously — that level of device detail was outside what I could confirm in the time available and is worth a direct check against the device manual before Wednesday's session.

## Findings

**CRITICAL — the safety-envelope check now fails for a reason a clinician could easily misread as "the search found an unsafe setting," when it actually means "the device has, in the past, delivered more current than the PI now wants as a ceiling."** Live today, gate condition `amplitude_limits_inside_envelope_and_under_ceiling` (`routines/stage_gate.py` line 839) reads **FAIL**: "Left: upper limit 4.8 mA exceeds the declared ceiling of 4.5 mA." This 4.8 mA is not a proposed new setting — it is the highest current the left side has ever been run at historically, used automatically as a stand-in limit because no actual candidate limits were supplied (`checked=… defaulted to the delivered envelope`, same file lines 866-901). Since the PI lowered the stated ceiling from 5.0 to 4.5 mA on 2026-09-14 (decision 160, `safety_ceiling.py` line 48-51), this check can never pass on the left side until either the ceiling is reconsidered or a real candidate proposal (rather than the historical maximum) is checked. Clinical consequence: a clinician glancing at "closed loop may not start" sees a check named "amplitude limits" failing and could reasonably read it as "the search wants to push current higher than is safe," which is backwards — it is the record's own past exposure, not a proposal. Fix (backend): when no explicit candidate limits are supplied, either drop this condition to "not assessed" rather than "FAIL" (matching how the module already treats other unaskable questions), or state in the one-line detail that the failing number is historical exposure, not a proposed limit. Fix (frontend): `ClosedLoopChecks.js`'s `Numbers()` case for this condition already shows "limits defaulted to the delivered envelope" — add one clause naming that the 4.8 mA number is history, not a proposal, right next to the failing figure.

**HIGH — the amplitude-versus-side-effect statistic used by the safety gate rests on 15 rows, of which only 0-1 carry any real severity above "none," and the page never shows this number to the clinician.** `clinic_pain.amplitude_severity_evidence` (recomputed live per decision 166, "recompute always") returns rho = −0.037, p = 0.895, n = 15 scored steps, 0 of them above 4 mA. This number is carried into the response under both `two_stage.gate.conditions[…].evidence.side_effect_vs_current` and `two_stage.stage1.clinic_stream.side_effect_vs_current`, but `ClosedLoopChecks.js`'s `Numbers()` function, which prints the evidence for the `amplitude_limits_inside_envelope_and_under_ceiling` row, does not read that field at all — it is only ever surfaced as a sentence buried inside a longer refusal paragraph when the check happens to fail on the "above the delivered maximum" branch (`routines/stage_gate.py` lines 887-893). On this page a clinician cannot see, at a glance, that the "side effects don't move with current" statement rests on 15 data points with essentially no scored severity above zero. Clinical consequence: the reassurance ("does not move measurably with current") could be read as more established than 15 thin rows support. Fix (frontend): in `ClosedLoopChecks.js`'s `Numbers()` for that condition, always print `ev.side_effect_vs_current.sentence` (or "not assessable" with its reason) as a small caption, whether the check passes or fails.

**HIGH — the safety model's "tolerated" anchor for the clinic-and-home stream does not require anyone to have actually reported "no side effect"; it only requires that nothing was reported as moderate or severe.** `safety_ceiling.tolerated_anchors` (`safety_ceiling.py` lines 121-135) counts an epoch as severity-0 ("tolerated") whenever the current was above zero, held at least `CLINIC_MIN_TOLERATED_H` = 0.001 hours (3.6 seconds, `clinic_pain.py` line 762), and was not REPORTED moderate or severe — but the vast majority of the 816 recorded clinic steps carry no side-effect score at all (only 15 of them do, per the 2026-09-15 audit). An unscored step is treated the same as a step scored "none." Clinical consequence for question 4: 3.6 seconds is a defensible floor for "this was actually delivered under supervision" (the module's own stated reasoning, which is sound), but it is not the same as "a clinician confirmed there was no problem," and the safety picture cannot currently tell the two apart. Fix (backend): add a distinct anchor tier for steps with an actual "none" score versus steps that were merely delivered with no score recorded, so a future clinician (or the module itself) can see how much of the "tolerated" evidence rests on an observed absence of harm versus an absence of any note.

**MEDIUM — the noise model's duration term cannot discriminate between clinic-sheet steps at all, though its documentation implies it can.** `routines/objective.py`'s `observation_variance` (lines 286-302) inflates uncertainty for "short exposures" using `dur_ref_h = 168.0` (one week) as the reference. For every REDCap chronic-therapy stretch this is the right comparison (days versus a week). For a clinic-sheet step lasting 60 seconds to a few minutes (`dur_h` around 0.017-0.08 hours), the term `max(0, 1 - dur_h/168)^2` sits at 0.9998-0.9995 regardless of whether the step was held 60 seconds or 5 minutes — it contributes essentially the fixed maximum penalty (`c_dur` × ~1.0 = 0.25) to every clinic epoch alike. This is not wrong (a clinic test genuinely has not reached steady state relative to a week), but it means a clinician cannot read "held longer" as "counted more" anywhere in this term; the real driver of a clinic setting's weight is how many times it was repeated (`n`), through the separate `s²/n` term. Fix (doc/backend): state this explicitly in the module docstring, or give the clinic-sheet stream its own, much shorter duration reference (e.g. minutes, not a week) so the term actually discriminates a 60-second test from a 10-minute one.

**MEDIUM — the REDCap and clinic-sheet streams, kept separate as designed, are currently fitted at DIFFERENT pulse-width pairs, and the page never says so.** Live today, the REDCap stream's fitted combinations are (60,160) microseconds and (140,180); the clinic-sheet stream's are (60,160) and (100,100). The two streams share one combination (60 left / 160 right) but the clinic stream's second-best-populated combination (100/100 microseconds) was never fitted on the REDCap side at all, and vice versa. Keeping the two streams unpooled is the right scientific call (they are different data-collection contexts on the same 0-10 scale, and pooling would hide disagreement, per the module's own reasoning). But nothing on the page tells a reader that the two "independent" verdicts may be describing genuinely different historical device configurations rather than two measurements of the same one. Fix (frontend): in `CurrentMapCard.js`'s `ClinicStreamSection`, add one sentence naming which pulse-width pairs the two streams share and which they don't.

**MEDIUM — the titration plan's timing is internally consistent and well-matched to the biomarker analysis, but this was not independently checked against Closed-Loop Deployment's own post-ramp margin switch.** `titration_plan.hold_per_step` (`titration_plan.py` lines 224-235) budgets a 60-second test row as 30 seconds settled window + 20 seconds post-ramp margin + 10 seconds slack, correctly matching `within_visit.RAMP_EXCLUDE_S` (20 s) and `PRE_CHANGE_WINDOW_S` (30 s). This is good arithmetic. However, decision 144 records that the 20-second post-ramp margin is currently switched OFF in the closed-loop analysis (`ClosedLoopDeployment/post_ramp.py`) because on the current record it flips a verdict from two removed points, and the module's own comment says the margin turns on once a titration session like this one exists. A clinician reading only this page has no way to know that the margin this session is designed to unlock is not yet applied anywhere. This is a cross-module note, not a bug in Stim Optimizer itself; flagging it here because it affects whether following this plan "resolves" the question it claims to.

**MEDIUM — the joint model treats a side at 0 mA as an ordinary low point on that side's current axis, which is a real and disclosed change from the old per-side model, but the page never states how much of the fit's precision now rests on those off-currents.** `stage1_openloop.py`'s module docstring states 44 of 120 epochs have one side at 0 mA and the other on; these are now included in the joint fit rather than excluded as "a different therapeutic state." This is a defensible modelling choice (it is what lets the joint fit separate the two currents' effects at all), but the honest-current rule's coverage check (`current_coverage`, `stage1_openloop.py` lines 486-517) counts a (0, x) pair the same as any other current pair when checking "6 distinct combinations with enough spread." A clinician cannot currently see, from the page, how many of the qualifying current combinations behind a future "yes" answer will be zero-current points versus genuinely bilateral ones. Fix (frontend): in `CurrentSurfaceHeatmap`, mark points at 0 mA on either axis with a distinct symbol.

**LOW / documentation — `OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md` and much of `TWO_STAGE_DESIGN.md` describe an architecture (independent per-side fits, a 12×50 two-dimensional grid, an incumbent at epoch 102/2026-08-12) that predates the joint model, the honest-current rule, the titration and current-map cards, and the PI-stated safety ceiling — all built since. Nothing in either file is marked superseded. A reader following the "READ FIRST" instruction on this module, as I was asked to, gets a materially out-of-date picture of how the search works before ever opening the code. Fix (doc): add a short "superseded by" note at the top of both files pointing at `stage1_openloop.py`'s own docstring and the decision log entries 157-166, the way this project already does elsewhere for superseded documents.

**LOW — `clinic_pain.py`'s ingest correctly refuses to guess at free-text side-effect notes (28 of 29 workbooks have no numeric severity column), which is the right, disclosed behaviour, but it means the safety-relevant statistic in the finding above will stay thin (n = 15) until the new sheet format is used consistently at every future visit.** Worth stating plainly to the clinical team rather than treated as a code gap: the fix here is a data-collection habit (always filling in the numeric "SIDE EFFECT / SCORE" column), not a code change.

## What is done well

The joint (left current, right current) model is a real, substantive correction to a genuine confound — the earlier per-side fits could and did credit a pain change to the wrong stimulator, and the module's own documentation shows this was caught by directly measuring the old and new answers against each other, not merely asserted. The honest-current rule (three separate, all-must-pass checks: not flat, beats the incumbent by more than the noise, and enough distinct current combinations tried) is exactly the right kind of caution for a Bayesian search running on 8-24 epochs per rate, and it is currently doing its job: it is correctly saying "no" everywhere on RCS08's real data today rather than printing an overconfident number. The pooled, across-visit response-shape test (`within_visit.amplitude_response_shape_pooled`) correctly tests for a rise-then-fall relationship rather than only a straight line, and pools across visits with a visit-level baseline and cluster-robust statistics, avoiding a documented past mistake (treating repeated, non-independent measurements as if they were all independent). The titration and home-current-map cards are genuinely actionable: they name a specific ladder of currents, a specific hold time with its own arithmetic shown, which sensing contact to use, and which brain-signal bands to avoid because they would just be measuring the stimulator's own electrical signal rather than the brain. The safety ceiling now lives in exactly one place in the code (`safety_ceiling.PI_STATED_CEILING_MA`) with its date and the PI's own words attached, which is a real improvement over the earlier practice of inferring a ceiling from device-programmed ranges that were not actually safety limits.

## Three things a clinician could be misled by

1. **A failing "amplitude limits" check reads as "the plan wants an unsafe current," when today it actually means "the device has, in the past, been run above the currently-stated ceiling."** (Critical finding above.)
2. **The "side effects do not move with current" reassurance, quoted inside a gate condition's detail text, does not show its own sample size (15 scored steps) anywhere a reader would notice it, even though the module computes and carries that number on every request.** (High finding above.)
3. **"No current can be recommended" (both streams, today) could be read as "the two independent streams agree there is nothing to find," when in fact the two streams are currently evaluating different pulse-width combinations of the device and have not actually been compared to each other on the same setting.** (Medium finding above.)
