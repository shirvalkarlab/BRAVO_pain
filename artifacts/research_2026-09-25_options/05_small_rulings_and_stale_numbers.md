# Option 5: three small rulings, plus a check for stale numbers

> Research report
> Researcher: worker-research | Date: 2026-09-25
> Confidence: MIXED — part (a) rests on general pain and DBS literature plus this project's own dataset findings, not a study that tested this exact comparison; part (b) is a plain arithmetic correction plus a code-reproducibility problem found while checking it; part (c) is a direct file search.

## Executive summary

The clinic-sheet ratings that can be switched into the Closed-Loop deployment summary are not simple extra data points: they are collected during active, explicit stimulation-amplitude testing (in clinic or at a home testing visit), while REDCap ratings are collected during ordinary daily life. The pain literature says ratings taken this way are shaped by expectation, awareness of the setting being changed, and the presence of a person running the test — mechanisms that have nothing to do with any brain signal — and this project's own record shows the same warning sign twice (11 to 22 of 22 bands "fall with pain" together once these ratings are included, and the deployment summary's area-under-the-curve score gets worse, not better, 0.623 to 0.564, when they are added). The recommendation is to keep the button off by default, as it is today, and to build one more check before trusting the "on" number: run it with the stimulation current taken out, using machinery this project already built for the heat-map grid. Separately, checking decision 262's correction of decision 240 turned up that the specific "p = 0.04" figure being asked about cannot currently be reproduced from the tested code at all — the module that computes it only calculates a chance-level comparison for the plain reading, not the current-removed one — so that number came from a one-off scratch script that no longer exists. A file search for old, superseded numbers found no live page text was wrong, but three research documents from 2026-09-24 still state decision 240's retracted figures as if they were current fact, and a completed plan's log still repeats the daily-cycle finding that decision 265 overturned.

## (a) Clinic-sheet ratings in the deployment summary: evidence, options, recommendation

### What is being switched on

On the Closed-Loop page, a button reading "Clinic-sheet ratings in the summary: on / off" (`Client/src/views/Reports/ClosedLoopSim/ClinicSheetsSummaryButton.js:36`) controls whether the sign-off sheet's deployment ROC — the curve that says how well a candidate band's power tells apart the patient's worse-pain days from their better ones — is built from REDCap reports alone, or from REDCap reports plus the ratings entered on the lab's clinic/home-testing workbooks (decision 258). The merge itself happens once, in one shared helper the heat-map grid also uses (`BRAVO/modules/Biomarkers/bravo_service.py`, tests in `BRAVO/modules/Biomarkers/tests/test_sheet_ratings_in_summary.py`), and the sign-off sheet always prints which ratings were used (`Client/src/views/Reports/ClosedLoopSim/DeploySignoffCard.js:467`, "Pain ratings used: REDCap reports plus N clinic-sheet ratings"). The default is off (decisions 186, 258).

**What these workbooks actually are.** They are not simply "clinic visits." Per the module that reads them (`BRAVO/modules/StimOptimizer/clinic_pain.py:1-14`), they are "the lab's clinic and at-home testing workbooks," imported on the PI's 2026-09-14 instruction to capture "in-clinic AND at-home testing visits" as pain ratings tied to specific, deliberately-changed amplitude steps — the same rows `ClosedLoopDeployment/clinic_steps.py` already uses for the ramp/settling-window analysis. So a "clinic-sheet" rating is, structurally, a rating recorded at (or immediately around) one step of a deliberate current ladder, whether that ladder ran in the clinic room or during a scheduled home test — not a rating taken during quiet daily life the way a REDCap entry is.

### Evidence this project already has

- **Decision 229** (an exploratory search across 252 combinations of window length, direction, and rating cap): with clinic-sheet ratings turned off, at most a handful of bands out of 22 look positive with pain in any one setting, matching what you would expect from real, band-specific signal mixed with noise. With clinic-sheet ratings turned ON, 11 to 22 of the 22 bands fall with pain together in most settings (1,545 rows below the 5%-chance mark once corrected for testing 22 bands at once). A biological signal specific to one band does not usually move every band on the sensing pair together; a shared cause that moves the whole recording at once (the current itself, or something about the testing session) usually does. This is the single strongest piece of evidence in the record that clinic-sheet ratings are picking up something structural about how and when they were taken, not just adding more of the same kind of data REDCap already has.
- **Decision 234** built the tool needed to check this directly: it can recompute a band's correlation with pain after mathematically removing the effect of whatever stimulation current was running when each rating was taken. It has not yet been run specifically on the clinic-sheet-included version of the deployment summary (see recommendation below).
- **Decision 258's own live measurement**, read directly from `DECISIONS_and_open_items.md`: on RCS08, band L 1-3+ at 24.5 Hz, Left Leg VAS score — with the button off, the deployment ROC reads 0.623 (95% range 0.463 to 0.731); with it on, adding 207 clinic-sheet ratings, it reads 0.564 (0.396 to 0.684). Adding data made the band look *less* able to tell high pain from low pain, not more. That is the opposite of what you would expect from simply having more of the same kind of measurement, and it is consistent with the added ratings being a systematically different kind of measurement.

### What the pain and DBS literature says about ratings taken this way

I could not find a study that measures, directly, whether a pain scale (VAS or a 0-10 number scale) means the same thing when it is filled out during an active stimulation-amplitude test as when it is filled out at home on an ordinary day (see Unverified, below — this looks like a genuine gap in the published literature, not a failed search). The following studies bear on the pieces of the question:

- **Ratings collected during active DBS programming can be internally consistent, but only under conditions RCS08's clinic sheets do not document as met.** Off et al. (2026, *Brain Communications*) had 15 Parkinson's patients rate the same repeated subthalamic stimulation setting during programming and found good test-retest agreement (Spearman r = 0.70; 83% of repeats within 2 points on the scale) — but only because the protocol **blinded patients to the actual stimulation parameter**, used a fixed stimulation duration (a 15-second test produced systematically lower, not just noisier, scores than 60-120 seconds), and used a standardized "washout" period before each test. RCS08's clinic sheets, by the project's own description, record the amplitude and pain score together, openly, at each ladder step — none of the controls that made the Off et al. numbers trustworthy are described as present here.
- **Verbal suggestion moves pain ratings more than the stimulation change itself does, and patients can tell when the setting is being changed even when not told.** Rosenkjær et al. (2024, *Scandinavian Journal of Pain*), 24 Parkinson's patients: "stimulation does not seem to influence ongoing and evoked pain, but verbal suggestions may influence pain levels," and patients could detect when stimulation was changed, which the authors flag as a blinding problem for any future trial. This is the closest direct evidence that a pain rating given in the middle of a deliberate stimulation change is shaped by the patient's awareness of and expectation about that change — a mechanism no biomarker on this platform models or removes.
- **Placebo, nocebo, and lesion-related effects on DBS outcome measures are a recognized, under-controlled problem specifically in programming-visit assessments.** Mestre, Lang, and Okun (2016, *Movement Disorders*) review this directly and argue it requires deliberate trial design (blinding, sham) to separate from a true stimulation effect — which a routine clinical titration visit, by its nature, does not have.
- **The clinical encounter itself is a general, well-documented driver of pain-report shifts (placebo/nocebo), independent of the biology being tested.** Rossettini, Carlino, and Testa (2018, *BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders*) review how the patient-provider relationship, the setting, and explicit belief manipulation shift reported pain in musculoskeletal conditions; the mechanism (expectation and context, not the treatment itself) generalizes to any face-to-face intervention, including a DBS titration visit.
- **Ratings that require recall, or that are given under conditions of heightened attention to the symptom, are prone to a peak/recency bias not present in a plain momentary rating.** Stone, Schwartz, Broderick, and Shiffman (2005, *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*) is the foundational study; May, Junghaenel, Ono, Stone, and Schneider (2018, *The Journal of Pain*), a systematic review of 105 EMA studies, documents how much the specific rating method and scale construction shape what gets measured; Mindlis, Reid, and Rodebaugh (2026, *Primary Health Care Research & Development*, a small pilot, n=18) found real-time and later-recalled ratings of pain diverge, with pain showing the largest gap of the symptoms they tracked. None of these studied a DBS-titration visit specifically, but together they support treating "a rating taken while actively being tested" as a different measurement condition from "a rating entered on an ordinary day," which is exactly the distinction between clinic-sheet and REDCap ratings here.

### Options

1. **Keep the default off, keep the button** (status quo, decisions 186/258). Conservative: does not present a number built by mixing two measurement conditions as the headline unless someone deliberately asks for it.
2. **Make the default on.** Not recommended on current evidence: it would make the default headline number *worse* (0.564 instead of 0.623) with no demonstrated gain in accuracy, only a demonstrated change in what is being measured.
3. **Always show both numbers side by side**, rather than a toggle that hides one. More transparent than a toggle, at the cost of one more line on an already dense sign-off sheet.
4. **Keep the toggle, but before trusting the "on" number, run it through the current-taking-out machinery already built for the heat-map grid (decision 234) and for the evidence triangle (decision 242).** This has not been built yet for the deployment summary specifically. It would answer, directly and for the first time, whether the 0.623-to-0.564 drop is explained by the clinic-sheet ratings being concentrated during the moments when current is actively moving — turning an unexplained discrepancy into a described one, which is what the house rules and decision 233's ruling on descriptive-only adjustments already ask for.

### Recommendation

Keep the default off. The evidence — both the general literature on expectation, unblinding, and clinical-encounter effects during DBS programming, and this project's own dataset (decision 229's near-universal band effect, decision 258's own AUC drop) — points the same way: these are two different measurement conditions, and merging them by default would present a number that looks like "more data, so more trustworthy" when the record suggests the opposite. Recommend, as a build item for later (not yet built, and this is the PI's call to schedule): extend decision 234's current-adjustment to the deployment-summary ROC specifically for the sheets-on case, so the sign-off sheet can state in one sentence whether the drop survives once the concurrent current is taken out. This is the PI's ruling to make; the above is evidence and a recommendation, not a decision.

## (b) Reporting the corrected 240 numbers: the multiplicity arithmetic and recommended wording

### What decision 262(c) actually found, read from the source table

`artifacts/research_2026-09-24_SYNTHESIS_state_washin_threshold_offperiods.md` §4c gives the table decision 262(c) summarizes. At the page's real 60-minute matching window (not the buggy 60-second window decision 240 had accidentally used), on the left sensing pair (L 1-3+):

| L 1-3+ | 30 s of signal (201 reports) | 60 s of signal (187 reports) |
|---|---|---|
| current alone | 0.600 | 0.708 |
| every band | 0.722 (chance-level 95th 0.716, p 0.035) | 0.743 (chance-level 95th 0.673, p 0.040) |
| every band, current taken out | 0.681, inside the chance range | 0.683, **outside** the chance range |

The one number Option 5 asks about — the bands-with-current-removed score of 0.683 against a chance-level ceiling of 0.673 at 60 seconds of signal — is the only one of these that lands outside its own chance range. The SYNTHESIS document states this came with "p 0.04, uncorrected for the 12 pair-and-length combinations tried" (also in `docs/decision_log_full_2026-09-19.md` row 262 and `DECISIONS_and_open_items.md` Part 3, decision 262). The document also states, for one of the other combinations it names individually, "the other five pairs stay inside their nulls (R 0-3+, 459 reports: bands 0.445, current alone 0.357)" — i.e., at least one other combination was checked and was not close to crossing its own chance range.

### The multiplicity arithmetic

Treat p = 0.04 as one result out of a family of m = 12 things tried (two sensing pairs across several signal lengths).

- **Bonferroni (multiply the p-value by how many things were tried):** 12 x 0.04 = **0.48**.
- **Holm's step-down correction:** for the single smallest p-value in a family, Holm's first step is identical to Bonferroni: min(1, m x p) = **0.48**. (Holm can only give a *smaller* number than Bonferroni for p-values *other* than the smallest one in the family; since the only other individually-reported result, R 0-3+, was described as staying inside its own chance range — i.e., not a small p-value — there is no reason to think this 0.04 is not the smallest, or close to it, in the family.)
- **Benjamini-Hochberg (this project's own standard correction for testing many bands at once, decision 63):** the corrected value for the smallest p-value in a family can never be *larger* than the Bonferroni number, only equal to or smaller. Without the other 11 raw p-values (see below), an exact BH figure cannot be computed, but it is capped at 0.48 and, given that the only other individually-quoted result was well inside chance, is unlikely to be much below it.

**All three honest corrections land in the same place: roughly a 1-in-2 result, not a 1-in-25 one.** This is not a new, harsher standard being applied after the fact — it is the same kind of correction this project already applies as a matter of course to its 22-band heat-map grids (decision 63) and states explicitly should never be skipped when many things are tried (decision 240's own text: "uncorrected for the eight combinations tried"; decision 262's: "uncorrected for the 12... combinations tried"). Ruling 233(2) already establishes that any current-adjusted reading is descriptive only and must never move a verdict or re-select a band; this arithmetic is the reason that ruling matters here specifically.

### A reproducibility problem found while checking this

Reading the actual code that produces this kind of number (per CLAUDE.md §8 rule 11 — open it and read the values, do not trust the shape of the answer):

- `BRAVO/modules/Biomarkers/routines/confound_diagnostic.py` (`pre_build_diagnostic`, lines 194-213) computes a chance-level comparison (a shuffled-data p-value) **only for the plain "every band" reading** (`plain["p_value"]`). It does **not** compute an equivalent p-value for the "bands with the current taken out" reading anywhere in this module — the module only reports whether that reading's score is above or below the chance range's 95th percentile, never a p-value for it.
- The live page feature that runs this exact check today, `BRAVO/modules/ControlAnalyses/runners.py:241-292` (`run_current_explains`, wired into the Control Analyses card on the Biomarkers and Stim Optimizer pages per decision 264), confirms this: it stores `p=d["bands_plain"].get("p_value")` — the plain reading's p-value — and, for the current-removed reading, only whether it is "outside" or "inside" the chance range (line 284, `outside = ... r["bands_without_current"] > r["null_p95"]`). The rendered figure (`Client/src/views/Reports/ControlAnalyses/figures.js:106-138`) draws three dots and a tick mark for the chance-range boundary; it never prints a p-value for the current-removed reading either.
- The live card also only runs this check at **two lengths (30 s, 60 s) times however many sensing pairs have usable data** (`lengths=(30.0, 60.0)` at `runners.py:241`) — for RCS08's two usable pairs, that is a family of **4**, not the 12 the decision log cites.
- **Conclusion: the specific "p = 0.04" figure in decision 262(c) cannot currently be reproduced from the tested, shipped code.** It came from a one-off script, `_probe_confound_live.py`, that lived only under `BRAVO/_agent_bridge/_*` — a location `CLAUDE.md` §5 states is disposable scratch, never preserved — and it is gone. Nor is it clear that the probe's family of 12 is the same family the live Control Analyses card runs (4), or a larger one it does not.

### Recommended wording

Never state "p = 0.04" alone, on any page or in any future document, for this finding. Recommended phrasing, matching the plain style the Control Analyses card's own reading text already uses (`runners.py:285-287`, "outside the shuffled-data 95th"):

> "On the left sensing pair, at 60 seconds of signal, the band powers with the stimulation current taken out score 0.683 on data the check never saw, just outside the range shuffled data reaches by chance (0.673). This was one of several such checks tried; correcting for that (the same kind of correction this project already applies to its 22-band grids) puts the result at roughly even odds, not 1-in-25 — read it as a lead, not a finding. It changes no band recommendation (ruling 233)."

As a build item (not yet built): extend `confound_diagnostic.pre_build_diagnostic` to also compute a shuffled-data p-value for the current-removed reading (mirroring the null loop it already runs for the plain reading, at line 199-212, but refitting `all_bands_auc(..., covar=c)` per shuffle instead of the unadjusted call), pin it with a test, and decide — as a matter of code, not a one-off script — exactly which combinations count as "the family" so a real Benjamini-Hochberg number can be quoted rather than only a Bonferroni ceiling.

## Feasibility risks

- **The exact family behind decision 262(c)'s "12 combinations" cannot be reconstructed.** The probe script that produced it lived only under `BRAVO/_agent_bridge/_*`, which is disposable scratch by project rule (`CLAUDE.md` §5); it is gone, and no surviving document lists the other 10 combinations' raw scores. Any attempt to "fix" the multiplicity correction exactly, rather than build a new, tested version, will not reproduce the original number.
- **Any change to the deployment summary's default (clinic sheets on/off) touches the live sign-off sheet a clinician reads before programming a device setting.** Per rule 8, plan approval is not execution authority — this needs the PI's explicit go-ahead, and any change should ship with the project's standard live proof (field count and difference count on the live participant, never a tolerance) before being trusted.
- **Building the current-adjusted deployment-summary ROC (the recommendation in part (a)) reuses decision 234/242's machinery, but is new wiring in `ClosedLoopDeployment`/`Biomarkers` sign-off code.** It does not touch the three files under standing do-not-edit (`resultCache.js`, `useCachedResult.js`, `RecomputeBar.js`), but should be scoped narrowly to avoid them.
- **The literature on ratings taken during active stimulation change is almost entirely from Parkinson's DBS programming, not chronic-pain DBS.** The one study measuring pain specifically during a stimulation change (Rosenkjær et al. 2024) used experimentally-evoked pain in 24 Parkinson's patients, not a chronic-pain patient's own ongoing pain; transfer to RCS08 is plausible, not established.
- **No published study measuring whether a pain scale means the same thing in a titration-visit context versus a home/EMA context was found.** Three separate search strategies (general "measurement invariance," clinic-versus-home NRS comparability, and EMA-versus-clinic-visit comparability) came back without a directly-on-point study; this looks like a real gap in the literature, and the recommendation in part (a) rests on adjacent evidence (expectation/unblinding effects, clinical-encounter placebo/nocebo effects, recall bias) rather than a single decisive study.
