## What the synthesis gets right

- It caught its own source report's factual error (report 3 misdating the titration session and claiming
  the carry-over check had not been run) by cross-checking against the decision log rather than trusting
  the report — good practice, and it did not just silently correct it, it explained the discrepancy
  (§3a).
- Reading `confound_diagnostic.py` and `ControlAnalyses/runners.py` directly, rather than trusting the
  decision log's "p = 0.04," and finding that the shipped code cannot reproduce that figure, is exactly
  the kind of "open it and read the values" verification `CLAUDE.md` §8 rule 11 asks for, and it is the
  single best piece of adversarial work in the whole batch (§3b).
- Flagging the stale-pain-rating risk in the Closed-Loop evidence-inputs cache (a possible violation of
  CLAUDE.md §8 rule 5, a pain rating hiding in a payload keyed on something else) as higher priority than
  any of the seven scientific options is the right call: it is cheap, it is a correctness question rather
  than a scientific judgment call, and if real it would silently undermine numbers already on a clinician's
  screen.
- Recommending clinic-sheet ratings stay off by default, and be reported side by side rather than merged,
  is a defensible, appropriately conservative reading of the converging (if imperfect) evidence — decision
  229's near-universal band effect and decision 258's AUC drop both point the same direction, even if the
  explanation offered for why (M1, above) is incomplete.
- Declining to reverse decision 196 (no time input) given the already-measured identifiability failure
  (decision 194: "time soaked up the settings' effect," collinearity of -0.61/+0.39/-0.48) is the right
  statistical instinct: none of the published time-varying methods surveyed in report 3 were validated
  under this record's own entanglement of settings and calendar time.

## Recommendations that should change

1. Do not sequence the rate swap (report 2, Part 2) behind any engineering Lane A/B item; it has no
   dependency on them and clinic scheduling runs on its own clock. Start the sign-off process for it now,
   in parallel (addresses C3).
2. Before quoting a corrected p-value for the confound-diagnostic finding again, fix the actual family
   size in code (already proposed) and report the correction as a range, not a single number, until that
   family is verifiable (addresses C2).
3. Re-test decision 253's "the setting did not change and the patient's response to it did" against the
   simpler explanation that the whole record's pain has been declining regardless of setting (r=-0.61 with
   days already on record); do not repeat that sentence in a future document until report 1's Step 6, or
   an equivalent trend-adjusted check, has actually run (addresses C1).
4. Require a number (sample size, currents, effect size, interval) alongside every future citation of
   decision 272's carry-over finding (addresses C4).
5. Add a day-to-day (or week-to-week) autocorrelation estimate for the REDCap pain series and use it to
   correct the "52-98 independent days" and similar power figures in reports 1 and 2 before they are used
   to argue what a realistic visit schedule can or cannot achieve (addresses M2).
6. When the rate-swap or any single-visit "decisive" test is run, pre-specify a minimum detectable effect
   and treat a null result as inconclusive rather than confirmatory unless that threshold is met
   (addresses M3).
7. Add one control to the clinic-sheet investigation: check whether bands far from any plausible pain
   relationship (very low or very high frequency, or already flagged as harmonic-adjacent) also move in
   lockstep with current during titration. If they do, an electrical-artifact explanation is at least as
   supported as the psychological one currently favored, and it points to a different fix (exclude
   affected bands, not exclude a rating source) (addresses M1).
