# Offline RL and related methods for single-patient pain DBS settings: literature and model search

Researched 2026-10-02. No patient data was used. Nothing here is built; this is reading only.

## 0. How to read this file

**Verification levels used in every table row.**
- **V1** means I opened a record or page (publisher, arXiv, Europe PMC, PMLR, JMLR, IJCAI) and the title, authors and year matched. The summary text on those pages came back through a small summarising tool, so numbers I quote from abstracts are as that tool reported them.
- **V2** means I read the page images of the full paper myself. Only three papers reach V2: Gao 2022 (pages 1-2), Gao 2023 (pages 3-9), Sui 2018 (pages 7-9).
- **V3** means the identifier came from search results only and I could not open the page. No row below is V3.
- "DOI resolves" means the DOI link redirected to the publisher, but the publisher page itself blocked me (IEEE, PubMed, PMC returned 403, a cookie page, or a CAPTCHA). The title and authors in those rows were confirmed through Europe PMC or Crossref instead.

**Terms, defined once.**
- **Reinforcement learning (RL):** a program learns which action to take in each situation by trying actions and seeing a reward.
- **State, action, reward:** state is what the program knows now; action is what it chooses (here, a stimulation setting); reward is the score that says how good the result was.
- **Offline RL:** learning only from data already collected, with no new trials.
- **Bandit:** the simple case of RL where each choice is judged on its own and does not change the next situation. A **contextual bandit** also sees some background facts (the context) before choosing.
- **Bayesian optimisation (BO):** picking the next setting to try by fitting a curve with uncertainty to past results. A **Gaussian process (GP)** is the usual curve. This is what the existing module does.
- **Off-policy evaluation (OPE):** estimating how well a new rule would do using data collected under an old rule, without running it. **Fitted Q evaluation (FQE)** is one way to do it.
- **Pessimism:** assuming the worst plausible value for any setting the data barely cover.
- **Constrained decision problem:** maximise one score while keeping other scores inside limits.

## 1. Corrections to the request, and what was not found

- **"Agarwal et al., Duke / Pajic group, offline learning of closed-loop DBS controllers"** is actually **Gao, Schmidt, Chowdhury, Feng, Peters, Genty, Grill, Turner, Pajic, ICCPS 2023**. I found no Agarwal paper. Verified (V1 and V2).
- **"Gao ~2020 model-based deep RL for adaptive DBS"** is **Gao, Naumann, Jovanov, Lesi, Kamaravelu, Grill, Pajic, ICCPS 2020**, titled "Model-Based Design of Closed Loop Deep Brain Stimulation Controller using Reinforcement Learning". Verified (V1, through Crossref).
- **"Lu et al."** is **Lu, Wei, Che, Wang, Loparo, IEEE TNSRE 2020**. Verified (V1).
- **"Castano-Candamil RL for essential tremor"** does not exist as stated. Two real papers exist. The 2019 one builds a simulated test environment for RL in Parkinson's closed-loop DBS. The 2020 essential tremor paper is a pilot of adaptive DBS using a regression model, not RL. Both verified (V1).
- **"Watts et al."**: I could not identify this paper after three searches. Unverified, not listed.
- **"Rouhani et al."** (spinal cord stimulation): not found. Unverified, not listed.
- **"Zhao et al. SCS RL"** is really **Zhao et al. 2021, Bayesian preference learning**, not RL. Verified (V1).
- **"Louie 2021/2023"**: the 2021 paper is verified. I found no 2023 Louie paper. The SafeOpt DBS work is by Cooper and Netoff (2022 preprint) and by Cole, Connolly and colleagues (2024).
- **"Connolly Bayesian optimisation for DBS"** is the 2024 SAFE-OPT paper (Cole, Connolly et al.). Verified (V1).
- **Sacral nerve stimulation, TMS, RL:** I found no RL or bandit study for sacral stimulation. TMS has one Bayesian optimisation pipeline (BOONStim), which optimises coil targeting from a brain model, not from patient ratings.
- **No published RL or bandit paper for choosing chronic pain DBS settings was found.** Shirvalkar et al. (2025 preprint) is closed-loop pain DBS driven by machine-learned pain biomarkers, not RL. This search is not exhaustive.

## 5. What this means for a single-patient pain DBS dataset

Each lesson is tied to the rows above. Items marked "my inference" are not stated in any paper.

**1. Treat the first version as a contextual bandit, not full RL.**
Each clinic step is one setting held for minutes and rated at the end. The rating of one step is unlikely to depend much on the step before it once a washout gap passes (my inference). A bandit needs far less data than RL. The only DBS paper that compared both said the bandit was more sample-efficient (A10, simulation). A pain-clinic recommender used a contextual bandit on 21 implanted patients (B22, preprint). The one deep RL result in real people used four patients, 84 to 182 minutes per controller each, and a fast 2-second control loop (A9). Our decisions are one per step, so we are nowhere near that regime. The existing GP module is already a bandit method (B1, B2, B10). The new module adds value only if it uses extra context (visit day, home pain trend, side effects) or carry-over between steps. Caution (my inference): time is modelled nowhere in this project (rule 13), so any state that encodes carry-over needs a decision from the PI first.

**2. Keep the six scales apart; do not collapse them into one reward on day one.**
Gao 2023 trained on a beta-power reward. It used the patient rating, grasp rate and tremor only to score finished policies, with equal fixed weights (10 or 25) in that score (A9). A weighted sum hides trade-offs, and the multi-objective guide says to check whether one weighted sum is enough (C21). Louie 2021 is a direct warning. The rigidity-only optimum was the highest tolerable frequency, but the participant's preference model pointed to 70 to 110 Hz (B2). Suggested design (my inference): pain intensity as the objective; unpleasantness as a second objective reported alongside; mood, anxiety, energy and side effects as limits that must hold (C20, B9). If the scale weights are contested, learn from pairwise comparisons instead (B12, B10, C22).

**3. Explore only inside a clinician-approved set, and plan for weak, noisy effects.**
In the one-patient spinal cord study, 564 trials over 10 weeks were needed before the safe region was mapped and the algorithm beat the physician's best choice (B10). We have a few hundred steps in total, across rate, current, pulse width and contacts. Standard GP optimisation fails to find the best setting reliably when the gap between settings is under 0.3 of the noise, which is plausible for pain ratings (B6). Mitigations from the papers: a boundary-avoiding kernel and input warping (B6); batch selection, because a visit tests several settings before any home data return (B11); a clinician-supplied prior, which helped in animal work (B15); a forgetting kernel because the best setting may drift over months (B8). For offline data, Gao 2023 seeded the buffer with a random controller whose amplitude had a lower limit, chosen for patient safety and acceptance (A9).

**4. Safety lives in the action set and in separate safety curves.**
SafeOpt and StageOpt fit a second curve for safety (here, side effects and unpleasantness) and only sample where it is likely above the safety level (B9, B10). In the one-patient study no unsafe pattern was sampled (B10). SAFE-OPT learns the limit per subject (B4). A formal route: the user states what must never happen, and the method must show it with confidence or return no answer (C19). Because nothing writes to the device and a clinician programs by hand, there is already a human check. The module should still never propose a setting outside the device ranges or outside the tried region without saying so.

**5. Offline evaluation is the weakest link; plan the evaluation before the algorithm.**
The health-RL guidelines list what to check: whether the problem suits RL, whether the evaluation is honest, whether the policy is safe (C17, C16). Importance-sampling estimates have high variance with long histories (A8). A learned-model estimate ranked policies better than two other estimators in four real patients (A9). In sepsis data, fitted Q evaluation ranked policies best but costs the most (C14). For bandit data, the doubly robust estimator is the standard (C15). Offline RL is very sensitive to its settings, and there is no clean way to pick them without trials (C13). Do not cut irregular steps into equal time bins, because that made retrospective scores 1.5 to 3 times too good in a simulator (C25). Our steps differ in length, so keep their own durations. The only real test is prospective: Gao 2023 tested two to four candidate policies on each trial day of a monthly visit (A9). Plan the same. The module outputs a ranked short list plus uncertainty, and the next visit tests it. Compare every candidate with plain imitation of the clinician, since a small imitation term alone was competitive on benchmarks (C4).

**6. With a few hundred steps, pick simple, pessimistic methods.**
CQL, IQL, TD3+BC, BCQ, AWAC and Decision Transformer were shown on benchmark sets, usually hundreds of thousands of steps (C2 to C7). I did not find a test at 300 steps. The idea they share is staying near the data (C1, C5, C10). On a few hundred points, the equivalent is a GP or Bayesian linear model of each rating, with the lower edge of its uncertainty used to rank settings (C10, C9). That is the existing module with a pessimistic rule instead of an exploring one (my inference). If a policy method is wanted, use a discrete grid of settings and the discrete versions of CQL or BCQ in d3rlpy (C28). Decision Transformer (C7) and Trajectory Transformer (C8) need long return-labelled trajectories; skip them (my inference).

**7. Contacts are a menu, not a number.**
Contacts are a small set of choices (Left 0 to 3, Right 8 to 11 per the project notes). Prior work treats site as its own choice: electrode contacts in the psychiatric DBS simulation (B5), electrode site in human nerve implants (B16), a hierarchy over sites then timing (B14). Never pool across electrodes (rule 13). Give each contact its own model, or one shared model with a contact label.

**8. The home record is confounded; use it to confirm, not to teach.**
Settings at home were not randomised: the clinician or patient chose when to change them. That is the confounding the observational health papers warn about (C16, C17). Daily scores with rare setting changes means the real sample size is the number of setting changes, not the number of days (my inference). This matches the project rule that clinic ratings choose and home ratings confirm. The patient's remote also switches groups, so the setting in force must come from the device record. A time-varying kernel is the only drift tool found (B8).

**9. No precedent uses real pain-DBS data for RL, so claim little.**
Of the 17 rows in Table A, 12 are simulations or simulated environments (A15 and A16 not fully read), 2 use animal brain tissue (A1, A2), and 3 involve real patients (A4, A9, A17). Only A9 uses offline RL on real patients (4 people, Parkinson's, an outcome other than pain); A4 and A17 are not RL. The only randomised-trial evidence for RL in pain care is behavioural therapy with 278 people and three choices (B23). Keep wording to "recommends settings to test", never "learned the best setting". Never say a setting does not respond from a narrow tested range (house rule). Gao 2023's "no difference" came from non-significant tests and is not proof of equivalence (A9).

**10. Log who chose each setting, so the next visit's data are usable.**
Every step should record the setting, start and end time, which scales were rated and when, and who chose the setting (clinician plan, algorithm suggestion, patient). Without the chooser, a later offline analysis cannot tell exploration from habit (C16, C13). This also serves the project rule that every write-back carries its provenance.

## 6. Hugging Face findings (checked 2026-10-02)

I used the search pages and the Hugging Face model list service (API). The search page showed some download counts that looked wrong (for example 39M for an old TabPFN mix and 88.3k for a sepsis model); the API gave 958 and 7. I use the API numbers. These are recent download counts, not lifetime.

**Decision Transformer checkpoints.**
- The main set is `edbeeching/decision-transformer-gym-{halfcheetah,hopper,walker2d}-{expert,medium,medium-replay}`. Top by downloads is `decision-transformer-gym-hopper-medium` (6,592), then `hopper-expert` (318). All are for the `transformers` library class `DecisionTransformerModel`.
- I read the Hopper config: state size 11, action size 3, three layers, one head, width 128, a tanh on the action. Trained on the D4RL "medium" Hopper data, and the card says the stored normalisation numbers must be used with it.
- **Not usable here.** These weights encode how a simulated robot leg moves. They share nothing with stimulation settings, and a state size of 11 and action size of 3 do not match. The model also needs trajectories labelled with return-to-go, and a few hundred steps is too little to train one from scratch (my inference).
- **Useful as a template only:** the `DecisionTransformerModel` class and its input format (states, actions, rewards, return-to-go, timesteps).
- A Trajectory Transformer set exists under `CarlCochet/trajectory-transformer-*` (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d; D4RL). Same verdict: robot tasks, no transfer.

**d3rlpy and CORL.**
- Searching "d3rlpy" returned one repository, `Vyachesss/d3rlpy-pickle-rce-poc`, a gated security demonstration. Its card says `d3rlpy.load_learnable("model.d3")` runs `pickle.load` on the file, so a crafted file can run code. **Do not load any d3rlpy model file from an unknown source.** Training our own and loading our own files is fine. I could not open its files.
- d3rlpy itself is a library, not a model store. It supports continuous and discrete action versions of CQL, IQL, TD3+BC, BCQ, AWAC and Decision Transformer, plus fitted Q evaluation (C28). It is the right code to try if a policy method is wanted; no checkpoint is needed.
- A search for "CORL" returned unrelated models. CORL is code on GitHub, not a Hub model set (C29).
- Searches for "conservative q-learning", "td3+bc" and "tabpfn-ts" returned no results.

**Medical or neuromodulation RL models on the Hub.**
- Searches for "deep brain stimulation", "neuromodulation" and "medical reinforcement learning" returned no results. I did not try the full-text search option. No stimulation model exists on the Hub as far as I can tell.
- The only clinical offline RL models found were two sepsis policies. `morpeN1/mimic-sepsis-cql`: CQL, 62 state features, 25 discrete actions, MIT licence, decision step 4 hours, 2,585 evaluation episodes, FQE mean 15.69 (95% interval 15.62 to 15.76), weighted importance sampling mean 10.02 (4.12 to 12.66). `ryan12345441/mimic-sepsis-iql`: IQL, same inputs and actions, research-only licence, FQE 2.85, importance sampling 8.20, agreement with clinicians 0.41. Both cards say "not for clinical use" and report no prospective test.
- **Not usable** (different patients, 25 discrete actions, intensive care). They are worth copying as a reporting pattern: state the evaluation method and an interval. My observation: the FQE interval (15.62 to 15.76) is far narrower than the importance-sampling interval (4.12 to 12.66) from the same data. That suggests the narrow one reflects resampling of episodes, not the error in the learned value model (my inference; see C16).

**TabPFN (a small-data prediction model that could replace the GP as the reward model).**
- `Prior-Labs/TabPFN-v2-reg` (tabular regression, 51,361 recent downloads), `TabPFN-v2-clf`, and later `tabpfn_2_5`, `tabpfn_2_6`, `tabpfn_3`, `tabpfn_3_5`. Older `autogluon/tabpfn-mix-1.0-*`.
- The Nature paper reports an advantage on up to 10,000 rows (C30). Our few hundred rows are inside that range. Whether it handles repeated measures from one patient better than a GP is untested; it assumes rows are exchangeable.
- Licence: v2 is "Prior Labs License" (Apache 2.0 plus an attribution requirement). Later weights (2.5, 2.6, 3, 3.5) carry non-commercial licences. The TabPFN-3 card says it prohibits "any commercial or production purpose". The GitHub README says the first use opens a browser to log in and accept the licence, with a script to download weights ahead for offline machines. Ask UCSF whether a research recommendation tool counts as production.
- Uncertainty output: the pages I read did not say. I believe the library can return quantiles, but I did not verify this; check before relying on it.
- The card lists 16 GB or more of memory for v2 regression.
- **Verdict:** a possible reward model to compare against the GP by leaving out one visit at a time. It is not an RL method, gives no safety guarantee, and needs the licence step. Try it only after the GP baseline and the evaluation plan (lesson 5) exist.

**Summary of what is usable for a tiny continuous-action data set.**
- Usable: d3rlpy code (training on our own data), `DecisionTransformerModel` as a template, TabPFN-v2 as a candidate reward model, the sepsis model cards as reporting templates.
- Not usable: every pretrained checkpoint found (all are for robot simulators or intensive care), and any file of unknown origin that loads through pickle.

## 7. Confidence and gaps

- **High:** the identity, authors, year, DOI or PMID of every row marked V1 or V2. Each was checked against a publisher, preprint server, PMLR, JMLR or Europe PMC record. Two PMIDs I would have guessed from memory were wrong (Gottesman, Thomas); the values in the tables come from the records.
- **High:** the Gao 2023 numbers (4 patients, 84 to 182 min per controller, 20 to 55% energy saving, reward form) and the Sui 2018 numbers (564 trials, 10 weeks, safe region at 300 to 400), because I read those pages.
- **Medium:** all abstract-level numbers in other rows, since they passed through a summarising tool. The Louie 2021 and Zhao 2021 abstracts were read as full abstract text from Europe PMC.
- **Not checked:** state, action and reward definitions for A5, A6, A7, A11, A13, A14, A15, A16, B4, B15, B17; data sizes for A1, A2, B4, B15, B17, B19, B21; whether the Tchrakian 2023 result (B22) is prospective or a replay; whether TabPFN returns quantiles; the published version of BOONStim; the venue of CQL, IQL, AWAC and Trajectory Transformer papers (I list arXiv only).
- **Limits of the search:** the Hub search pages are live and change daily. Absence of a model on 2026-10-02 is not proof it does not exist. The literature search is not systematic; no review protocol was used.
- **Project interaction (my analysis, not from a paper):** rule 13 (time modelled nowhere; never pool electrodes), rule 5 (no pain rating in the key of recording-derived products), and the clinic and home separation all constrain the design. They are noted in lessons 1, 7 and 8 and need the PI's decision before any state-based design.
