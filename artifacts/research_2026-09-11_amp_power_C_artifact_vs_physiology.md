# Amplitude-Dependent LFP Band-Power Changes on Sensing DBS Implants: Artifact vs. Physiology

> Research report
> Researcher: worker-research | Date: 2026-09-11
> Confidence: MIXED — core aliasing/saturation mechanisms are well documented (multiple corroborating peer-reviewed sources); Percept-specific numeric thresholds and the ERNA-as-8–30Hz-confound link are thinner and rely more on inference from device specs.

**Scope note:** This report covers ONLY the artifact/saturation/ERNA side of amplitude-vs-power relationships and the controls used to separate artifact from physiology. It deliberately does NOT catalogue the physiological dose-response literature itself or curve-fitting methodology — those are covered by other workers.

## Definitions (plain English, first use)

- **LFP (local field potential):** the slow (roughly 1–100 Hz) electrical signal recorded from the DBS electrode, thought to reflect summed synaptic/population activity near the contact.
- **BrainSense:** Medtronic's brand name for the Percept device's ability to record LFPs from the same electrode that is delivering stimulation.
- **Aliasing:** when a digitizer samples a signal too slowly to capture its true frequency, the signal's energy appears ("folds") at a false, lower frequency. Governed by the **Nyquist frequency** = half the sampling rate.
- **Nyquist frequency:** for Percept's 250 Hz sampling rate, this is 125 Hz. Any real signal component above 125 Hz that is not filtered out before digitization will alias into the 0–125 Hz range.
- **Amplifier saturation / "railing":** when an input signal (e.g., a stimulation pulse artifact) exceeds the voltage range the amplifier or analog-to-digital converter (ADC) can represent, the output "hits the rail" (clips at the maximum/minimum value) and the true signal shape is lost for that interval.
- **ERNA (evoked resonant neural activity):** a stereotyped, damped, high-frequency (roughly 200–500 Hz) oscillation triggered by each individual stimulation pulse, distinct from ongoing ("spontaneous") LFP oscillations like beta.
- **ECAP (evoked compound action potential):** a fast (millisecond-scale) electrically evoked neural response used mainly in spinal cord stimulation (SCS) closed-loop systems; DBS-specific ECAP literature at 8–30 Hz is thin (see Gaps).
- **Charge density:** stimulation charge delivered per pulse phase divided by electrode contact surface area (μC/cm²/phase); a standard electrochemical-safety and tissue-damage metric, also relevant to how much artifact couples into the recording front end.

## Key Findings

1. **Below the Nyquist frequency (125 Hz), stimulation artifact appears at the programmed stimulation rate and its ascending harmonics; above 125 Hz, it aliases (folds) to `artifact frequency = |250 − stim frequency|` Hz.** This is the formula reported for the Percept device by Thenaisie et al. (2021, *J. Neural Eng.* 18:042002). For a common clinical rate such as 130 Hz, the alias lands at 250 − 130 = 120 Hz; for other rates the alias moves accordingly and can, depending on the exact stimulation frequency chosen, land inside or near the 8–30 Hz sensing band. CONFIDENT (single primary source, but formula is standard Nyquist-aliasing math and is corroborated in principle by Neumann-lab technical descriptions of the 250 Hz sampling / 125 Hz Nyquist limit found in the same literature cluster).

2. **Subharmonic artifact peaks (fractions of the stimulation rate — 1/2, 1/4, 3/4) can appear directly inside or adjacent to the beta band, and their appearance is amplitude-gated, not just frequency-gated.** Thenaisie et al. (2021) report a patient in whom stimulation induced narrow power peaks at half (64.9 Hz), one-quarter (32.5 Hz), and three-quarters (97.4 Hz) of the programmed stimulation frequency (~129.8 Hz), and that these subharmonics only appeared once amplitude exceeded roughly 2.5 mA. The quarter-harmonic (32.5 Hz) sits essentially at the upper edge of the clinically used 8–30 Hz sensing band. MEDIUM (one primary source, single-patient observation, but mechanism is device-general and plausible).

3. **Amplifier "railing"/saturation and its spectral consequences scale with stimulation amplitude and have been directly quantified on Medtronic's sensing-DBS hardware family.** On the Summit RC+S (a research-grade sibling platform to Percept, same manufacturer), Alarie et al. (2022, *Front. Hum. Neurosci.* 10.3389/fnhum.2022.1016379) documented "slew overflow" — the artifact's rate of voltage change exceeding what the ADC can measure — at amplitudes of 4.5–5.3 mA, producing a roughly six-fold expansion of the stimulation artifact's spectral bandwidth (from 147–153 Hz to 140–180 Hz at a 150.6 Hz stimulation rate). Default sense-blanking (0.755 ms, i.e., the amplifier's input is briefly shorted around each pulse to avoid saturating) still produced 60–80% data-packet overflow at high amplitude; increasing blanking to 2.0–2.5 ms reduced overflow by roughly 30–40% but caused up to ~37.5% data loss from the blanking itself. HIGH for RC+S (directly measured); MEDIUM by extension to Percept, because Percept's front-end circuitry differs (see Percept-specific notes) and — critically — **the Percept PC does not expose configurable sense-blank or telemetry parameters to clinicians/researchers**, so the RC+S mitigation knobs are not available on Percept (Alarie et al. 2022).

4. **Evoked resonant neural activity (ERNA) is a pulse-locked, amplitude-dependent high-frequency (≈200–500 Hz) response that grows with stimulation amplitude and correlates with — but is mechanistically distinct from — ongoing beta-band LFP power.** ERNA amplitude increases with rising stimulation intensity, and the amplitude difference between the first and later pulses in a train grows with current (Sinclair et al., *Brain* 2018, PMC7059818; Wiest et al. 2023, PMID 36706929, "Evoked resonant neural activity in subthalamic local field potentials reflects basal ganglia network dynamics"). Wiest et al. report that ERNA frequency and amplitude are significantly correlated with the relative beta-band power measured before/after DBS. MEDIUM-HIGH for the amplitude-growth relationship (multiple corroborating sources); LOW-MEDIUM for the specific claim that ERNA directly contaminates the Percept 8–30 Hz band-power readout, because ERNA's core energy (200–500 Hz) is above the 125 Hz Nyquist limit and above the ~100 Hz low-pass antialiasing filter reported for Percept's BrainSense front end (Neumann-lab technical description; device-profile literature) — meaning standard clinical BrainSense streams likely cannot capture true ERNA at all, and most ERNA studies use separate, higher-bandwidth research recording hardware (Summit RC+S, intraoperative systems), not Percept BrainSense. This is an important nuance for interpreting "does ERNA explain a Percept amplitude-power curve": the mechanism is real and important for other sensing-enabled platforms, but its direct applicability to Percept's clinically reported 8–30 Hz band power is unverified in the sources found and should be treated as an open question rather than an established confound (see Gaps).

5. **The device manufacturer's own methods literature (Hammer, Kochanski, Starr & Little, 2022, *Stereotact. Funct. Neurosurg.* 100(3):168–183, doi:10.1159/000521431) identifies three artifact sources in Percept LFP recordings — ECG (heartbeat), non-physiologic polyphasic artifact, and ramping-related artifact from amplitude changes — and shows that after template-based artifact removal, stimulation-ON-at-0-mA spectra matched stimulation-OFF spectra to within ≤3.5% normalized power difference in theta/alpha/beta bands.** This ≤3.5% figure is one of the only quantitative "how much residual artifact contaminates band power" numbers found in the literature and is a useful benchmark for how tight an artifact-vs-physiology validation should be. HIGH (peer-reviewed, quantitative, directly on Percept).

6. **A rise-then-fall ("inverted-U") power-vs-amplitude curve can be produced mechanically, without any change in underlying neural physiology, by the combination of (a) growing artifact/aliased-harmonic power at low-to-moderate amplitude and (b) amplifier saturation or blanking-related data loss that caps or even reduces the *measured* power once amplitude exceeds a device-specific ceiling.** No single source in this search modeled this exact composite curve end-to-end for Percept, but the mechanistic pieces are independently documented: artifact/subharmonic power increasing with amplitude up to a threshold (Thenaisie et al. 2021, point 2 above), and saturation/blanking effects that clip, distort, or discard data once amplitude exceeds a device-specific ceiling (Alarie et al. 2022, point 3 above; and the general ADC-saturation principle documented in Chen et al. 2021, *Front. Neurosci.* 10.3389/fnins.2021.637274, who show DBS artifact amplitude scaling with stimulation intensity while the ADC remains unsaturated only below a device-specific ceiling). Separately, papers studying real physiological beta suppression (e.g., dose-response pilot studies) report an apparent inverted-U in beta power with amplitude in some patients (power decreasing from ~1.0 mA, minimum near 2.0 mA, rebounding by 3.5 mA) and explicitly flag this could reflect either true physiological rebound near the side-effect threshold or a measurement artifact — underscoring why the controls in the checklist below are necessary before accepting an inverted-U as physiological. MEDIUM (mechanistically well-supported by independent pieces; the composite claim itself is an inference, not a single directly-tested finding — flagged LOW/inferential where stated as fact).

7. **Charge density and electrode impedance interact with artifact size, though the peer-reviewed quantitative literature on this specific point is thin; the clearest numeric figures found come from a Medtronic patent document rather than a peer-reviewed paper.** A Medtronic patent ("Systems and methods for monitoring neural activity," US 11,298,070 family) states a charge-density safety limit of 30 μC/cm²/phase for the platinum-iridium Model 3389 electrode (contact surface ≈0.06 cm²), and separately notes that the longer-duration, low-amplitude phase of an asymmetric (biphasic) stimulus pulse couples more charge into the recording front end than a symmetric pulse, pushing the amplifier into a non-linear (near-saturation) operating regime and increasing artifact/distorting evoked-response visibility; using a symmetric stimulus was proposed to reduce this coupling. LOW-MEDIUM confidence: this is patent-document language (not independently peer-reviewed), and inventor/authorship could not be confirmed in this session — treat as an industry engineering claim requiring independent verification, not an established scientific finding. Separately, multiple peer-reviewed papers (Neumann-lab technical descriptions cited in Thenaisie et al. 2021 and related work) note that Percept's use of the implanted pulse generator (IPG) as both stimulation return path and recording ground in monopolar mode is why ECG and other body-noise artifacts couple in more strongly in monopolar than bipolar sensing configurations — an impedance/grounding interaction distinct from charge density per se. MEDIUM (multiple corroborating sources for the monopolar-grounding mechanism).

## Controls Checklist a Study Should Apply Before Calling a Rise-Then-Fall Curve "Physiological"

1. **Off-stimulation (0 mA) baseline comparison.** Record LFP with stimulation amplitude set to 0 mA (device still "on" so hardware state matches) and confirm the spectral shape matches true stimulation-OFF recordings within a small tolerance (Hammer et al. 2022 achieved ≤3.5% normalized power difference in theta/alpha/beta after artifact removal — a reasonable quantitative benchmark). If 0-mA and true-OFF spectra differ substantially, residual hardware/lead artifact (e.g., cardiac coupling even at 0 mA) is present and must be resolved before trusting amplitude-sweep data.
2. **Harmonic-free band selection.** Before reporting power in a given band, check whether the programmed stimulation frequency, its aliased frequency (`|250 − f_stim|` for Percept), or any subharmonic (1/2, 1/4, 3/4 of stim frequency) falls inside that band at any amplitude tested. If so, either choose a different, harmonic-free reporting band or explicitly exclude/flag the contaminated bins.
3. **Amplitude-threshold check for discontinuous artifact onset.** Because subharmonic artifacts can switch on abruptly above a threshold amplitude (e.g., ~2.5 mA in one reported case) rather than growing smoothly, inspect the amplitude-power curve for a sudden discontinuity or kink coincident with a specific mA value, which is a signature of artifact onset rather than gradual physiological modulation.
4. **Ramp-transition exclusion.** Exclude a buffer window (on the order of 1–2 seconds, following the exclusion windows used in published studies) immediately after any amplitude change before computing power, to avoid the "soft-start/banding" ramping artifact.
5. **Raw time-domain inspection for clipping/saturation.** Visually or algorithmically check raw (time-domain) recordings at the highest tested amplitudes for flat-topped, clipped, or distorted waveform segments and for packet dropout/overflow flags, which indicate amplifier or ADC saturation rather than genuine signal decline.
6. **Symmetric ramp-up/ramp-down comparison.** Compare the power trajectory as amplitude is increased against the trajectory as the same amplitude steps are reversed (ramped back down). True physiological effects with hysteresis are possible but should be reported and distinguished from asymmetric measurement artifact (e.g., different blanking or saturation behavior on rising vs. falling edges).
7. **Impedance/contact-quality check.** Confirm electrode impedance is within the expected clinical range (and stable across the amplitude sweep) before and after the sweep; abnormal or drifting impedance can change how much stimulation artifact couples into the recording and can independently produce amplitude-dependent power changes unrelated to brain physiology.
8. **Bipolar vs. monopolar cross-check where feasible.** Because monopolar sensing is more vulnerable to grounding-related and cardiac artifact (the IPG can serves as both return path and recording ground), replicating a key amplitude-power finding in a bipolar sensing configuration — where geometrically feasible — strengthens the case that an effect is physiological rather than an artifact of the monopolar return path.
9. **Cross-device or cross-bandwidth corroboration for high-frequency claims.** If a claim depends on evoked high-frequency activity (e.g., ERNA-like content) contributing to a lower-band power change, verify using a recording system with adequate bandwidth/sampling rate to actually capture that high-frequency content (Percept's clinical 250 Hz stream with ~100 Hz low-pass filtering cannot); do not infer ERNA contamination of an 8–30 Hz metric without such direct verification.
10. **Report residual-artifact quantification, not just visual absence of peaks.** Following Hammer et al. 2022's approach, quantify the percentage difference in band power before/after artifact-removal or between matched ON/OFF conditions, rather than relying solely on qualitative "the spectrum looks clean" assessment.

## Percept-Specific Notes

- **Sampling and filtering chain:** Percept's BrainSense streaming digitizes at a fixed 250 Hz (Nyquist 125 Hz); the front end reportedly includes low-pass filtering around 100 Hz and high-pass filtering at 1 Hz (or a user-selectable 1/10 Hz), which is intended to attenuate high-frequency content — including most stimulation-frequency energy and virtually all ERNA-range content — before it can alias down into the sensing band (Neumann-lab technical descriptions cited in/around Thenaisie et al. 2021; corroborated in general terms by multiple secondary device-profile summaries). CONFIDENT on the 250 Hz/125 Hz figures (directly and repeatedly stated across sources); MEDIUM on the exact filter corner frequencies (consistently reported across secondary sources but not independently confirmed against a primary Medtronic engineering document in this session).
- **No configurable sense-blanking or telemetry parameters.** Unlike the research-only Summit RC+S, the commercial Percept PC does not expose sense-blank duration or telemetry-ratio settings to the clinician/researcher, meaning the artifact-mitigation strategies validated on RC+S (increasing blank duration to reduce overflow) cannot be applied on Percept (Alarie et al. 2022). This is a meaningful limitation for any Percept-based artifact-vs-physiology analysis: researchers cannot experimentally vary blanking to test whether an amplitude-power relationship depends on blanking-related saturation.
- **Template-subtraction is the primary published offline remedy.** Hammer et al. 2022 validated an automated template-based subtraction method (also released as open code) that removes ECG, non-physiologic polyphasic, and ramping artifacts from Percept recordings, restoring 0 mA spectra to within ≤3.5% of true-OFF spectra in theta/alpha/beta bands.
- **The open-source "Perceive" toolbox** (Neumann, Tinkhauser, Sieger; maintained by Vanhoecke et al., https://github.com/neuromodulation/perceive) is the most widely used community tool for extracting and preprocessing Percept JSON export data, and includes automated ECG-artifact removal and alignment of stimulation-amplitude metadata with LFP streams — relevant as a methodological reference point but its artifact-handling choices should be checked against a study's specific amplitude-sweep design rather than assumed sufficient.
- **Known contact-selection and mode restrictions** documented by Thenaisie et al. (2021): BrainSense streaming restricts sensing/stimulation contact combinations to a limited predefined set (often forcing stimulation through the middle ring contacts regardless of clinically optimal contact), and the chronic "Timeline" logging mode only records power within a narrow (5 Hz) band around one predefined center frequency — meaning any amplitude-sweep study intending to check for artifact leaking into adjacent frequencies should use BrainSense Streaming (broadband spectra) rather than Timeline mode.
- **Documented artifact prevalence:** cardiac artifact was observed in roughly 20% of patients in one series (Thenaisie et al. 2021) and reported at higher rates (~65% of left sub-clavicular implants) in Neumann-lab-associated work referenced in secondary sources — the discrepancy likely reflects differences in implant laterality, detection sensitivity, and cohort, and should be treated as a range rather than a single fixed rate (MEDIUM confidence on both individual numbers; the range itself is a notable finding).

## Contradictions

- **Cardiac-artifact prevalence estimates differ substantially across sources** (~20% in Thenaisie et al. 2021 vs. ~65% in a Neumann-lab-associated left-sub-clavicular cohort referenced in secondary search results). This may reflect real differences in implant side/anatomy (left-sided implants are closer to the heart) rather than a true contradiction, but the underlying primary source for the 65% figure could not be independently confirmed in this session — flagged as a gap.
- **Whether ERNA meaningfully contaminates Percept's clinical 8–30 Hz band power is not clearly established either way.** The device's reported filtering (250 Hz sampling, ~100 Hz low-pass) argues against direct ERNA leakage into the sensing band, yet ERNA research is frequently discussed in the same literature cluster as Percept beta-power findings, creating a risk that readers (and possibly some published discussions) conflate the two mechanisms. This report treats direct ERNA contamination of Percept 8–30 Hz power as unverified rather than confirmed.
- **The "banding" soft-start ramping artifact** was found described in secondary search-engine synthesis without a single unambiguous primary-source quote pinpointing the exact term; it is most consistent with Hammer et al. 2022's "ramping related artifacts," but the specific word "banding" and its visual description in the power spectrum could not be directly re-verified against the primary PDF/HTML in this session due to access restrictions (see Gaps).

## Confidence Per Claim

- **Aliasing formula `f_alias = |250 − f_stim|` for Percept:** CONFIDENT — stated explicitly in Thenaisie et al. 2021, consistent with standard Nyquist theory, and the 250 Hz/125 Hz figures are repeated consistently across the literature cluster searched.
- **Subharmonic artifacts (1/2, 1/4, 3/4 of stim frequency) with an amplitude-threshold onset (~2.5 mA in one patient):** MEDIUM — single primary source, single-patient observation; generalizability to other patients/leads/stim frequencies not established.
- **Amplifier saturation/slew-overflow scaling with amplitude on Summit RC+S:** HIGH — directly, quantitatively measured (Alarie et al. 2022) with specific numbers (2–5.3 mA range, 6-fold bandwidth expansion, 60–80% vs. 30–40% overflow at different blanking settings).
- **Percept lacking configurable sense-blank/telemetry parameters:** HIGH — stated explicitly and specifically as a platform limitation in Alarie et al. 2022.
- **ERNA amplitude growth with stimulation current:** HIGH — corroborated across multiple independent sources (Sinclair et al. 2018; Wiest et al. 2023).
- **ERNA directly contaminating Percept's clinically reported 8–30 Hz band power:** LOW — plausible in principle but not directly demonstrated in the sources reviewed; device filtering specs argue against direct leakage; flagged as an open question, not a finding.
- **≤3.5% residual artifact benchmark after template subtraction (Hammer et al. 2022):** HIGH — quantitative, peer-reviewed, directly on Percept.
- **Charge-density limit (30 μC/cm²/phase) and asymmetric-pulse coupling mechanism:** LOW-MEDIUM — sourced from a patent document, not independently peer-reviewed; inventor/full bibliographic detail not confirmed in this session.
- **Composite "inverted-U produced entirely by artifact+saturation" mechanism:** MEDIUM as a mechanistic hypothesis (each component independently well-supported); LOW as a demonstrated end-to-end finding for any specific published amplitude-power curve, since no source in this search directly attributed a published inverted-U curve entirely to artifact.
- **Monopolar-mode IPG-as-ground artifact mechanism:** MEDIUM — described consistently across sources but with limited independent quantification of its amplitude-dependence specifically.
