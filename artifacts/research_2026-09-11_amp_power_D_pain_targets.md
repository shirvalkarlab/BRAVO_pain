# Stimulation Amplitude and LFP Band Power in Pain-Relevant DBS Targets (ACC, OFC, VPL/VPM, PAG/PVG) and Closely Related Neuromodulation

> Research report
> Researcher: worker-research | Date: 2026-09-11
> Confidence: MIXED — solid qualitative/directional evidence on which bands track pain in each target; almost no published parametric amplitude-sweep data (amplitude vs. LFP power, or amplitude vs. relief, as a quantified curve) exists for human pain-DBS targets. The clearest quantitative inverted-U evidence comes from spinal cord stimulation (SCS) dorsal-horn physiology, not DBS.

**Definitions for non-specialist readers:** *LFP (local field potential)* = a summed electrical signal recorded from a group of nearby neurons via an implanted electrode, decomposed into frequency "bands" (delta ~1–4Hz, theta ~4–9Hz, alpha ~8–12Hz, beta ~13–35Hz [often split low/high], gamma ~35–90Hz+). *Amplitude* = the strength of the electrical stimulation pulse (measured in volts [V] for older constant-voltage devices or milliamps [mA] for constant-current devices). *Inverted-U (non-monotonic) relationship* = an effect that increases then decreases as amplitude rises (a "peak" or "sweet spot"), as opposed to a monotonic relationship that keeps rising (or falling) with amplitude. *ECAP* = evoked compound action potential, an electrically recorded signal reflecting how many spinal-cord nerve fibers were activated by a stimulation pulse; used by Saluda's Evoke device to auto-adjust current in real time. *Dwell time* = how long a given amplitude setting is held before being changed, during titration/programming.

## Key Findings

1. **The flagship UCSF pain biomarker paper (Shirvalkar et al. 2023, Nature Neuroscience) is a recording-only, non-stimulation study and contains no amplitude-vs-power analysis.** I obtained and read the full text (via eScholarship open-access PDF). Four participants (3 post-stroke pain, 1 phantom limb pain) had bilateral ACC and OFC electrodes (Activa PC+S) and reported pain scores 2–8×/day over 78–184 days (mean 320 reports/participant). The paper's entire analysis is about decoding pain state from six power-spectral bands (δ,θ,α,β,γ-low,γ-high) using LDA/LASSO — it does **not** vary or report stimulation amplitude at all; the device was used for chronic DBS therapy in parallel but amplitude was not a study variable. Chronic pain state was decoded best from contralateral OFC (AUCs 0.67–0.85 across participants, LDA, p<0.05 in all 4), while acute thermal pain leaned more on ACC. (CONFIDENT, primary source read in full — Shirvalkar et al. 2023, Nat Neurosci, escholarship.org/uc/item/3ck6m3jf)

2. **No published pain-DBS paper (from the named UCSF/Provenza/Sacré/Bijanki/Gilron/Frey-Lempka group of investigators, or any other group found) reports a systematic parametric amplitude sweep with simultaneous LFP band-power measurement in ACC, OFC, VPL/VPM, or PAG/PVG.** Across ~25 targeted searches and ~15 full-text or near-full-text retrievals, the closest analogues are (a) a 1V-step voltage-ramp safety test in ACC that measured *after-discharge* (seizure-like) activity, not graded band power (see #5), and (b) chronic, non-parametric LFP recordings correlated with clinically-observed pain relief during ordinarily-titrated therapy (Oxford/Aziz group, see #4). (HIGH confidence that this gap exists, based on breadth of search; cannot rule out an inaccessible/paywalled paper I could not retrieve — see Gaps.)

3. **The clearest quantitative inverted-U amplitude-response curve in pain neuromodulation comes from spinal cord stimulation (SCS) dorsal-horn physiology, not brain DBS.** A combined computational-model + in-vivo rat study (dorsal horn wide-dynamic-range [WDR] neuron recordings, n=8 rats/215 units for dorsal horn, n=6 rats/24 units for dorsal column axons) found that inhibition of pain-signaling WDR neurons was **non-monotonic in stimulation amplitude**: maximal inhibition occurred at ~75–85% of the "activation/sensory threshold" (model) or ~40–60% of motor threshold (in vivo), with amplitudes above this range producing **direct excitation that reduced the net inhibitory (analgesic-relevant) effect**. Mechanism proposed: "surround inhibition" via GABAergic interneurons activated by sparse, asynchronous dorsal-column fiber recruitment at moderate amplitude; higher amplitude synchronizes/over-recruits fibers and drives direct excitation instead. (MEDIUM-HIGH confidence, full text read — PMC9536854, rat model, human translational link is inferential only.)

4. **In the sensory thalamus (VPL/VPM) and PAG/PVG, chronically-recorded LFP band power (not stimulation amplitude) correlates with pain relief in consistent directions across two related Oxford-group (Aziz/Green/Huang) datasets, but amplitude itself was not swept.** Huang et al. 2016 (10 patients, PVG and sensory thalamus LFPs recorded around ordinary chronic stimulation): **theta power in both PVG and thalamus correlated inversely with pain relief**; **high-beta power in thalamus and alpha power in PVG correlated positively with pain relief**. Huang et al. 2017 (13 patients, sensory thalamus only): occurrence rate of a **low-beta oscillatory state correlated significantly with pain relief**, and combinatorial states (low-beta paired with delta/theta/alpha/high-beta/low-gamma) correlated more strongly than any single band. These are power/state-vs-relief correlations during a mix of stimulation conditions, not a controlled amplitude sweep. (MEDIUM confidence — full findings obtained via search-engine synthesis of abstracts/full text summaries, not independently read as full PDF; directionality is consistent across two papers from the same group.)

5. **The one documented amplitude-titration protocol with simultaneous LFP monitoring in a pain-DBS cortical target (ACC) shows a sharp, threshold-like (not graded/inverted-U) relationship between amplitude and abnormal LFP activity.** Huang et al. 2019 (Brain Sciences; Activa PC+S, 3 ACC-implanted pain patients): unilateral test stimulation ramped 0→6V in 1V steps (130Hz, 450µs pulse width, several-second off-interval between steps) triggered **after-discharges (seizure-like LFP bursts with elevated power across multiple frequency bands)** at a threshold of **5V (left lead)** and **4V (right lead)**; bilateral stimulation at 6V produced significant after-discharges on the right and mild ones on the left. Applying an **8-second ramp** on stimulation onset/offset **prevented after-discharges even at the previously-unsafe 6V level while preserving pain relief** — i.e., the *rate of amplitude change*, not only the final amplitude, determines whether the ACC LFP shows this pathological non-monotonic-adjacent response. (HIGH confidence, full text read — PMC6680545, n=3.)

6. **PAG/PVG DBS programming literature describes a qualitative "therapeutic window" (amplitude-dependent side effects bounding benefit) but no quantified inverted-U curve for pain relief vs. amplitude has been published.** A 2023 Frontiers case series (PAG/PVG for neuropathic facial pain) reports a typical titration protocol: start at **1V, 50Hz, 60µs pulse width**, then **gradually increase up to 5V** guided by clinical response; **eye deviation and other adverse effects emerge at higher voltages**, and NRS pain reduction was 54% at 3 months, 48% at 1 year, 45% at 2 years (a decline attributable to habituation/disease course over time, not to an amplitude-response curve within a session). Classic PAG/PVG electrode-placement confirmation criterion is a 5–6mA sensation of warmth/relaxation. Other PVG literature lists nausea, diaphoresis, and anxiety as amplitude-dependent autonomic/affective side effects. No paper found gives a quantitative pain-relief-vs-amplitude plot. (MEDIUM confidence — full text read for titration numbers; the "too much stimulation worsens pain/mood" idea is supported only by qualitative side-effect reports, not a measured relief curve.)

7. **Beta-band power in the VPM thalamus tracks severe facial neuropathic pain state in ambulatory Percept PC recordings, in a single-patient sensing study — this is the most direct "Percept + pain" finding located.** Ismail/Ben-Haim group (OHSU), using Medtronic Percept PC BrainSense in a patient with VPM/PAG leads for facial neuropathic pain: beta peaks were present in **100% of pain-episode recordings with NRS>9, 56% with NRS>7, and 50% of "burning"-quality pain events**, suggesting beta power as a candidate closed-loop biomarker. A companion abstract (O106) reports periodic and aperiodic (1/f) power-spectral features from the same VPM/PAG Percept PC dataset. Neither publication reports an amplitude sweep. (MEDIUM confidence — abstract/press-summary level detail only; full-text of the primary Neuromodulation journal article was not retrievable, PubMed blocked automated access.)

8. **Saluda Evoke ECAP-controlled closed-loop SCS shows a monotonic, saturating dose-response for pain relief in a large real-world dataset — not an inverted-U — within the amplitude range studied.** A retrospective analysis of 690 patients (Jan 2017–Jun 2023) found pain reduction improved as "dose ratio" (delivered ECAP amplitude relative to a threshold target) rose from 0 to ~1.2, after which "% time spent above ECAP threshold" plateaued near 100%; dose ratio vs. usage-over-threshold showed Spearman r²=0.81, and higher dose/accuracy groups showed significantly better outcomes (Jonckheere-Terpstra trend test, p<0.01 for dose and usage, p<0.03 for accuracy). Median maximal analgesic effect was ~79–82% pain reduction in higher-dose groups. The authors explicitly state a theoretical ceiling (and by extension any downturn) was **not reached** in this population — i.e., this large clinical dataset provides no evidence of an inverted-U at the amplitudes clinically used, in contrast to the preclinical dorsal-horn finding in #3. (MEDIUM-HIGH confidence, full text read — PMC12015077.)

9. **A related closed-loop SCS trial (ECHO-MAC, n=42, RCT/crossover) shows ECAP-based amplitude control reduces variability in delivered dose rather than establishing a dose-response curve.** Closed-loop (ECAP-controlled) SCS reduced the standard deviation of delivered ECAP amplitude to 8.72µV vs. 19.95µV for open-loop; 97.6% of subjects had reduced "overstimulation" sensations, and 88.1% preferred closed-loop. This supports the "peak"/therapeutic-window concept operationally (keeping dose near a target avoids under- and over-stimulation) but does not itself plot relief against amplitude. (MEDIUM confidence — abstract-level detail; full text was 403-blocked.)

10. **The 2025 UCSF/Provenza personalized closed-loop DBS preprint (the most likely source for amplitude-vs-power data across multiple pain targets) could not be fully accessed during this research — this is a significant, explicitly-flagged gap.** Search-engine summaries indicate: double-blind, sham-controlled intracranial trial in 6 participants, 5 receiving permanent closed-loop DBS implants; novel basal ganglia targets identified (left caudate body, ipsilateral globus pallidus internus) in addition to cortico-striatal-thalamocortical targets; closed-loop DBS reported superior to sham, with durability up to 3.5 years. I was unable to retrieve the full PDF/HTML (medRxiv returned 403/401 errors on all access attempts, including a proxy reader) to extract amplitude ranges, titration step size/dwell time, or band-power-vs-amplitude data. **This paper should be re-attempted with authenticated/institutional access before any claims about its amplitude-power relationships are treated as established.** (LOW confidence on any granular claim from this source; the topline summary above is MEDIUM confidence, sourced from multiple independent search-engine synopses of the same abstract.)

11. **No paper could be located that is co-authored by both a "Frey" and "Lempka" on pain-DBS amplitude modeling, despite this being named in the assignment.** Lempka's identified direct pain-DBS contribution is the 2017 Annals of Neurology randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of DBS at the ventral striatum/anterior limb of the internal capsule (VS/ALIC) for post-stroke pain (n=10): the trial was **negative for its primary endpoint** (≥50% Pain Disability Index improvement) but showed significant secondary improvements in affective measures (depression, anxiety, quality of life). This paper does not report LFP amplitude-power analysis. No amplitude-response curve was found. (MEDIUM confidence on the Lempka 2017 trial description; HIGH confidence that no "Frey + Lempka amplitude model" paper was found after 3 targeted searches — flagged as an open gap, possibly a citation the requester has in mind that is not indexed under these exact terms.)

12. **"Sacré" in the pain-neuromodulation literature refers to Pierre Sacré (Johns Hopkins / now Université de Liège), whose relevant published work is dorsal-horn/peripheral-nerve-stimulation computational modeling, not ACC/thalamic pain-DBS LFP-amplitude work.** Sacré, Beauchene, Yang, Guan & Sarma modeled dorsal-horn wide-dynamic-range neuron responses to peripheral nerve stimulation in rats, but used a **single fixed amplitude (5mA biphasic, 0.5ms pulses)** — no amplitude sweep was performed in this paper. (MEDIUM confidence — full text read, PMC8788123; this appears to be the person the assignment intended, but his contribution is modeling infrastructure, not an amplitude-response finding.)

## Titration Protocols Reported

- **ACC (Huang et al. 2019, n=3):** Test stimulation ramped 0→target voltage (1–6V) in **1V steps**, each step held with a several-second off-interval before the next step, unilaterally per lead; fixed 130Hz / 450µs. For chronic therapeutic use, a mitigation protocol of **8-second ramp on/off** was adopted to avoid after-discharges at higher (therapeutic) amplitudes. One patient's cycling protocol (3 min ON / 11 min OFF) was set empirically from observed onset (3 min to relief) and offset (relief lost ~11 min after stopping) kinetics — the only explicit pain-relief-kinetics-derived dwell-time example found in this search. (HIGH confidence, primary source read.)
- **PAG/PVG (Frontiers 2023 case series):** Start at **1V, 50Hz, 60µs pulse width, monopolar** (case positive, two adjacent contacts negative); amplitude **gradually increased up to 5V** guided by symptom relief vs. side effects (specific step size/dwell time not reported in the retrieved text). Classic intraoperative confirmation criterion (multiple sources): 5–6mA producing warmth/relaxation sensation. Chronic PVG stimulation elsewhere described as low-frequency (10–25Hz), 1–5V. (MEDIUM confidence — step size/dwell not explicit.)
- **General DBS monopolar-review convention (Parkinson's literature, cited for context/comparison only, not pain-specific):** 0.5mA/V step evaluation of each contact to find benefit and side-effect thresholds, sometimes narrowed to 0.1mA steps near the threshold. This is the source of the general "therapeutic window" programming concept referenced in the pain literature but was **not** confirmed as the protocol actually used in any pain-DBS paper retrieved. (LOW-MEDIUM confidence for direct applicability to pain targets — flagged as an analogy, not a pain-specific finding, per scope constraints.)
- **UCSF closed-loop pain DBS "wash-in/wash-out" testing (Provenza/Shirvalkar 2021 Frontiers review, "Practical Closed-Loop Strategies for DBS: Lessons from Chronic Pain," open-access, full text read):** Amplitude is not manually stepped in fixed increments in this framework; instead, patients report the moment they feel pain **decrease** after stimulation onset ("wash-in") and the moment pain **returns to baseline** after stimulation offset ("wash-out"). This defines the therapeutic time-window used to set intermittent/duty-cycled stimulation, and closed-loop amplitude changes are gated by a linear-discriminant-analysis (LDA) biomarker threshold crossing, with a programmable **ramp rate** (seconds) controlling how fast amplitude moves to its new value once triggered. No numeric step size or dwell time is given for the underlying amplitude changes themselves. (HIGH confidence, full text read — escholarship.org/uc/item/8kp0k084.)
- **Saluda Evoke ECAP-controlled closed-loop SCS:** Not a manual step-and-dwell titration — a real-time feedback controller continuously adjusts current amplitude to minimize the error between a target ECAP amplitude and the measured ECAP amplitude, updating within each stimulation cycle. "Dose ratio" (measured/target ECAP amplitude relative to a clinician-set threshold) is the operative titration variable rather than raw current. (MEDIUM-HIGH confidence, full text read — PMC12015077.)

## Contradictions

1. **Inverted-U (preclinical, dorsal horn) vs. monotonic/saturating (large human ECAP registry).** The rat dorsal-horn/SCS modeling study (PMC9536854) found a clear inverted-U in neuron-level inhibition, peaking well below motor threshold, with higher amplitude reducing net inhibition. The large human Saluda Evoke dataset (PMC12015077, n=690) found relief rising monotonically with dose ratio up to 1.2 and then plateauing, with **no observed downturn** even at dose ratio 1.6. Possible reconciliations: (a) different outcome measures — single-neuron inhibition vs. patient-reported relief, which integrates many circuit-level and perceptual factors; (b) the human dataset's amplitude range may not have extended far enough past the "optimal" zone to reveal a downturn, since patients would likely reduce or stop stimulation before tolerating grossly supra-threshold, paresthesia-intolerable amplitudes; (c) genuine species/circuit differences. This is a substantive open contradiction relevant to the assignment's central question and should be flagged as unresolved rather than papered over.
2. **Qualitative "too much stimulation is bad" (PAG/PVG side-effect literature) vs. absence of any quantitative relief-vs-amplitude curve.** Clinical programming literature for PAG/PVG repeatedly describes amplitude-dependent side effects (anxiety, autonomic symptoms, eye deviation) that create a practical therapeutic window, which is consistent with the *idea* of an inverted-U for net "tolerated benefit." However, no paper retrieved actually plots pain relief (or LFP power) against amplitude to demonstrate a downturn in relief itself at high amplitude — the "inverted-U" here is inferred from separate benefit-threshold and side-effect-threshold observations (standard monopolar-review logic), not measured as a single continuous curve. This is a conceptual gap, not a data contradiction, but it means claims of a demonstrated inverted-U in *pain relief* for PAG/PVG DBS should be treated as unverified.
3. **An older secondary-source claim (surfaced only via search-engine synthesis, not independently verified in primary text) states that PVG-region field potentials show "5–25Hz stimulation produces maximum pain relief while 50–100Hz makes pain worse."** This is a **frequency**, not amplitude, effect, and it comes from historical Kiriakopoulos/Tasker-era Pain-journal papers ("Thalamic field potentials during deep brain stimulation of periventricular gray in chronic pain") that were paywalled (Ovid) and could not be independently confirmed in full text during this research. I flag this explicitly as **unverified and out of the amplitude-specific scope of this assignment** — it should not be cited as an amplitude finding, and its accuracy as a frequency finding is also unconfirmed by primary-source reading here.

## Gaps (stated plainly)

- **The central question of the assignment — does DBS amplitude produce an inverted-U or multi-peak change in LFP band power within ACC, OFC, VPL/VPM, or PAG/PVG — is essentially unanswered in the published, accessible literature.** No paper found reports a controlled amplitude sweep (fixed frequency/pulse width, multiple amplitude steps, LFP power measured at each step) in any of these five named targets in pain patients. The literature instead offers: (a) recording-only pain-state biomarker studies with no amplitude manipulation (Shirvalkar 2023), (b) threshold/step-function safety data for one adverse phenomenon (ACC after-discharges, Huang 2019), and (c) band-power-vs-clinical-relief correlations during ordinarily-programmed (not systematically swept) stimulation (Oxford PVG/thalamus studies). This is a genuine, not merely search-limited, evidence gap as far as could be determined.
- **The 2025 Provenza/UCSF closed-loop DBS preprint — the single most likely candidate to contain this exact analysis across multiple novel targets — could not be accessed.** All attempts (direct HTML, direct PDF, proxy reader) returned 401/403 errors. This should be re-attempted with UCSF-authenticated access or by contacting the authors directly; it is very likely to contain amplitude/programming details relevant to this exact research question given the group and topic.
- **No pain-relief-vs-amplitude curve (inverted-U or otherwise) has been quantitatively published for any of the five named brain targets** (ACC, OFC, VPL/VPM, PAG/PVG). The "therapeutic window" concept is used qualitatively (benefit threshold vs. side-effect threshold) but not as a measured continuous curve in any pain-DBS paper retrieved.
- **"Percept" + "pain" and "BrainSense" + "pain" literature is thin:** the main identifiable line of work is a small (down to n=1) VPM/PAG facial-pain sensing case series from OHSU (Ismail/Ben-Haim group); no amplitude-titration data were found associated with Percept/BrainSense specifically for pain.
- **Could not confirm the existence of a "Frey + Lempka" pain-DBS amplitude paper.** Lempka's own pain-DBS RCT (2017, VS/ALIC for poststroke pain) does not contain LFP-amplitude analysis. This should be clarified with the requester — possibly a different/more recent citation was intended that is not yet indexed under these terms, or the two authors' relevant collaboration is in a different (non-pain) DBS-modeling context (e.g., Parkinson's, excluded from this scope).
- **Pierre Sacré's identifiable published contribution (dorsal horn/peripheral nerve modeling with Sarma/Anderson/Guan/Beauchene) does not include an amplitude sweep** — it uses a single fixed amplitude (5mA). If the requester has a different, more recent Sacré pain-DBS paper in mind (e.g., something from the UCSF group specifically), it was not located in this search.
- **The historical PVG/thalamic field-potential frequency-dependence claim ("5–25Hz good, 50–100Hz worsens pain")** could not be verified against primary text (paywalled Ovid/Pain-journal sources) and is explicitly flagged as unverified; it is also a frequency, not amplitude, finding and therefore only tangential to this assignment's scope.
