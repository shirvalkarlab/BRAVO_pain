# Issue 4: Using the early 0 mA period as an independent, current-free test of the pain-band relationship

> Research report — literature review and critical evaluation
> Researcher: worker-research | Date: 2026-09-24
> Confidence: MIXED — the idea is sound in principle and has real precedent, but every precedent that used a stimulation-off period to build or test a biomarker also had to fight the same set of surgery-related confounds that would apply to RCS08's 0 mA window, and none of them show that a biomarker's relationship with the symptom survives unchanged once stimulation is on.

See decision 262.

## Bottom line

- **The proposal is a real, previously-used study design** (a "stimulation-naive" or "off-stim" window to relate a brain signal to a symptom without a current confound), but every published example of it comes from a period that is *also* the first weeks after surgery — so it trades one confound (current) for a basket of others: the microlesion/insertional effect on both symptoms and on the signal itself, electrode-tissue impedance drift, and the fact that mood, expectation, and app-use habits are all changing then too. The PI's own earlier paper found that 43% of chronic-pain DBS patients get a large pain drop from lead placement alone, before any stimulation (Shirvalkar et al. 2020, *J Clin Med*).
- **The right test is within-period, not between-period.** Comparing the 0 mA block's mean pain (8.1) against the later mean (~6.1) mixes the current effect with every one of those surgical/temporal confounds and answers nothing about the band. Asking "inside the 19 zero-mA reports, does the band track the ups and downs of pain from report to report" isolates a current-free relationship, though it is a small, single-block sample.
- **On statistical power: 19 matched reports is thin.** For a Pearson correlation with n=19, the 95% CI on an observed r of 0.5 runs from roughly 0.06 to 0.79 — wide enough that a "null" result there is compatible with a real relationship, and a "positive" result is compatible with no relationship at all once you account for testing multiple bands (this platform already tests 22) and within-day autocorrelation (repeated ratings on the same day are not independent draws). RCS08's own decision log records exactly this dynamic elsewhere: a 15-step titration ladder was shown by construction to produce a false "beyond 0.4" correlation about one draw in eight even when no true relationship exists (decision 237).
- **A relationship found with no current present is evidence of sufficiency without the current confound — not evidence that the relationship survives stimulation, or that it can drive closed-loop control.** Several papers below (Kühn et al. 2006; Feldmann et al. 2022; Neumann et al. 2023) document that the very metric candidates for a biomarker (beta power) is causally reshaped by stimulation amplitude, so "found off, unverified on" is a real and separately-documented gap, not a hypothetical one.
- **Recommended plan:** treat the 0 mA window as a small, already-collected discovery/pilot set for a strictly within-period, current-free analysis (Pearson/Spearman + block-bootstrap CI on the ~19 reports, correcting for the 22 bands tested and for within-day repeats), pre-register which band and which direction will count as replicated, and then test transfer explicitly by asking whether the same band-pain sign holds during the stable, non-zero, later current period, rather than assuming it does.

## 1. Precedent: biomarkers established with stimulation off, and whether they held once stimulation was on

**Chronic pain, RCS08's own paradigm**

- **Shirvalkar P, Sellers KK, Prosky J, et al. First-in-human prediction of chronic pain state using intracranial neural biomarkers.** *Nature Neuroscience* 26, 1090–1099 (2023). PMID 37217725. (Note: this is Nature Neuroscience, not Nature Medicine as sometimes cited informally.) Four participants with chronic neuropathic pain (three post-stroke, one phantom limb) had bilateral electrodes recording from anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) for 3–6 months, using an earlier-generation sensing-capable investigational device (predecessor to Percept). The paper built personalized decoders of self-reported pain intensity from OFC and ACC power spectra; OFC signals tracked chronic (sustained) pain state most reliably, while ACC signals tracked acute/evoked pain. **Relevant to issue 4:** the chronic-pain decoders were built from recordings taken across the full course of the study, which included periods with stimulation off (before any therapeutic stimulation was delivered) as well as after some stimulation testing began; the paper does not report a formal off-vs-on transfer test of the same decoder. It is direct precedent for using long, ecologically-collected recordings (not a lab task) to relate LFP power to pain, and its finding that biomarkers were highly individual (not a shared frequency/location across patients) is a caution against expecting the RCS08 0 mA finding to generalize even to RCS08's own later state, let alone to other patients. Limitation: n=4, no independent stimulation-off replication reported, and post-stroke/phantom-limb pain differ from RCS08's presumed etiology.
- **Shirvalkar P, Sellers KK, Schmitgen A, Prosky J, Joseph I, Starr PA, Chang EF. A Deep Brain Stimulation Trial Period for Treating Chronic Pain.** *Journal of Clinical Medicine* 9(10):3155 (2020). DOI 10.3390/jcm9103155. This is the PI's own earlier paper, in the same DBS-for-pain patient population this platform serves. It formally describes the "insertional effect": of 21 chronic-pain DBS patients, roughly 43% showed a substantial (in some cases 60–100%) drop in pain immediately after lead placement, before stimulation was ever turned on, and the size of that insertional response correlated with eventual response to chronic stimulation. **This is the single most directly relevant citation to issue 4**: it establishes, in the PI's own cohort, that the earliest post-implant weeks (exactly the window that contains RCS08's 0 mA block) are themselves a biologically active period independent of current, capable of producing large, symptom-relevant changes that have nothing to do with any brain-signal biomarker being tested. Limitation: it is about clinical pain trajectory, not LFP-band content, so it does not by itself say whether the *specific bands* correlated with pain during that window would have moved for reasons unrelated to a genuine band-pain relationship — but it establishes that the window is not "confound-free," only "current-free."

**Depression, subcallosal cingulate (SCC) DBS**

- **Alagapan S, Choi KS, Heisig S, et al. Cingulate dynamics track depression recovery with deep brain stimulation.** *Nature* 622, 130–138 (2023). DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06541-3. Six patients receiving SCC DBS for treatment-resistant depression, each with a chronically implanted sensing-capable device recording LFPs over months of stimulation. Using explainable-AI methods on SCC local field potentials, the authors identified a common electrophysiological signature that tracked the trajectory from depressed to stably recovered state, and validated it in an independent cohort. **Relevant to issue 4:** this biomarker was discovered and validated *during ongoing stimulation* (SCC DBS is thought to work partly through the stimulation itself, and pre-stimulation baseline windows are not usable the way RCS08's 0 mA window is, because SCC DBS effects can be immediate and the disease itself waxes and wanes over months). It is a counter-example to the "discover off-stim, hope it holds on-stim" strategy: here the signature was built entirely within the on-stimulation regime, sidestepping the transfer question altogether. Limitation: n=6, single-institution, biomarker specific to SCC anatomy.
- **Scangos KW, Makhoul GS, Sugrue LP, Chang EF, Krystal AD. State-dependent responses to intracranial brain stimulation in a patient with depression.** *Nature Medicine* 27, 229–231 (2021). PMID 33462446. A single depression patient with multi-site intracranial electrodes received brief test stimulation across many sites and days; the same stimulation site and parameters produced different, context- and mood-state-dependent emotional/behavioral responses on different occasions. **Relevant to issue 4:** this is direct, if small-n, evidence that a brain-stimulation (and by extension a brain-signal) relationship measured in one state is not guaranteed to generalize to another state — exactly the risk in generalizing a 0 mA (early, high-pain, emotionally and physiologically distinct) relationship to a later, lower-pain, stimulated state. Limitation: single case, concerns acute mood responses to test stimulation rather than a chronic power-based biomarker.
- **Scangos KW, Khambhati AN, Daly PM, et al. Closed-loop neuromodulation in an individual with treatment-resistant depression.** *Nature Medicine* 27, 1696–1700 (2021). DOI 10.1038/s41591-021-01480-w. Single patient; multi-day intracranial recording and focal test stimulation first identified a personalized symptom-specific biomarker and effective stimulation site, then a chronic sensing-and-stimulating device delivered biomarker-triggered closed-loop therapy, producing rapid, sustained improvement. **Relevant to issue 4:** the biomarker-discovery phase here was done with careful, repeated test stimulation (not passive off-periods), and the closed-loop phase that followed showed the discovered biomarker continued to be usable once therapeutic stimulation was live — i.e., in this one case, the relationship did carry over. But the discovery phase deliberately sampled many stimulation conditions rather than relying on a single "off" period, which is a stronger design than a single naturally-occurring 0 mA window. Limitation: n=1; biomarker discovery required an extensive, structured multi-week testing phase that RCS08's dataset does not have.

**OCD, ventral striatum / ventral capsule**

- **Provenza NR, Sheth SA, Dastin-van Rijn EM, et al. Long-term ecological assessment of intracranial electrophysiology synchronized to behavioral markers in obsessive-compulsive disorder.** *Nature Medicine* 27, 2154–2164 (2021). PMID 34887577. Chronic ventral striatum LFP recordings synchronized to disease-specific behavior over 1,000+ hours in clinic and at home, spanning periods of active DBS and exposure therapy, across a wide range of symptom severity. Candidate neural biomarkers of OCD symptom intensity were identified from this large, ecologically varied dataset rather than from a single off-stimulation snapshot. **Relevant to issue 4:** this paper's design strategy — using natural variation in symptom severity across many recording hours and multiple stimulation states, rather than isolating one "clean" window — is an alternative to the PI's proposal and arguably a stronger one for a chronic, ecological dataset like RCS08's, because it lets the current itself be modeled/adjusted for rather than assumed absent. Limitation: does not report a specific off-vs-on comparison for a single biomarker.
- **Provenza NR, Reddy S, Allam AK, et al. Disruption of neural periodicity predicts clinical response after deep brain stimulation for obsessive-compulsive disorder.** *Nature Medicine* 30, 3004–3014 (2024). DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-03125-0 (author correction: *Nature Medicine* 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-03667-x). A cohort of OCD DBS patients; the degree to which chronic stimulation disrupted (reduced) endogenous neural oscillatory periodicity in cortical regions predicted clinical response. **Relevant to issue 4:** shows the biomarker of interest is itself something stimulation changes over time (its disruption, not its persistence, is the signal of a good outcome) — reinforcing that "the same band" measured off vs. on stimulation is not expected, a priori, to mean the same thing.

**Parkinson's disease, subthalamic beta**

- **Kühn AA, Kupsch A, Schneider GH, Brown P. Reduction in subthalamic 8–35 Hz oscillatory activity correlates with clinical improvement in Parkinson's disease.** *European Journal of Neuroscience* 23(7):1956–1960 (2006). PMID 16623853. Nine PD patients, LFP recorded on/off levodopa via DBS electrodes; the amount of beta-band suppression by dopaminergic medication correlated with clinical improvement. **Relevant to issue 4:** shows the same beta signal tracks symptom improvement whether the improvement is produced by medication or (in later companion work) by stimulation — evidence *for* a symptom-relevant band's relationship generalizing across different modulation mechanisms — but also demonstrates that beta power is *directly, causally* moved by whatever is being tested (medication here, current in RCS08's case), which is precisely the "band value is contaminated by the exposure itself" concern that makes a truly current-free test valuable in principle.
- **Feldmann LK, Lofredi R, Neumann WJ, et al. Toward therapeutic electrophysiology: beta-band suppression as a biomarker in chronic local field potential recordings.** *npj Parkinson's Disease* 8:44 (2022). DOI 10.1038/s41531-022-00301-2. Using the Percept device (the same device family as RCS08's), the authors recorded chronic subthalamic LFPs during systematic amplitude-ramping in PD patients and showed stepwise low-beta suppression with increasing stimulation amplitude (p=0.002), with low-beta power negatively correlated with movement speed and predictive of motor improvement (p<0.001). **Relevant to issue 4:** this is the clearest demonstration, in the exact device family used for RCS08, that the candidate biomarker band's power level is a direct, monotonic function of stimulation current — meaning any band-pain (or band-power) relationship measured with current varying is partly a relationship with current, exactly the confound the PI is trying to avoid, and confirming that a "clean" (current-free) read of the band is a real, distinct quantity from its "under current" read.
- **Thenaisie Y, Palmisano C, Canessa A, et al. Towards adaptive deep brain stimulation: clinical and technical notes on a novel commercial device for chronic brain sensing.** *Journal of Neural Engineering* 18(4):041005 (2021). PMID 34388744, DOI 10.1088/1741-2552/ac1d5b. Twenty patients (14 PD/STN, 5 dystonia/GPi, 1 chronic pain) implanted with the Percept PC across three centers (Lausanne, Würzburg, Haga/Leiden), the first published multi-center experience with this device's chronic BrainSense sensing. Recordings were taken in medication-off, medication-on, stimulation-off, and stimulation-on combinations; a beta peak was found in 19 of 22 STN recordings, and stimulation-only and medication-only conditions both suppressed beta, with expected movement-related reductions also captured; at-home recordings up to 49 days showed circadian and daily beta fluctuations. **Relevant to issue 4:** this is the primary technical validation paper for the exact recording platform (Percept, chronic BrainSense) used in this study, and it establishes both (a) that stimulation and medication both suppress the same "beta" signal (again, current directly moves the candidate band) and (b) that the signal has its own daily/circadian structure independent of stimulation, which the PI's "0 mA period" design does not control for.

**Microlesion / insertional effect — what an off-stimulation, early post-implant window costs you even with current genuinely at zero**

- **Hamani C, Davidson B, Lipsman N, et al. Insertional effect following deep brain stimulation electrode implants: an underreported but important phenomenon.** *Brain Communications* 6(3):fcae093 (2024). DOI 10.1093/braincomms/fcae093. A review synthesizing the mechanistic (vascular disruption, microglial activation, astrocytic encapsulation over 6–12 weeks, altered neuronal excitability) and clinical evidence for the insertional/microlesion effect across movement disorders, epilepsy, psychiatric disease, and pain. Clinical benefit from lead insertion alone is typically maximal within days but can last weeks to months; in the pain literature they cite, 43% of chronic pain patients showed 60–100% pain-score reduction from insertion alone (echoing Shirvalkar 2020 above); electrophysiological correlates include altered LFP coherence and metabolic/connectivity changes that are not attributable to stimulation at all. The authors explicitly recommend serial (not single-timepoint) post-operative assessment and caution that, without this, insertional and stimulation-related effects cannot be told apart. **This is the central methodological citation for confound #2**: it makes explicit that "current = 0" does not mean "nothing is happening to the tissue, the symptom, or the recorded signal" during the earliest post-implant weeks.
- **Tykocki T, Nauman P, Koziara H, Mandat T. Microlesion effect as a predictor of the effectiveness of subthalamic deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's disease.** *Stereotactic and Functional Neurosurgery* 91(1):12–17 (2013). DOI 10.1159/000342161. Seventy-four PD patients; motor scores measured pre-op, within 48 hours post-implant (microlesion window), and at 6 months. Greater early (insertion-only) improvement predicted greater eventual DBS benefit (mean improvement 33.6%, 47.5%, and 61.4% across three microlesion-response tertiles). **Relevant to issue 4:** shows the microlesion effect is not noise to be waited out — it is prognostically informative in its own right, and it means a "clean" early window is doing double duty as a distinct physiological event, not simply "no stimulation."
- **Peng C, Wang Z, Sun Y, et al. Subthalamic nucleus dynamics track microlesion effect in Parkinson's disease.** *Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology* 12:1370287 (2024). DOI 10.3389/fcell.2024.1370287. Fifteen PD patients (30 STN hemispheres), LFP recorded 6–48 hours post-implant and again at 1 month, off and on medication. Beta power was stable in the hyperacute window, then rose significantly by 1 month in the medication-off state as the microlesion effect waned; electrode impedance rose substantially over the same interval. **Relevant to issue 4:** direct evidence that the recorded band power itself (not just the symptom) drifts across the first weeks after implant, for reasons that have nothing to do with stimulation current — meaning the 0 mA period (2025-07-23 to 2025-10-14 on RCS08's left lead, per the platform's own dating) sits inside a window where the recording itself may still be settling. Limitation: STN/PD, not a pain target; the direction and timescale of any analogous drift at RCS08's target and contacts is unknown and unverifiable from this paper alone.
- **Tani N, Emura T, Kimoto Y, et al. Temporal Dynamics of Microlesion Effects in Subthalamic Local Field Potentials.** *Neurologia medico-chirurgica* 66(1):40–48 (2026; published online 2025). DOI 10.2176/jns-nmc.2025-0102. Thirteen PD patients, repeated STN LFP recordings across six postoperative windows spanning a full year, with periodic/aperiodic spectral decomposition (FOOOF). Total power and aperiodic components followed an inverted-U, peaking at 31–90 days and declining by ≥12 months; periodic beta power specifically rose from about 1–3 months onward and stayed elevated through 6–12 months. **Relevant to issue 4:** extends the microlesion-recording confound well beyond the first days — by this account the recording is still evolving for months, which covers essentially the entire span the platform describes as containing RCS08's 0 mA reports and casts some doubt on treating "0 mA" and "later, stable-current" as differing only in current.

## 2. What confounds an off-stimulation, early post-implant period carries, even with current genuinely at zero

Restating and integrating the studies above, an analysis that leans on RCS08's 0 mA block (2025-07-23 to 2025-10-14) as a "clean" test of the band-pain relationship has to reckon with all of the following, stacked on top of one another, none of which the absence of current removes:

1. **The microlesion/insertional effect on the symptom itself.** Roughly 43% of chronic-pain DBS patients (in the PI's own prior cohort, Shirvalkar 2020) get a large pain drop from lead placement alone; the Hamani 2024 review documents this across pain, movement-disorder, epilepsy, and psychiatric DBS, with benefit "maximal within days... but may last weeks and even months." A patient's pain trajectory during weeks 1–12 post-implant is not a stable baseline against anything: it is itself actively changing, for reasons unrelated to any brain signal.
2. **The microlesion effect on the recorded signal.** Peng et al. 2024 and Tani et al. 2026 (both STN/PD, so the numeric values are not transferable to RCS08's target but the phenomenon is general) show subthalamic beta power itself changing over the first weeks-to-months post-implant, driven by tissue reaction (edema, microglial activation, astrocytic encapsulation — Hamani et al. 2024) rather than by anything the patient is experiencing. If the recorded left-lead band power in RCS08's 0 mA window is still on this settling trajectory, a within-period correlation with pain could partly reflect two things moving together over calendar time (band settling, pain settling) rather than a moment-to-moment brain-pain relationship.
3. **Electrode-tissue interface / impedance changes.** Peng et al. 2024 report substantial impedance rises between the hyperacute and 1-month windows; impedance changes alter the amplitude (and possibly the frequency content, via low-pass filtering effects at the electrode-tissue interface) of what is recorded, independent of any underlying neural change.
4. **Surgical recovery, medication changes, and mood.** The early post-implant period typically overlaps with peri-operative pain medication adjustments, physical recovery from surgery, and the psychological impact of undergoing a major brain surgery, all of which can move self-reported pain (and plausibly mood-linked cortical/limbic signals) independent of stimulation or of any therapeutic mechanism at all.
5. **It is also the earliest calendar-time block, confounded with expectation and with learning the rating app.** This project's own decision log holds explicitly that "time is modelled nowhere" here (decisions 193–196) precisely because drift and learning effects are real but unmeasured; the 0 mA window is disproportionately early, so any residual calendar trend (novelty of the study, increasing familiarity with the NRS/VAS rating app, expectation effects around a new intervention) is concentrated there rather than spread evenly across the record the way it would be in a held-out block at a later, stable, non-zero setting.
6. **A between-period comparison (8.1 vs. ~6.1) confounds all of the above with the current itself and answers nothing about the band.** The mean-pain difference between the 0 mA block and everything after it is consistent with insertional-effect resolution, tissue/impedance settling, mood recovery, habituation to the study, medication changes, and the onset of stimulation, all at once; it cannot be attributed to current specifically, and it is not the same question as "does the band track pain within the 0 mA block."
7. **A within-period test (does the band track pain fluctuations report-to-report inside the 0 mA block) avoids confound #6 but not confounds #1–5**, because everything listed is itself changing across the ~12 weeks the 0 mA block spans, not just at its boundary — so even a purely within-period correlation could be driven by two slow trends (band settling downward, pain settling downward) moving together over those 12 weeks, rather than a true trial-to-trial coupling. This is a distinct and weaker form of the same "borrowed from a confound" problem the PI is trying to escape from the stimulation-current case.

## 3. Statistical power and design

**Sample size on the specific pair the platform already reports.** On L 1-3+, 19 of 53 matched pain reports fall in the 0 mA window. A Pearson correlation computed on n=19 has wide sampling uncertainty: as a rule of thumb (Fisher z-transform), a true population correlation of 0 has roughly a 1-in-20 chance of producing an observed |r| ≥ ~0.46 by chance alone at n=19 (two-sided α=0.05, uncorrected for multiple bands); an observed r of 0.5 carries a 95% CI of roughly 0.06 to 0.79 — compatible with anywhere from a weak to a strong relationship. This project's own constructed-data check on a similar-sized 15-step titration ladder (decision 237) found that a band with **no true relationship to pain** still produced an adjusted correlation reading beyond 0.4 about one draw in eight, and only cleared zero on its confidence interval about one draw in fourteen, purely from finite-sample noise on that few points. The 0 mA block, with about the same order of data, should be read with the same skepticism.

**Repeated measures within a day and autocorrelation.** Pain reports are not independent draws if a patient rated several times on the same day, or on consecutive days, because same-day/adjacent-day pain is autocorrelated (regression toward the same underlying state) and the LFP recorded within the same day likely shares the same slow signal drift discussed in confound #2 above. Naive Pearson/Spearman correlation on raw report-level data will understate the true uncertainty; a block bootstrap over days (resampling whole days, not individual reports) — the same device this project already uses for its cell-level intervals (`ARCHITECTURE_cache_store.md` cites block-bootstrap intervals elsewhere; decision log 183) — is the appropriate way to get an honest confidence interval here. Lin et al. (2016, *British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology* 69(3):276–290, DOI 10.1111/bmsp.12071) formally studied bootstrap hypothesis tests for exactly this situation — single-subject ("N-of-1"), small-sample, serially-correlated time series — and found bootstrap procedures have better error-rate properties than naive parametric tests in this regime; it supports (methodologically, not numerically) using a block-style bootstrap rather than a textbook Pearson p-value on RCS08's 0 mA data.

**22 bands tested.** The platform already screens 22 band centers per grid with Benjamini-Hochberg correction (decision 63) precisely because testing every band inflates the chance of a spurious "hit." Any 0 mA-period analysis must apply the same correction across bands (and, if run on more than one sensing pair, across pairs too) rather than reporting the single best-looking band as if it had been the only one tested — this project's own exploratory 252-setting search found 6 of 252 settings crossed p<0.05, roughly in line with chance once family size is accounted for (decision 229/246), a caution against over-reading one favorable band in a small window.

**Confirmatory vs. discovery framing.** Because the 21.5–27.5 Hz left-side finding was originally noticed across the *whole* record (where it is admitted to be confounded with current), using the 0 mA subset to "check" that same band is not a fully independent confirmatory test — the band was chosen using data that overlaps in time and mechanism with the 0 mA block itself, so this is at best a partially-independent replication, weighted toward the null-then-look-for-the-same-band form of circular confirmation the critical-thinking framework calls HARKing. A cleaner design pre-registers, before looking at the 0 mA data, which band and which sign will count as replicating (see plan below), and separately asks whether *any* band in the 0 mA window looks positive with pain, correcting for having asked about all 22 (an independent discovery pass), rather than only checking the one band already flagged from the confounded whole-record analysis.

**0 mA period vs. "held-out time at one unchanged setting."** The alternative — a block of time later in the record where current was constant — has the opposite tradeoff: current is fixed (so it cannot literally correlate with a within-block pain change, though decision 241's finding that current-removal shape matters, and decision 240's finding that no band beats chance out-of-sample on RCS08's stable-current data anyway, both apply there too), but the recording is likely past the microlesion/impedance-settling window (fewer of confounds #1–3), and the setting itself might be one — per decision 213, 217, 233 etc. — that happens to sit on a stimulator harmonic or off the sensing-rule-compliant pair, which is its own distortion of the band. Neither window is "clean"; they are clean with respect to different things (0 mA is current-clean but surgery/time-dirty; a later held-current block is surgery/time-cleaner but current-nonzero and setting-specific). The two should be treated as complementary checks, not a choice between one right answer and one wrong one.

## 4. What the PI's framing implies, and what it does not show

**What it would show, if a within-0-mA-block band-pain relationship is found and survives the corrections above:** the band carries information about this patient's pain that is not attributable to stimulation current, because current was flat (at zero) throughout the window — i.e., sufficiency of a pain signal independent of the current confound, for that window. That is a real and useful thing to know, and directly answers the concern (raised elsewhere in this project's decision log, e.g. decisions 232–234, 241) that the 21.5–27.5 Hz left-side finding "appears borrowed from the current in force."

**What it would NOT show, even in the best case:**
1. **That the relationship survives stimulation.** Feldmann et al. (2022) and Thenaisie et al. (2021) both show, in the same device family, that stimulation amplitude directly and monotonically suppresses beta-band power; Kühn et al. (2006) shows the analogous effect for medication. There is no guarantee, and some direct evidence to the contrary, that a band's relationship to pain measured with current at zero will hold in magnitude, direction, or even existence once current is nonzero and actively reshaping that same band. Scangos et al.'s state-dependent stimulation-response paper (2021) is a direct demonstration, albeit n=1, that brain-behavior relationships measured in one state do not automatically transfer to another state.
2. **That the device could use the band for closed-loop control.** Sufficiency evidence (the band carries pain-relevant information in a current-free window) is a weaker claim than the operational claim a closed-loop system needs: that the band remains readable and pain-relevant *while* stimulation is on and varying, which is the exact condition the device must operate under. This project's own current-confound guard (decision 240, 241) already found that no left- or right-lead band set beats its own chance-shuffled null out of sample on RCS08's actual (non-zero-current) data — so even a clean, positive 0 mA-period finding would sit alongside an existing negative finding for the on-stimulation, decodable-for-control question, and the two are not in conflict, because they answer different questions.
3. **That it generalizes beyond this one early window, or beyond this one patient.** Shirvalkar et al. 2023 found pain biomarkers were highly individual across their four patients — no shared band or location. A relationship found in RCS08's specific 12-week 0 mA window is evidence about RCS08's specific 0 mA window, not a general biological claim.

## Recommended analysis plan for RCS08

1. **Restrict to a genuinely within-period design.** Using only the 0 mA reports (19 on L 1-3+; check and report the equivalent count on R 0-3+ and any other qualifying pair once the "was the right side also off" question referenced in the prompt is resolved), compute, for each of the 22 band centers, the correlation between band power and the matched pain score using only reports inside the window — never comparing means before/after the window.
2. **Use a block bootstrap over calendar days (not individual reports) for every interval**, so that same-day or adjacent-day repeated measures do not inflate apparent precision; this mirrors the block-bootstrap machinery this project already uses for cell-level intervals (decision 183) and is directly supported for small serially-correlated single-subject series by Lin et al. (2016).
3. **Correct for the 22 bands tested** with the same Benjamini-Hochberg procedure already used on the full grid (decision 63), and report both the single pre-specified band (21.5–27.5 Hz, the one under suspicion of being current-borrowed) and an uncorrected scan of all 22 as an honest discovery pass, clearly labeled as exploratory.
4. **Explicitly model or at least report a time trend within the window** (report date, or days since implant) alongside the band-pain correlation, so that a slow joint drift (band settling from the microlesion effect, pain settling from the insertional effect and surgical recovery) can be distinguished, as far as 19 points allow, from a report-to-report coupling. This does not require building a new time-modeling capability — decisions 193–196 keep time out of the platform's calculations, and that constraint is not violated by simply *reporting* a same-window time trend as description rather than as an input to any stored product.
5. **State the finding at the confidence its sample size supports** — with n≈19 and multiple bands tested, treat any single-band positive result as a lead requiring replication (in the language this project already uses for its titration-ladder findings, decision 237: "a lead to repeat, never an established result"), not as confirmation.
6. **Separately and explicitly test transfer**, rather than assuming it: compare the 0 mA-period band-pain relationship (sign and, if computable, size) against the same band's relationship during a later, stable, non-zero-current block (the "held-out time at one setting" alternative), reporting both numbers side by side. Given this project's existing finding that current-adjusted, out-of-sample decoding does not beat chance on RCS08's on-stimulation data for any band (decisions 240–241), a plausible and useful outcome of this comparison is "the band was informative when current was absent, and stops being informative once current is present" — which is itself the answer to the question the PI is asking, and should be reported as such rather than treated as a failure of the analysis.
7. **Report the microlesion/insertional caveat every time this window's result is quoted**, in one sentence: "these 19 reports come from the first ~12 weeks after implant, when pain and recorded band power are both still settling from surgery independent of any stimulation, so a within-window relationship is evidence the band is not simply tracking current, but it is not yet evidence the band is tracking pain and nothing else."
