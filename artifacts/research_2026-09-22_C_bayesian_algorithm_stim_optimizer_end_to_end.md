# The Bayesian algorithm in Stim Optimizer: what it does, where it goes on screen, and whether it needs an overhaul

> Research report
> Researcher: worker-research | Date: 2026-09-22
> Confidence: MIXED — the search-and-safety engine is sound and well tested (HIGH); the recommendation it produces for RCS08 today is correctly "no current, most rates" because the record is thin and confounded with time (HIGH); two tracks described in the module's own documents (the acute in-clinic search stage, the preference model, the "back" parallel optimizer) are not reached by the page the clinician actually opens (HIGH); whether a runtime calibration gate blocks a bad batch, and whether the acute-rating instrument now exists, could not be confirmed in the time available (LOW — flagged in Gaps).

## Five-minute summary

Stim Optimizer fits a Gaussian-process (GP) surrogate — the same family of model used in every clinically-deployed DBS optimizer in the literature (Sarikhani 2022, Cole 2024, Louie 2021) — over stimulation rate and the two currents (left and right), and picks the next setting to test by Expected Improvement under a second, separate GP that models side-effect risk and vetoes anything not judged safe. That machinery is well built: it distinguishes "measured and not better" from "not measurable at all" everywhere, it will not silently extrapolate past the safety model's evidence, and when the BoTorch/PyTorch alternative was actually tried and measured against it (`BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md`), it added no accuracy and was correctly deleted. The device's own rule — that stimulation rate and pulse width lock in the moment closed-loop sensing is configured, so they must be settled *before* closed loop can be considered — is encoded as an explicit two-stage pipeline with a four-condition gate that reports "not assessed" rather than guessing when a question was never asked.

The reason the page currently recommends nothing is not a flaw in this engine; it is an honest read of a small, confounded, mostly-single-replicate record (on the order of 90-120 exposure "epochs" depending which capture you read, few with more than one rating, current and calendar time tangled together for most of the record). The module's own "honest current" rule (decision 158) requires three things to hold before it will print a milliamp number for a given stimulation rate — the current-response surface actually varies more than its own noise, the best cell beats today's setting by more than the uncertainty in that difference, and at least six distinct current pairs have been tried with real replication on real different days — and on RCS08 today essentially no rate clears all three. Separately, and for a reason that has nothing to do with statistics, the device itself currently allows closed-loop sensing on only two contact pairs on RCS08, and neither shows a biomarker that both moves with current and tracks pain (decision 217); closed-loop Adaptive Therapy is not programmable on this participant today for a hardware-configuration reason, before any statistical question is even asked.

Two tracks that are correctly built and referenced in the module's own literature review — the preference-learning GP over forced A/B choices, and the acute in-clinic Stage-1 search the two-stage design document calls for — are not reached by the page the clinician opens: the preference model runs only inside an "ILLUSTRATIVE" figure fed synthetic comparisons manufactured from the same number the model is supposed to check against, and the acute-rating instrument the in-clinic search needs was, as of the design document, not in the PRO battery at all (status unconfirmed as of today — see Gaps). The frontend, for its part, states these things about as plainly as the engineering allows, but its running order puts the open-loop recommendation and two separate closed-loop-readiness tables ahead of the single most consequential yes/no on the page (can closed loop even be programmed today), and that yes/no arrives last, in the card with the most words.

**Recommendation: (b) partial overhaul**, four named, bounded changes (§4) — none of which touches a standing PI decision — plus a frontend re-ordering (§5). No case is made here for (c) a full overhaul of the surrogate or acquisition engine: that was already tried once (the PyTorch/BoTorch branch), measured, and correctly rejected on the evidence.

## 1. The algorithm as built

### 1.1 What is maximised (minimised), and the side-effect cost

The quantity the search minimises is `J`, defined in `BRAVO/modules/StimOptimizer/routines/objective.py` (`build_objective`, `PAIN_METRICS`, `SE_LADDER`):

```
J(x) = J_pain(x) + J_SE(x) + w_E * J_energy(x)      (lower is better; J = 0 at today's setting)
```

- **J_pain** is the chosen pain score at setting `x` minus the same score at the *incumbent* — the most recently rated setting, not a stim-off baseline (there is no clean stim-off period on RCS08: the two historical all-zero-left epochs still had the right side delivering 3.1 mA). The default outcome is the **left leg** score (`PAIN_METRICS["left_leg"]`), chosen because a per-site fixed-effects analysis found the global "Overall" rating does not detect an on/off stimulation effect at all (+0.27 points, p = 0.25) while the left-leg score does (+1.31 points, p = 0.016) — the participant's global rating tracks her back, not her leg. A second site, "back," is registered as an intended **parallel** optimizer (never a blended average — the two sites' rankings of the same 71 historical epochs correlate only 0.48, so averaging them would erase a real disagreement) but **is not currently computed on the live two-stage page** — see §2 and §6.
- **J_SE**, the side-effect cost, is a four-rung ladder in NRS points: none = 0, mild = 1.0, a persistent-mild score of 2 on the clinic sheet's own scale = 2.0 (the PI's ruling of 2026-09-15), and moderate or severe = **+infinity** — a hard veto, not a large finite penalty, because a finite penalty could in principle be outbid by a large enough pain benefit and the PI's intent is that it never can be.
- **J_energy** (total electrical energy delivered, `amplitude² × pulse width × frequency`) exists and is implemented but its weight defaults to **0** and it is not exposed on any control the page draws; it is documented as available for a case-report use, off by default (`OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md` §2.4).

Every historical exposure period ("epoch") enters as one observation with its own variance — smaller for a densely-rated, long-held setting, larger for a single-report or short one — rather than one row per rating, which is what "use all the data, and do not overtrust the small pieces" means concretely (`objective.py` `observation_variance`, referenced from `OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md` §3). An age-based extra distrust term that used to inflate old observations' variance further was **removed** (decision 194) after it was found to strengthen, not weaken, the time confound: because amplitude and calendar time move together for long stretches of this record, distrusting old (low-amplitude) points more than new (high-amplitude) ones made the "amplitude helps" reading look nearly three times stronger than the unweighted data support. Confidence: **HIGH** (file read directly; the finding and its reversal are decision 194 and `OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md`'s dated amendment of 2026-08-29).

### 1.2 The surrogate: kernel, inputs, noise, and how the two sides and pulse width enter

`BRAVO/modules/StimOptimizer/routines/surrogate.py` implements `ObjectiveGP` and `SafetyGP`, both scikit-learn `GaussianProcessRegressor` fits with a **Matern-3/2 kernel with automatic-relevance-determination (ARD) length scales** — the kernel every clinically-deployed implementation the module cites also uses (Sarikhani 2022, Cole 2024, Louie 2021). Frequency (or rate) is placed on a **log2 axis**, because DBS frequency effects are conventionally multiplicative and one length scale cannot serve 10 Hz and 165 Hz on a linear axis; amplitude axes are linear; every axis is then standardised to unit scale. Observation noise enters through scikit-learn's per-point `alpha` (the fixed-noise variant), so the heteroscedastic weighting of §1.1 reaches the fit directly rather than through a single shared noise term.

Two grid shapes exist:

- `ParameterGrid` — 2-D (rate, amplitude), used by the **original flat entry point** `pipeline.run`, which fits one such surface per (pain site × hemisphere) "arm." **This entry point is no longer called by the live page** (see §2).
- `JointParameterGrid` — 3-D (rate, amplitude-left, amplitude-right), used by the **two-stage path** the page actually calls. It exists because both stimulators on this device are reprogrammed *concurrently* — there is one shared rate column in the settings history, never a per-side rate — so a model that varies one side's current while treating the other side's simultaneous current as inert is confounded by construction (`surrogate.py` docstring, decision "model the left and right sides together because they're always on," 2026-09-14). This is a genuine, stated engineering reason for pooling the two sides, not a convenience.

Inside the joint fit, epochs are further split into **pulse-width strata**: one 3-D surface per (pulse-width-left, pulse-width-right) pair with at least 8 epochs (`stage1_openloop.py`), because a single kernel spanning pulse width would let the fit borrow strength across pulse widths the design never actually varies independently of rate — and the module found, and fixed, exactly this failure once already (§3.1). Within each stratum, the **current recommendation itself** is read off a further-reduced 2-D `RateStratum` surface — amplitude-left × amplitude-right, fitted at **one** rate — rather than off the pooled 3-D surface, because the pooled surface's apparent precision at any one rate was found to be borrowed from *other* rates through the shared rate axis (measured on RCS08: the pooled surface's own range across every safe cell at 55 Hz was 0.965–0.969, i.e. flat, because its kernel's signal amplitude sits at its lower bound — its argmin there is noise). This is documented in `RateStratum`'s own docstring as the reason the class exists, dated 2026-09-14.

**Frequency's length scale is pinned, not fitted**, at one octave (0.823 on the standardised log2 axis), because marginal-likelihood maximisation on the historical design drives it to the edge of its box (log marginal likelihood essentially flat between 0.02 and 0.15) — a property of the *design* (frequency levels are separated in time, so between-frequency contrasts absorb the temporal trend) rather than of the physiology. `BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md` profiled this properly with a second backend and found the likelihood **bimodal** (a "treat every frequency as independent" mode at 0.22 and a "borrow across everything" mode at 2.56, 2.8 nats apart) and that a log-normal length-scale *prior* — the more fashionable, more "Bayesian" alternative to pinning — does not resolve this: at the prior scale BoTorch uses by default it reproduces the same near-independent answer maximum likelihood does, and at a tight enough scale to reach the smooth answer it simply reports the *prior's* number back, "less honest... because it would carry the appearance of having been estimated." The module's conclusion — state the pin as a declared scientific assumption rather than dress it up as either an MLE or a prior — is, on the evidence quoted, the right call. Confidence: **HIGH**.

Amplitude's length scale, by contrast, *is* fitted, and its value has moved substantially across data corrections (from "the response is nearly flat in amplitude, 2.23 on a unit axis" in the earliest 45-epoch fit, to a real fitted dose-response, 3.81, once a parser defect that had silently truncated the amplitude record was fixed) — a concrete illustration that this surrogate's conclusions have been sensitive to data-pipeline correctness, not only to sample size.

### 1.3 The acquisition rule

`BRAVO/modules/StimOptimizer/routines/acquisition.py`: **Expected Improvement (EI) for minimisation**, `(best − μ)·Φ(z) + σ·φ(z)`, evaluated **exhaustively** over the grid (228–612 cells depending on the amplitude range in force) rather than through a gradient-based acquisition optimiser, "which is what Sarikhani et al. did" — a grid this small has nothing for a continuous optimiser to buy. Batches of `q` settings for one visit are chosen **sequential-greedy**: after each pick, the surrogate is re-conditioned on a *fantasy* observation at the chosen cell (the kriging-believer heuristic, posterior mean as the pretend outcome, hyperparameters frozen) so a batch does not collapse onto one mode. A GP-UCB acquisition and a between-visit batch selector (Kaplan & Murray 2021, doi:10.1111/biom.13313) were implemented, found to have **zero production callers**, and deleted 2026-09-12 (decision 137/`review_2026-09-12_StimOptimizer.md` S14) — a second example of the team measuring before keeping.

The **exploration queue** — "what to test next, ranked" — is every never-tested cell (fewer than 3 reports) whose *optimistic* bound (`μ − 2σ`) still beats the incumbent; this is reported as the honest reading of "is the apparent plateau local or global," not as a ranked list of good bets, and `FIGURES.md`'s own figure 5 caption says so explicitly: on the 45-epoch run, 369 of 373 under-tested cells still beat the incumbent on their optimistic bound, "a statement about how little this warm start knows, not a list of 369 promising settings."

### 1.4 The safety ceiling and the safe set

`routines/surrogate.py`'s `SafetyGP` is a **second, independent** GP over side-effect severity with a **monotone-non-decreasing-in-amplitude polynomial prior mean** (Sarikhani et al.'s device), guaranteed monotone by a running-maximum envelope so it cannot predict *lower* risk at *higher* current on extrapolation. The safe set is `μ_SE + β·σ_SE < 3.0` (the "moderate" severity rung), with `β = 2.0` the single exposed conservatism knob — Cole et al. 2024 (doi:10.1088/1741-2552/ad6cf3) swept 30 configurations across 6 subject models × 15 trials and found `β` the only parameter that significantly predicted unsafe overshoot, which is the module's stated reason for exposing only that one. A per-batch expansion cap (+0.4 mA if nothing worse than "none" has been seen, +0.2 mA after a "mild," 0 after moderate/severe) is a second, independent brake against the known tendency of a confidence-bound safe set to expand aggressively.

**What actually seeds the safety model on RCS08 today** is not a fitted severity model at all: it is a **two-anchor synthetic seed** (`safety_ceiling.py`, `safety_seed`) — severity 0 at every (rate, current) this participant has *sustained* for at least 72 hours, and severity 3 (the veto threshold) at **one current the PI has stated per side**, replicated across every rate on the grid as a flat line. That stated ceiling is currently **4.5 mA both sides** (lowered from 5.0 mA on 2026-09-14, "his words, verbatim"), replacing two earlier, measurably worse choices: a 2026-08 hard-coded snapshot of twelve device-reported `UpperLimitInMilliAmps` values, and then every such value read live from the settings stream — both were found to be wrong **in kind**, because a device-reported upper limit is sometimes the adaptive controller's own working range rather than a clinician's stated ceiling (decision 136), and using it as a severity-3 anchor collapsed the Left side's reachable safe ceiling to 0.1 mA against a record showing 4.5–4.8 mA tolerated for weeks. A genuine, prospectively-fitted ordinal severity model (`routines/safety_ordinal.py`, an AEPsych-derived ordered-probit GP over the coded clinic-sheet steps) **was built, evaluated at length in `BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md`, and then deleted** on 2026-09-12 as reached by nothing in the running platform — its own findings (the safe set covers only 14–26 of 450 grid cells depending on random seed and how strictly "no side effect noted" is read; **zero** cells above 4 mA are ever classified safe; the risk structure sits in **frequency**, not amplitude, with the highest estimated risk near 125 Hz) are a genuinely useful characterisation of this participant's record that the live safety model, running on the two-anchor synthetic seed, does not currently use at all. Confidence: **HIGH** for what exists in code today; **MEDIUM** on whether the ordinal model's findings should be revived, since that document itself says its absolute risk numbers are uncertain by roughly a factor of three depending on how ambiguous side-effect notes are coded.

### 1.5 The two-stage device gate

`stage1_openloop.py`, `routines/stage_gate.py`, `stage2_closedloop.py` encode the Percept device's own constraint (A610 Clinician Programming Guide pp. 34–35): **rate and pulse width freeze the instant BrainSense sensing is configured**, and a group running Adaptive Therapy has a **higher minimum rate (55 Hz, PI-stated and consistent with the labelling's stated direction)** than an open-loop group. Stage 1 therefore searches rate × pulse width × amplitude and **freezes** a `FrozenConfiguration` (an immutable dataclass — `cfg.rate_hz = 130` raises rather than silently changing the plan) only when the frozen rate and pulse width are **resolved** against their own uncertainty, using the same shared arithmetic everywhere (`routines/resolution.py`: `gain > k·sd_of_difference`, `k = 1`, with the joint variance deliberately *not* subtracting the (uncarried) covariance term, which makes the rule **conservative** — it can withhold a call it might have supported, never manufacture one). A clinician may override an unresolved freeze, but only with a **mandatory, non-empty stated reason**; an override with no reason is refused as indistinguishable from disabling the check.

Once frozen, `stage_gate.evaluate_gate` checks **four conditions, none short-circuiting**, each returning `True` / `False` / **`None`** (a genuinely different answer from `False`: "the question could not be put to the data" rather than "the data answered no"):

1. Rate ≥ 55 Hz on every frozen side.
2. Rate **and** pulse width resolved (or overridden).
3. A sensed band **entirely inside 8–30 Hz** shows power falling with stimulation current — the device's control signal — **per frozen side**, on that side's own sensing evidence at that side's own frozen rate (a real, found-and-fixed bug: the first live run let one side's sensing contact license closed loop on *both* sides, §3.1).
4. Proposed adaptive current limits sit inside the delivered range and under the PI-stated ceiling.

Stage 2 only enumerates closed-loop policies if the gate passes; every candidate is validated against device rules (`percept_adaptive.validate_policy`) and a policy that fails is **discarded with its reason recorded, never clipped into a nearby "fixable" value** — the module's stated reasoning is that a clipped value is "a fabrication wearing the shape of a result." Because no closed-loop pain outcome has ever been observed on this device, valid policies are ranked by a **device-deployability** criterion (the standardised separation between the two captured band-power readings) rather than by predicted benefit, and every summary text says so.

### 1.6 The honest-current checks (decisions 158, 184)

For each stimulation rate inside each pulse-width stratum, `stage1_openloop._rate_stratum_resolution` and `current_coverage` require **all three** before a milliamp figure is printed:

- **Not flat**: the safe-cell range of that rate's 2-D (left-mA, right-mA) surface exceeds one median posterior standard deviation.
- **Beats the incumbent**: the best cell's predicted gain over the incumbent (read from the 3-D joint model's own extrapolation-aware incumbent prediction, but compared against *this rate's own*, never-borrowed candidate mean) exceeds one standard deviation of that difference.
- **Enough combinations tried**: at least **6** distinct (left, right) current pairs, each with **≥5** reports, each spanning **≥2** distinct California calendar days (decision 184's coverage counts occasions, not ratings), with the qualifying pairs spanning **≥1.0 mA** on *each* axis separately.

This is a materially stricter bar than a plain significance test, and it is stated as a design choice rather than a limitation: "a current can be recommended... only when the record can actually distinguish it." On RCS08, essentially every rate fails at least one leg of this today (§3.2).

### 1.7 The preference model (built, not reached)

`routines/preference.py` implements a probit pairwise-preference GP (Chu & Ghahramani's formulation, Laplace-approximated posterior, sigma fixed at 1.0 following Louie et al. 2021) exactly as the cited literature specifies, including a proper held-out accuracy check (`holdout_accuracy`, K-fold, mirroring Zhao et al. 2021's validation standard). It is used in exactly one place: `routines/plots.fig4_dual_model`, an explicitly **"ILLUSTRATIVE"** figure whose own caption says "no A-versus-B preference judgements exist in this dataset" — the 345 "comparisons" it fits on are manufactured deterministically from the same `J` the objective GP already regresses (`J_i + 0.15 < J_j`), so its reported 84.1% held-out accuracy is stated in the same document to be "optimistically biased by construction," a warning about self-consistency, not a genuine result. **This whole track is unreached by the live page**: `fig4` is only ever drawn by `pipeline.run`, which the live request path stopped calling on 2026-09-14 (§2), and even when it was called the figure was fed synthetic, not real, comparisons because the forced-choice A/B instrument does not exist in the record.

### 1.8 Validation

`OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md` §6 pre-registers a runtime requirement: "select settings before the surrogate passes held-out calibration... a surrogate that cannot predict a held-out era does not get to choose." `ObjectiveGP.loo_predict` (leave-one-epoch/leave-one-group-out, refit from scratch per fold so hyperparameters do not leak) is implemented and is the mechanism such a check would use. **I could not confirm, in the files read, that this calibration check is wired as a runtime gate inside `pipeline.run_two_stage` / `stage1_openloop.run_stage1`** — every calibration run I found evidence of (the frequency-length-scale profiling, the leave-one-era-out comparisons in `BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md`) was performed as a one-off analysis, not as code that would refuse to emit a batch. This is flagged as a gap (§6), not asserted as a defect, because the relevant call sites (`stage1_openloop.py` beyond what was read, `pipeline.run_two_stage`) were not fully read line-by-line for this specific control-flow question.

## 3. Critique

### 3.1 Where the engineering is sound

- **The device constraint is modelled as a hard sequence, not a soft preference.** The two-stage split, the immutable `FrozenConfiguration`, and the mandatory-reason override are a faithful, testable encoding of a real hardware limitation (A610 pp. 34–35), and the module found and fixed its own violation of this discipline on the first live run (Stage 1 froze 40 Hz, below the adaptive minimum, before the envelope constraint existed — `routines/adaptive_envelope.py`'s own docstring names this as the reason it was built).
- **Three-state logic is applied consistently.** `resolved / not resolved / not assessed` (or `PASS/FAIL/None`) is used identically in the resolution rule, the gate, the readiness screen, and the honest-current checks, and `None` is never silently treated as a pass. This is the single most load-bearing engineering habit in the module and it is applied without exception in everything read for this report.
- **The team measures rather than assumes, repeatedly, and reverses itself on the evidence.** The pooled-3D-surface-borrows-precision finding (§1.2), the age-inflation-worsens-the-confound finding (§1.1), the wrong-kind safety anchors (§1.4), the Right-hemisphere-pulse-width-mislabelling bug (S1, `review_2026-09-12_StimOptimizer.md`) were all caught by deliberately re-deriving a number and comparing it against what the code claimed, not by inspection. The BoTorch evaluation (`BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md`) is a genuinely rare thing in software: a team that built an alternative, measured it honestly against the incumbent, found it added nothing, and deleted it rather than keeping it "just in case."
- **The safety model's structural choices track the literature closely and correctly cite it**: the Matern-3/2 kernel, the single exposed `β` (Cole 2024), the monotone prior mean and per-batch expansion cap (Sarikhani 2022), the two-anchor seed design. This is not superficial citation-dropping; the numbers measured against each choice (§1.4) are the numbers the cited papers' own ablations would predict.

### 3.2 Where it is fragile, specifically with this participant's data

- **Sample size and replication.** Depending which dated capture is read, the historical record holds somewhere between roughly 45 and 123 "epochs" (stretches of one unchanged setting), with the number of epochs carrying **three or more** ratings — the module's own floor for treating a cell as "tested" at all — running from 23 to 56 across those same captures. A 3-D grid with up to 33 amplitude levels per side has vastly more cells than replicated observations; this is precisely the "sparse warm start" regime the module's own figure captions describe, where "almost any cell's optimistic bound will beat an incumbent whose mean is [near zero]" (Figure 5's caption, `FIGURES.md`) — the exploration queue is wide not because many settings look promising, but because almost nothing has been measured.
- **Time is the dominant confound, and it is a closed question, not an open one.** `days` correlates with `NRS` at −0.49 to −0.61 depending on the capture, and with `amplitude` at anywhere from −0.14 to +0.92 depending on which period of the record is read — the *sign* of the amplitude–time relationship has flipped at least once as more data and a parser fix arrived. The module's response — era-blocked estimation, randomised within-visit order for prospective data, an explicit refusal to attribute the historical trend to any one parameter — is the correct response to a confound this severe, and it is a **standing PI decision** (193/196) that time is not modelled explicitly, on clinical grounds specific to this participant (more than three years into disease, so drift is read as a stimulation effect, not disease progression). Any recommendation to "just add a time-varying kernel" would reverse that ruling and is not made here.
- **Most cells are tried once, and the honest-current rule correctly refuses on nearly all of them.** The coverage requirement (≥6 current pairs, ≥5 reports each, ≥2 distinct days, ≥1 mA span each axis) is a genuinely strict bar, and on RCS08 it is failed at essentially every stimulation rate today — which is the right answer given the data, not a bug in the rule.
- **The device configuration itself, independent of statistics, currently forecloses closed loop.** Decision 217 establishes that the sensing pair the device allows is fixed by which contacts are stimulating (the pair must flank the stimulating contact), and on RCS08's current contacts the only two allowed pairs (L 1-3+, R 0-3+) have **no** band that rises with pain — so the usable-cell count for closed loop is **0 of 50** today, for a reason that would not change no matter how much more pain-rating data arrived. This is a hardware/programming fact, correctly modelled, and it is easy to miss on the page because it is stated in one table (`SensingEvidenceTable.js`) that a reader could plausibly not connect to the separate closed-loop-checks table lower down that asks a related but not identical question (§5).
- **The safety model's live seed is a synthetic two-point construction, not a fitted risk surface.** The one genuinely fitted, prospective severity model that exists in the module's history (`safety_ordinal.py`) found real, non-trivial structure — risk concentrated near 125 Hz, essentially no evidence above 4 mA, and a safe set that is not simply an amplitude ceiling — and none of that reaches the live safety model, which instead treats every rate as equally risky up to one PI-stated number. This is a reasonable, conservative, and transparent stand-in given that the underlying label data are ambiguous (58% of "no side effect" labels in the ordinal model's own audit were *absent* notes, not observed absences), but it means the live safe set is coarser than what this participant's own record could in principle support.

### 3.3 What the literature says the failure modes are, and whether they are present here

- **Boundary over-sampling under a small effect size.** Dastin-van Rijn & Widge 2025 (doi:10.1088/1741-2552/ade189) find that standard GP-based Bayesian optimisation fails to reliably locate the optimum once the true effect size falls below roughly Cohen's d = 0.3, because posterior variance grows disproportionately at the edges of the search space as sampling proceeds, systematically over-sampling boundaries; their mitigation is an input warp plus a boundary-avoiding (Iterated Brownian-bridge) kernel, which extends reliable performance down to d ≈ 0.1. This maps closely onto what `FIGURES.md` reports on RCS08's early fit: the posterior mean spans under one NRS point across the whole grid while the posterior SD sits near 0.33–0.47 everywhere, and the simulated forward batches are classified "explore" 93–100% of the time with amplitudes clustering at the untested edges of the safe region. **Not implemented here.** This is the single most concrete, literature-backed, low-risk improvement available (§4, item 1).
- **Non-stationarity.** The module's own `README.md` names this as its central unsolved problem, cites the two relevant published mitigations (Fleming et al. 2023 TV-BayesOpt, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011674; Aiello, Valle & Raspopovic 2023, doi:10.1088/1741-2552/acc975) precisely, states correctly that neither has prospective chronic-human validation, and proposes the right acceptance test for either ("if a time-varying kernel cannot predict a held-out era better than the static kernel with age-inflated variance, it has not earned its extra parameters"). This is a case where the module's own stated position matches the literature's actual state of the art; the gap is real but it is a research gap, not an implementation shortfall, and building it now would reverse decisions 193/196.
- **Preference-versus-objective disagreement.** Louie et al. 2021 (doi:10.1186/s12984-021-00873-9) found their objective optimiser driving toward the highest tolerable frequency while their preference model peaked at a markedly lower frequency (70–110 Hz) — a genuine, clinically important disagreement between "what a measured outcome says is best" and "what the patient would choose." The module's illustrative figure reproduces the *direction* of this on RCS08 (objective optimum 55 Hz vs a synthetic "preference" optimum at 40 Hz) but, as its own caption insists, this is a property of how the synthetic comparisons were constructed from the same `J`, not independent evidence of a real preference/outcome gap. Whether such a gap exists for this participant is genuinely unknown, because the forced-choice instrument the real preference model needs does not exist in the data.
- **Whether pooling left and right, or pooling pulse widths, is the right call.** Hierarchical/multi-output GP pooling across a dimension is only justified when the pooled dimension actually carries shared structure the data can support (the general caution in the Bayesian-optimisation-for-clinical-dose-finding literature, e.g. Bekele et al. and related dose-finding BO work on personalised versus pooled surfaces, is that pooling without justification manufactures apparent precision). The module's choice to pool the two sides is justified on a **mechanistic**, not merely statistical, ground (both currents act concurrently on the same reports), and — critically — the module *tested* whether pooling across pulse widths or across rates was safe and found it was not in the naive form, which is exactly the diagnostic the literature would recommend and exactly what led to the `RateStratum` redesign (§1.2). The pooled-pulse-width option added on 2026-09-21 (decision 189) is offered **off by default, behind a toggle, with its assumption stated on-screen** — the correct way to offer a pooling choice the data cannot fully justify.
- **Contextual bandits / Thompson sampling as an alternative framing** (searched per the assignment) are used elsewhere for n-of-1 treatment selection and slow-feedback settings similar to this one, but nothing in the literature surveyed here suggests they would outperform a GP-based safe-BO approach on a **continuous, low-dimensional** space with a genuine safety constraint; bandit formulations are more naturally suited to a small, discrete arm set (e.g., choosing among 3–5 named programs) than to a continuous (rate, current, current) surface with an amplitude-monotone risk model, and none of the cited neurostimulation literature (Sarikhani, Cole, Louie, Zhao, Grado) uses a bandit formulation for this exact problem. No change is recommended on this account.

## 4. Options and recommendation

**(a) Keep and patch.** Insufficient on its own: it would leave a literature-identified, concretely mitigable failure mode (boundary over-sampling under small effect sizes) unaddressed, and leave a real gap between a standing decision (the "back" parallel optimizer, `OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md`'s 2026-08-30 amendment) and what the live page actually computes.

**(c) Full overhaul of the surrogate/acquisition engine.** Not recommended, and not merely as a matter of taste: this was already attempted (the PyTorch/BoTorch/ordinal-severity branch), measured against the incumbent on real RCS08 data, found to add no predictive accuracy at machine-precision agreement on the posterior when hyperparameters are matched, and correctly deleted (`BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md`). Rebuilding it would need new evidence this report did not find — specifically, a search space large enough that exhaustive grid evaluation of the acquisition function stops being practical, or a genuine move to real-time closed-loop operation where the safety classification must be recomputed inside a request. Neither applies today.

**Recommended: (b) partial overhaul**, four bounded, named changes:

1. **Add a boundary-avoiding kernel option to the amplitude axis(es)** (`routines/surrogate.py`'s `_make_kernel`, `ParameterGrid`/`JointParameterGrid` transforms), following Dastin-van Rijn & Widge 2025's input-warp + Iterated Brownian-bridge construction, shipped **behind a flag**, validated the same way the module already validates every other hyperparameter choice: leave-one-era-out prediction of held-out epoch means, compared against the current kernel, on both the RCS08 record and the module's synthetic fixtures (mirroring the standard `README.md` itself already states for the deferred time-varying-kernel work: "if it cannot predict a held-out era better... it has not earned its extra parameters"). **Cost**: roughly 2–4 engineering days including the calibration comparison; new tests in `tests/test_surrogate.py` comparing calibration before/after on both real and fixture data. **Decisions touched**: none — this is squarely inside the module's own stated validation standard and reverses nothing.
2. **Turn the frequency-length-scale finding into a permanent, reported sensitivity check** rather than a one-off exploration document. `BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md` already recommends this ("If the group's position is that neighbouring frequencies should behave similarly, that position should be written into `OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md` as an assumption with a stated value... reported as an assumption everywhere it appears") and it was never carried out as a standing report field. **Cost**: under a day; touches `OBJECTIVE_SPEC.md` and, optionally, a small addition to the response so the pin is visible on the page rather than only in code comments. **Decisions touched**: none.
3. **Either wire the "back" arm into the live two-stage path, or explicitly retire it from the request.** Today the request (`Client/src/views/Reports/StimOptimizer/index.js`, `OPTIMIZER_REQUEST.Sites = ["left_leg", "back"]`) still asks the server for both sites, but the two-stage service call only ever optimises `left_leg`; nothing on the live page tells a reader that "back" is silently not part of the recommendation, and the module's own decision record (2026-08-30) treats a parallel back optimizer as a *requirement*, not an option. **Cost, if wiring it**: roughly 1–2 days — `bravo_service.two_stage_block` would run Stage 1 twice (once per site), which is close to double the current ~10 s Stage-1 cost per request, plus a new response block and a second `DecisionStrip`/`CurrentMapCard` pairing; new tests mirroring the existing per-site coverage in `tests/test_two_stage_wiring.py`. **Cost, if retiring it**: under a day — drop `"back"` from the request and say so in one sentence on the page. **Decisions touched**: none reversed; this closes a gap between a decision and its implementation rather than opposing either.
4. **A documentation and dead-code-labelling pass**, not a functional change: mark `routines/preference.py`, `pipeline.run`'s "Arms" output (`summary`/`blockers`/`recommendation_supported`), and `acquisition.check_stopping`'s output explicitly, in the module's own `README.md`, as "research-only, unreached from the live page" so a future reader does not mistake a stored `stim_optimizer_summary` entry for something the clinician has seen. **Cost**: under a day, zero code risk. **Decisions touched**: none.

Explicitly **not** recommended at this time, and named so the PI can see what was considered and set aside: reviving the ordinal severity model into the live safety seed (the label-provenance ambiguity documented in `BOTORCH_REFACTOR.md` — 58% of "no side effect" labels are absent notes, not observed absences — needs a clinical decision about the source records, not a modelling change); building the acute in-clinic Stage-1 search loop (the instrument it needs may not exist yet — see Gaps — and creating it is a clinic-workflow decision, not an engineering one); adding a time-varying kernel (would reverse decisions 193/196).

## 5. Frontend: order, one-sentence purpose per card, and concrete wording

### 5.1 What the PI's goal implies for ordering

Judged against "simple, human-readable, clearly separated ideas in logical order, followable by someone who has never read the code," the current order (`index.js`, top to bottom: Recompute bar → `DecisionStrip` → `SensingEvidenceTable` → "Evidence base" → `TitrationSessionCard` → `CurrentMapCard` → `CurrentMapScheduleCard` → `TwoStagePlanCard`) has three specific problems, each traceable to a component read in full:

1. **The headline decision loads slower than the supporting cards below it.** `DecisionStrip` depends on `useTwoStagePlan`, a *second* request fired only after the page's own response has arrived, taking on the order of ten seconds to a minute on first load (`TwoStagePlanCard.js`'s own loading text). On first paint, the top-of-page card can be showing per-side spinners while `SensingEvidenceTable`, "Evidence base," and `TitrationSessionCard" below it are already fully rendered from the faster, first request — so a first-time reader's eye meets incomplete content exactly where the page most wants it to land.
2. **Closed-loop readiness is answered twice, in two places, on two different subsets of evidence, with nothing on the page explaining why they might disagree.** `SensingEvidenceTable.js` (near the top) reports every sensing contact × rate combination that was screened; `ClosedLoopChecks.js` (inside `TwoStagePlanCard`, at the very bottom) reports the *same kind* of check but restricted to the one sensing contact per side that was actually selected at the actually-frozen rate. These can legitimately show different counts (the code says so explicitly — a contact judged deployable in general screening need not be the one the gate selected), but nothing on the page tells a first-time reader this is expected rather than a discrepancy.
3. **The prescription (what to test next) currently precedes the diagnosis (why nothing can be recommended yet), and the single most consequential yes/no on the page (can closed loop even be programmed today) arrives last, in the longest card.** A reader meets `TitrationSessionCard`'s "run this ladder next" before `CurrentMapCard`'s "and here is why no current can be recommended," and meets `TwoStagePlanCard`'s "closed loop may not start" only after three cards that already presuppose the reader knows the open-loop story.

### 5.2 Proposed order and one sentence per card

| Order | Card (component) | One sentence it must say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recompute bar / cache status (unchanged) | "This is when the numbers below were last built, and whether anything has changed since." |
| 2 | **Merged closed-loop readiness** — fold `SensingEvidenceTable.js`'s per-cell detail under `ClosedLoopChecks.js`'s headline, presented as one card with one number | "Closed loop [is / is not] programmable on this participant today, and here is the one reason why." |
| 3 | `DecisionStrip.js` | "For each side, here is what's programmed now, what the search would prefer instead, and whether that preference is proven better than today's setting." |
| 4 | `CurrentMapCard.js` | "Here is every current combination tried on each side, and why none of them yet earns a recommended number." |
| 5 | `TitrationSessionCard.js` + `CurrentMapScheduleCard.js` (kept as two cards, but adjacent and framed as one "next steps" unit) | "Here is the exact next session — in clinic and at home — that would fill the gap shown above." |
| 6 | `TwoStagePlanCard.js` (its remaining content: "if closed loop could start...") | "If closed loop could start today, here is what it would be programmed to do." |
| 7 | "Evidence base" | Demoted to a one-line footer strip rather than a full card — no decision on the page reads it directly (the 2026-09-12 design review made exactly this point about one of its fields: "Left amplitude levels 22 is dropped: no decision reads it"; the same reasoning applies to the whole card). |

### 5.3 Concrete wording changes, by component

- **[`index.js`]**: the section heading above `DecisionStrip` reads "What the joint search prefers, per side" — a description of the method, not a computed answer. Replace with a headline computed from the data, in the same style `ClosedLoopChecks.js` already uses for its own headline ("Closed loop may not start: 1 of 4 checks block"): e.g. "No side has a setting proven better than today's" or "Left has a proven better setting; Right does not," derived from the `resolved` states already on the page. This follows the figure-conventions rule the module already applies elsewhere: every headline is computed from the numbers in the same render, never a fixed string.
- **[`ClosedLoopChecks.js`]**, `CHECK_LABELS`: "A sensed band inside 8–30 Hz responds to stimulation current" names a mechanism before it says what it means. Per the house writing rule (say what it does first, name it in brackets only if needed to find the code), prefer: "Does a usable recording site's power change with current?" and keep the 8–30 Hz range and the code name as a hover or footnote for whoever needs to find the code.
- **[`SensingEvidenceTable.js`]**: today the card can read "0 of 50 contact-and-rate combinations usable for closed loop" with the *reason* (that the device currently allows only two sensing pairs at all, decision 217) available only via the "why not" column on individual rows. Because this reason applies to every row at once, it should be the **first sentence on the card**, ahead of the count: "Only 2 sensing pairs are allowed while today's contacts are stimulating; neither has a usable band, so 0 of 50 combinations are usable for closed loop" — so a reader is never left wondering why a count that may have read differently on an earlier visit now reads zero.
- **[`CurrentMapCard.js`]**: the short legend explaining what green/yellow/red and the star mean is currently hidden behind "Expand descriptions," off by default. Given the PI's own stated goal (a reader who has never read the code), the one-paragraph legend should load open by default; only the longer method paragraphs (the pooling caveat, the clinic-stream methodology) need to stay folded.
- **[`DecisionStrip.js`]**: the footer sentence "Resolved means the gain is larger than 1 standard deviation of the difference itself" is a good, plain definition and should be kept, but "resolved" should be defined the first time it is used on the page (not only in this footer), since the same word recurs in `GainBar.js`'s `VerdictGlyph` and in `ClosedLoopChecks.js`'s per-side sub-answers with the identical meaning each time — one definition, stated once near the top of the page, would satisfy the house rule that a technical term is defined on first use without repeating the definition four times.
- **[`TwoStagePlanCard.js`]**: title "Closed loop: may it start on the frozen setting?" is already plain and should be kept unchanged.
