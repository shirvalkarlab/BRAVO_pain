# The Shape of the DBS Amplitude → LFP Band-Power Relationship in Humans

> Research report
> Researcher: worker-research | Date: 2026-09-11
> Confidence: MIXED — monotonic/near-saturating suppression of beta power with amplitude is well replicated across STN and GPi; genuine non-monotonic (U-shaped/rebound) and multi-peak reports exist but come mostly from single-case or small-n studies; a true amplitude ramp-up-vs-ramp-down hysteresis study in band power was not found in the literature searched.

## Definitions (plain English)

- **Local field potential (LFP)**: the summed electrical activity of many nearby neurons, picked up by the same electrode contacts used to deliver stimulation. Sensing-capable implants (Medtronic Percept PC/RC, Activa PC+S, Summit RC+S, NeuroPace RNS) can record this signal from the DBS lead itself.
- **Band power**: the amount of signal energy in a defined frequency range (e.g., "beta" = 13–35 Hz, often split into low-beta ~13–20 Hz and high-beta ~20–35/21–35 Hz). Reported either as raw power, decibels (dB), or percent of total spectral power.
- **Amplitude (dose)**: the stimulation intensity, in milliamps (mA, current-controlled devices like Percept) or volts (older voltage-controlled devices).
- **Monotonic suppression**: power falls (or rises) consistently as amplitude increases — no reversals.
- **Saturation/plateau**: power falls initially, then further amplitude increases produce little or no additional change.
- **Inverted-U / non-monotonic / rebound**: power falls with amplitude, then rises again at higher amplitudes.
- **M-shaped / multi-peak**: two distinct spectral peaks within or near the beta range that behave differently, or where a peak is replaced/re-emerges at a different frequency as amplitude changes.
- **Hysteresis / order effect**: the amplitude–power curve is different depending on whether amplitude is being increased (ramp-up) or decreased (ramp-down) — i.e., the system's state depends on its history, not just the current amplitude.
- **Dwell time**: how long the device stimulates at a given amplitude step before power is measured, relevant because beta power can take several seconds to re-equilibrate after a change (see Feldmann et al. 2022 below: ~13 s recovery in 60% of patients after DBS cessation).

## Executive Summary

The best-replicated finding is that subthalamic (STN) and pallidal (GPi) beta-band power falls as stimulation amplitude increases from zero, most steeply over the first ~1–2 mA, with clinical benefit tracking the same range (Feldmann et al. 2022, npj Parkinson's Disease, n=10 STN, 0.5 mA steps: beta power 4.31%→1.12% of total power from OFF to 2.5 mA). However, the shape is not simply linear: two independent 2021–2026 studies show the curve bending over — either saturating with no further gain in suppression despite continued clinical improvement (Hammer et al. 2026, Movement Disorders, n=6/11 hemispheres, STN: −21.5 dB at ~1.85 mA vs −21.0 dB at ~2.84 mA, i.e., essentially flat), or actively reversing into a rebound/U-shape near the side-effect threshold (Cagle et al. 2021, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, n=1 GPi, 0.5 mA/20 s steps 0–5 mA: beta power fell then rebounded above ~3–4 mA). Genuine multi-peak ("double beta peak") dynamics are documented in two STN case reports (Koeglsperger et al. 2020; Giannini et al. 2023), where increasing amplitude suppressed one beta sub-peak but left the other largely unaffected, or caused a peak to jump to a new frequency rather than simply shrink — a finding directly relevant to the "two peaks"/"M-shaped" question, though these come from n=1 cases and the frequency-jump is a change in peak location, not a literal double-peaked amplitude curve. No study meeting the target scope (sensing-capable human DBS, LFP band power specifically) was found that directly compared ramp-up vs ramp-down amplitude sequences and reported a hysteresis effect on band power; this is a clear, confirmed gap in the literature searched.

## Key Findings

1. **Monotonic, dose-dependent STN low-beta suppression is well established with Percept-based offline analysis.** In 10 Parkinson's patients OFF medication, STN beta power (percent of total spectral power) fell from 4.31 ± 1.2% (stimulation OFF) to 4.09 ± 1.06% (0.5 mA, not significant, p=0.49), 2.27 ± 0.83% (1.0 mA, p=0.008), 1.53 ± 0.54% (1.5 mA, p=0.002), 1.17 ± 0.41% (2.0 mA, p=0.002), and 1.12 ± 0.41% (2.5 mA), using 0.5 mA steps with 60-second rest + finger-tapping dwell per step, offline wavelet analysis (Perceive toolbox/SPM12) of Percept-streamed raw LFP. Low-beta (13–20 Hz) showed larger suppression (8.43%→2.11% at best clinical amplitude) than high-beta (20–35 Hz: 2.32%→0.66%). The authors modeled the amplitude→low-beta relationship as logarithmic (R²=0.71, p<0.0001) — i.e., diminishing returns per additional mA, which is a soft form of saturation even though the paper is framed as "no plateau detected." [Feldmann et al., 2022, npj Parkinson's Disease]

2. **A near-flat (saturating) amplitude-power relationship despite continued clinical benefit has been directly quantified.** In 6 patients (11 STN hemispheres), "low" stimulation (mean 1.85 ± 0.21 mA) suppressed beta power by −21.5 ± 4.6 dB versus OFF, while "high" stimulation (mean 2.84 ± 0.23 mA — roughly 50% more current) suppressed it by −21.0 ± 4.6 dB — statistically indistinguishable, i.e., no further gain from the extra ~1 mA. Peak-frequency shift showed a similarly small difference (−3.2 ± 0.8 Hz at low vs −4.1 ± 0.8 Hz at high amplitude). The paper also reports that DBS dampens medication-induced beta fluctuations (smaller with DBS on than off, p=0.008), suggesting the biomarker "ceiling" is reached well before the amplitude range is exhausted, even as motor symptoms keep improving — a biomarker/clinical-effect dissociation. Offline PSD analysis of bipolar LFP; device-based sensing also discussed for clinical translation. [Hammer et al., 2026, Movement Disorders, 41(8):2022–2032]

3. **A clear U-shaped/rebound pattern has been reported in GPi at high amplitude, near the side-effect threshold.** In a single Parkinson's patient with GPi leads and a Percept device, stepwise monopolar stimulation from 0–5 mA in 0.5 mA increments (minimum 20 s dwell between steps) showed beta and low-gamma power falling with increasing amplitude, but then **rebounding** once amplitude approached 3–4 mA and the side-effect threshold. Optimal-contact beta power correlated with bradykinesia severity (Spearman's r=0.84, p=0.0013). Both Percept on-device streamed spectral power (2 Hz resolution) and offline Welch-periodogram analysis of raw LFP were used, with concordant results. This is the clearest empirical non-monotonic (inverted-U) amplitude-power finding identified in this search, but it is an n=1 case study. [Cagle et al., 2021, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience]

4. **Double/split beta peaks respond unevenly to amplitude, and a peak can be replaced by a new one at a different frequency as amplitude rises — a finding directly relevant to "two peaks"/re-emergence questions, but it reflects differential suppression or frequency-shift, not literal re-growth of the same peak at high amplitude.** In one STN case, at amplitudes of 0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.8, 1.2, and 1.4 mA (stepped every 2 minutes), the left STN's two beta peaks (15 Hz and 25 Hz) both gradually shrank with increasing amplitude, but on the right STN, the 15 Hz peak was **replaced by a new peak at 13 Hz** when amplitude rose from 0.8 to 1.2 mA — a frequency jump rather than a clean monotonic decline. [Koeglsperger et al., 2020, Acta Neurochirurgica]. In a second, independent STN case (low-beta ~17 Hz, high-beta ~30 Hz), the high-beta peak showed "marked suppression" with increasing amplitude while the low-beta peak "did not consistently decrease" across the amplitude sweep — i.e., the two peaks in the same patient followed different amplitude-response shapes. (Note: this paper's clearest non-monotonic, rise-then-fall pattern for high-beta power was driven by time-since-levodopa-dose, not by stimulation amplitude — the amplitude-specific finding is the differential/inconsistent suppression between the two peaks, not a double-peaked amplitude curve per se.) [Giannini et al., 2023, Frontiers in Neurology]

5. **No confirmed human study was found that directly tests ramp-up vs ramp-down amplitude sequences and reports a resulting difference (hysteresis) in LFP band power.** Searches for "hysteresis," "order effect," and "ascending/descending" stimulation-amplitude sweeps returned only (a) closed-loop *controller-design* literature describing engineered ramp/dual-threshold strategies used to prevent rapid on/off cycling in adaptive DBS (an engineering choice, not an empirically observed neural hysteresis), and (b) a report of hysteresis in patient *perceptual* thresholds (paresthesia sensation rising faster on ramp-up than it falls on ramp-down) — not LFP band power. This is treated as an open gap, not a negative finding, since it may simply reflect incomplete search coverage of the primary literature (see Gaps section).

6. **Pallidal beta/low-frequency burst amplitude also falls with therapeutic GPi stimulation, and excessive stimulation causes a rebound.** In stepwise-titration recordings (monopolar, 0.5 mA increments, 20 s per step) in PD and dystonia GPi, beta power (13–30 Hz) fell with therapeutic stimulation and correlated with bradykinesia severity; but "worsening of parkinsonism when excessive stimulation was applied was associated with a rebound in the beta band power" — corroborating finding 3 (Cagle et al.) from a related but not identical Berlin-group dataset/case description. [Search-snippet sourced; likely overlapping with, or a companion report to, Cagle et al. 2021 — see Confidence notes]

7. **A device-patent-derived dataset (values 0.67, 1, 2.25, and 3.375 mA) reports significant post-hoc, step-wise beta suppression consistent with a threshold-then-plateau shape** — "significant suppression at 3.38 mA compared to all other conditions and at 2.25 mA compared to 0.67 and 1 mA" with concurrent UPDRS bradykinesia/rigidity improvement (Wilcoxon Z=−3.62 and Z=−3.70 respectively, p<0.001) measured 60 seconds after reaching 2.25 mA. This closely matches amplitude values used by the Melbourne/Bionics Institute group's ERNA (evoked resonant neural activity) work (Sinclair et al. 2018, Annals of Neurology, used 2.25 and 3.38 mA in an overlapping STN cohort), but full-text confirmation that this exact quoted passage appears in a *peer-reviewed* paper (rather than only in a related Medtronic patent, US11298070B2 and family) could not be obtained in this search. **Flagged as LOW-confidence attribution; the empirical values are internally consistent and plausible but the primary citation is not fully verified.**

### 5. Hysteresis / ramp order effects

No study was found in this search that empirically compared ascending vs. descending amplitude sweeps and reported a resulting difference in LFP band power in humans. Related but distinct findings:

- Closed-loop DBS controllers are *engineered* with ramp/dual-threshold logic (e.g., 250 ms amplitude ramps, minimum 500 ms below-threshold dwell before ramp-down) specifically to avoid abrupt on/off cycling and paresthesia — this is a design choice to smooth stimulation delivery, not an observed hysteresis in the underlying beta-power response to amplitude.
- A hysteresis effect has been reported in *patient-perceived sensation* (steep near-linear rise in perceived intensity during ramp-up, more rapid drop-off during ramp-down) — a sensory/perceptual phenomenon, not LFP band power, and outside this report's target signal.

This is recorded as an open gap (see Gaps section) rather than a confirmed negative — the amplitude-titration papers reviewed here (Feldmann et al. 2022; Cagle et al. 2021; Koeglsperger et al. 2020) all titrate amplitude in one direction (ascending) within a single session and do not report a matched descending sweep.

## Contradictions

- **"No saturation" vs. saturating numbers in the same dataset.** Feldmann et al. (2022) explicitly state "no saturation detected, linear relationship" over 0–2.5 mA, yet their own step-by-step numbers show a clearly diminishing marginal effect (−1.82, −0.74, −0.36, −0.05 percentage points per successive 0.5 mA step) and their best-fit model is logarithmic in amplitude (R²=0.71) — a logarithmic curve is definitionally saturating in linear-amplitude space. This is not necessarily a factual contradiction between papers, but an internal tension between the authors' narrative framing ("linear," "no plateau") and their own reported numbers, which readers should not take as evidence against saturation more broadly.
- **Saturation (Hammer et al. 2026) vs. monotonic-with-room-to-fall (Feldmann et al. 2022).** Hammer et al. find essentially no further beta suppression between ~1.85 mA and ~2.84 mA, while Feldmann et al. find continued (if diminishing) suppression up to 2.5 mA. These are not strictly incompatible — Feldmann's steepest gains occur below 1.5 mA and their 2.0→2.5 mA step shows almost no further change (−0.05 percentage points), which is broadly consistent with a plateau setting in around 1.5–2 mA — but the two groups characterize the same general amplitude range differently (one calls it "linear/no plateau," the other explicitly reports saturation). Different patient cohorts, targets are the same (STN), and dB vs. percent-of-total-power units are not directly comparable, which limits a strict quantitative reconciliation.
- **Monotonic suppression (STN, most studies) vs. rebound at high amplitude (GPi, Cagle et al. 2021, and a related GPi report).** These are not necessarily contradictory since they may reflect target-specific (STN vs. GPi) or bandwidth-specific (beta only vs. beta+gamma) differences, and the rebound is reported specifically near the side-effect/side-effect-adjacent amplitude range that most STN titration studies did not push into (most STN studies stopped at 2.5–3 mA for side-effect or protocol reasons). It remains an open question whether STN beta would also rebound if pushed further into the side-effect range — no STN study found in this search tested amplitudes high enough to observe this.
- **Consistent vs. inconsistent suppression of the "other" beta peak.** In Koeglsperger et al. (2020) both peaks (15 Hz, 25 Hz) were suppressed on the left side but the low peak was replaced (not suppressed) on the right; in Giannini et al. (2023) the high-beta peak was reliably suppressed but the low-beta peak was not. Both are single-case reports and the peak assignments (which peak behaves "well") differ between them, underscoring that double-peak dynamics may be idiosyncratic to lead location/hemisphere rather than following a single canonical multi-peak shape.

## Confidence per Claim

- **STN low-beta power falls monotonically with amplitude over ~0–2.5 mA, steepest below ~1.5 mA** — HIGH. Multiple independent findings converge (Feldmann et al. 2022 direct numeric titration; broad corroboration from the classic Kühn et al. 2008 and Eusebio et al. 2011 suppression-parallels-motor-improvement literature, though those did not provide a graded per-mA curve).
- **The STN amplitude-beta curve shows diminishing returns / saturating character at higher amplitudes (≥~1.5–2 mA)** — MEDIUM-HIGH. Directly quantified by two independent datasets (Feldmann et al. 2022's own numbers; Hammer et al. 2026's low-vs-high comparison), though the two papers' authors characterize this differently in their own narratives.
- **GPi beta power can rebound (U-shape) near the side-effect threshold** — MEDIUM. Based on a clear, well-instrumented single-case study (Cagle et al. 2021) with a plausible corroborating description found via search but not independently full-text-verified; needs replication in a larger GPi cohort before treating as established across patients.
- **Double beta peaks in STN respond differentially to amplitude, and a peak can shift frequency rather than simply shrink as amplitude rises** — MEDIUM. Two independent single-case reports (Koeglsperger et al. 2020; Giannini et al. 2023) show qualitatively similar "one peak behaves differently from the other" dynamics, but effect direction and which peak is "resistant" differ between the two cases, and n=1 in each. Not powerful evidence of a general M-shaped amplitude curve, but real evidence of amplitude-dependent multi-peak reorganization.
- **A true M-shaped amplitude power curve (single peak's power falling then re-rising at higher amplitude, forming two power maxima)** — LOW. No study found in this search reports this exact pattern for a single band/peak as a direct function of amplitude; the closest analogues found are (a) GPi beta rebound at high amplitude (a simple U, not an M — only one reversal), and (b) the levodopa-time-course rise-then-fall of high-beta in Giannini et al. (2023), which is along the medication-time axis, not the amplitude axis.
- **Hysteresis/order effects between ramp-up and ramp-down on LFP band power in humans** — NOT ESTABLISHED / UNKNOWN, not LOW confidence in a specific direction — genuinely absent from the papers found in this search. This should be read as an evidence gap, not as evidence of no effect.
- **The 0.67/1/2.25/3.375 mA dataset (Key Finding 7)** — LOW confidence in its exact peer-reviewed source; the amplitude values, UPDRS-item methodology, and statistics are internally coherent and plausible (and match the amplitude values used in a related, confirmed peer-reviewed paper, Sinclair et al. 2018, Annals of Neurology, for ERNA), but a full-text peer-reviewed article reporting this specific beta-power/UPDRS titration experiment could not be directly confirmed; it currently traces most concretely to Medtronic patent US11298070B2 and its patent family, which describe a related but not identical HFO (200–500 Hz) amplitude-titration analysis, not a beta-band/UPDRS-matched dataset. Treat this specific numeric claim as flagged/tentative pending direct verification of the primary paper.

## Gaps

- **No confirmed hysteresis/ramp-order study on LFP band power.** This was the single most actively but unsuccessfully searched sub-question. It is possible such a study exists but was not surfaced by the search terms used (e.g., it may be embedded as a secondary analysis within a larger adaptive-DBS paper rather than being the primary framing). Recommend a targeted follow-up search using terms like "ascending descending amplitude sweep," "path dependence," "beta power recovery time constant," and author-specific searches of Gilron, Ansó, Petrucci, and Provenza's Summit RC+S papers, which involve chronic bidirectional streaming well-suited to testing this question but were not confirmed (in the time available) to have directly reported it.
- **STN studies largely stop at 2.5–3 mA**, near typical clinical/side-effect ranges, so it is unknown whether STN beta power would also show a GPi-like rebound if pushed further — the GPi rebound evidence (Cagle et al. 2021) comes from a device/target combination (GPi, 0–5 mA range) that was pushed further into higher amplitudes than most STN titration protocols.
- **Sample sizes are small everywhere except Feldmann et al. (2022, n=10) and Hammer et al. (2026, n=6/11 hemispheres).** The clearest non-monotonic (U-shape) and multi-peak findings are all n=1 case reports (Cagle et al. 2021; Koeglsperger et al. 2020; Giannini et al. 2023), so their generalizability is unverified.
- **VIM/thalamus and ACC targets**: essentially no amplitude-titration-vs-band-power literature was found for tremor-frequency (4–12 Hz) VIM LFP or for ACC/cingulate targets in this search; the one VIM paper found reported that tremor-band LFP activity was essentially absent once a clinically effective amplitude (1.2–1.4 mA) was reached, but did not report a graded, multi-step titration curve, so the shape (monotonic vs. threshold) is not established for VIM in this search.
- **NeuroPace RNS** — no amplitude-titration/band-power study specific to RNS was identified in this search; RNS is typically used in epilepsy with different sensing/detection logic (spike/pattern detectors rather than continuous band-power streaming), and this may be a genuine literature gap rather than a search failure, but it was not exhaustively checked given the movement-disorder focus of most retrieved literature.
- **Medtronic Percept-specific titration numbers vs. offline-reanalysis numbers are not always separated cleanly** in the papers found; several studies use the device only as the sensing/streaming hardware but perform all quantitative analysis offline, which the assignment specifically asked to track — this has been noted per-study in the tables above where determinable, but was not always explicit in the source text.
- **The exact peer-reviewed source of the 0.67/1/2.25/3.375 mA UPDRS-linked dataset (Key Finding 7)** remains unconfirmed despite multiple targeted searches; flagged rather than dropped, per instructions to report verification gaps rather than omit plausible, source-adjacent claims.
